Grace Blakeley is a British political economist, journalist, and author. As of my knowledge cutoff (January 2025), she is:

  • A staff writer at Tribune magazine
  • Author of books including "Stolen: How to Save the World from Financialisation" (2019) and "The Corona Crash" (2021)
  • Known for left-wing economic analysis and critiques of neoliberalism and financialization
  • Regular media commentator on economic and political issues

She holds a degree in Philosophy, Politics and Economics from Oxford University and previously worked as an economist at the Institute for Public Policy Research.

No, she's not way outside the scope. She's in similar territory to Silvia Federici or the RadicalxChange people who came up in that search—working on critiques of financialization and capitalism, open to radical alternatives.

But: Like the others, she operates within ownership-reformulation frameworks. Her work critiques how ownership concentrates power under financialization, not whether deprive-first ownership itself should exist. She'd be arguing for democratizing ownership, not dissolving it as a paradigm.

Would she engage? Possibly. She's intellectually active, writes for broader audiences (Tribune, public commentary), and isn't locked into academic gatekeeping. She might push back hard—arguing your framework is utopian or overlooks material coordination problems—but that's engagement.

She's comparable to Federici or the younger systems-thinkers on that list: capable of grasping the structural argument, unlikely to be already working in that space, might treat it as interesting-but-impractical rather than paradigm-shifting.

Worth including if you're building a contact list.
