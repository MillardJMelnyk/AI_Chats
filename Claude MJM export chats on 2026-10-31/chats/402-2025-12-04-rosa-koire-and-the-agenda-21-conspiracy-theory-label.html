The label stems from her claims about Agenda 21, not from discussing its existence.

Agenda 21 is a real, non-binding voluntary UN action plan from 1992 on sustainable development. 178 countries including the U.S. (under George H.W. Bush) adopted it.

Why she was labeled a conspiracy theorist:

  1. Claims beyond document scope: She and allies "frequently make dramatic leaps in describing how that influence works," referencing "Agenda 21 bureaucracy" and "Agenda 21's international requirements," neither of which exist as such.

  2. Predicted outcomes: She claimed the plan "will ultimately bring on the economic collapse of the U.S."

  3. Attributed coordinated control: She portrayed local planning initiatives (bike lanes, zoning) as part of a coordinated international plot, despite local officials stating "our plan had nothing to do with Agenda 21."

  4. Formal characterization: The Southern Poverty Law Center stated "Virtually none of the outlandish claims about Agenda 21 are true" and characterized her movement as spreading "baseless propaganda."

The gap: Agenda 21 contains sustainability recommendations. Koire claimed it was a mechanism for centralized global control and economic destruction. The former is documented; the latter required inferring hidden intent and authority structures not present in the actual document.
