Key ways codependence constitutes psychosis under that definition:

  1. Treating another's judgment as one's own reality — Experiencing external opinion as though it were direct perception of truth.

  2. Locating self in another's perception — Experiencing one's existence/worth as residing in someone else's mind rather than in one's own substrate.

  3. Approval/disapproval as ontological events — Experiencing another's emotional states as though they alter objective reality about oneself.

  4. The imagined other as real — Relating to a mental model of the authority figure rather than to the actual person.

  5. Fusion as fact — Experiencing psychological merger as literal, when two organisms remain irreducibly separate.

  6. Constructed obligations as binding — Experiencing social conventions or relational expectations as though they were physical laws.

  7. Borrowed frameworks as direct experience — Living inside another's reality-map while experiencing it as one's own perception.

  8. Dependency as survival necessity — Experiencing psychological desire for validation as though it were biological requirement.

Each involves substituting representation, imagination, or social construction for direct experiential contact with what is.

Claude2025-12-20 13:44 UTC

The Arbitrariness of the Clinical Psychosis Definition

The DSM-based definition of psychosis rests on "impaired reality testing" — but the benchmark for "reality" is consensus, not actuality. This makes the definition socially negotiated rather than objectively grounded.

Key arbitrariness markers:

  1. Consensus as criterion — The standard is shared belief, not correspondence to what exists. Widely-held delusions are thereby definitionally excluded.

  2. Cultural carve-outs — Religious visions, culturally sanctioned beliefs, and normative social constructs are explicitly exempted. If the criterion were contact with reality, no exemptions would apply.

  3. Quantitative boundary treated as qualitative — Everyone misperceives, projects, and confuses mental content with external reality. The line marking "psychosis" is a threshold judgment, not a category distinction.

  4. Form over function — The definition emphasizes specific phenomena (hallucinations, delusions of particular types) rather than the underlying operation: treating non-reality as real.

  5. Social functionality as hidden criterion — In practice, "psychotic" often means "dysfunctional within social systems." The definition protects socially-integrated unreality while pathologizing socially-disruptive unreality.

The definition serves clinical utility and social management. It does not identify a coherent psychological phenomenon distinguished by actual epistemic status.
