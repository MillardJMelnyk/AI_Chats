What's the path to residency in Uruguay
Claude2026-01-18 23:53 UTC

Uruguay Residency Paths for Canadians:

1. Temporary Residency (most common):

Requirements:

  • Clean criminal background check (from Canada AND any country lived in 5+ years)
  • Apostilled/legalized documents
  • Proof of income (pension, remote work, investments) - no fixed minimum but ~$1,500 USD/month commonly cited
  • Health certificate
  • Proof of housing in Uruguay (rental contract or property ownership)

Process:

  • Enter on tourist visa (90 days)
  • Apply at Dirección Nacional de Migración (Montevideo)
  • Initial approval grants provisional ID (cédula)
  • Full residency card issued after review (3-12 months processing)
  • Valid 1 year, renewable annually

After 3 years temporary residency:

  • Eligible for permanent residency
  • Must demonstrate continuous residence (minimal absences)

After 5 years (3 permanent + 2 temporary, or 5 temporary):

  • Eligible for citizenship
  • Must pass Spanish language test
  • No requirement to renounce Canadian citizenship (Uruguay allows dual)

2. Rentista/Retirement Visa:

  • Essentially same as temporary residency above
  • Designed for retirees/passive income
  • Same income proof requirements

3. Investment/Business Route:

  • More complex, requires local company formation
  • Not typically faster or easier than standard temporary residency

Key Challenges:

  • Document legalization bureaucracy - apostilles, translations, timing
  • In-person presence required in Montevideo for initial application
  • Income proof must be verifiable/stable
  • Processing times unpredictable

Costs:

  • Application fees: ~$200-500 USD
  • Legal assistance (recommended): $1,000-2,500 USD
  • Document preparation/translation: $500-1,000 USD
  • Background checks: $100-300 USD

Timeline:

  • Document gathering: 2-4 months
  • Application to provisional ID: 1-3 months
  • Full residency card: 3-12 months total

Compared to your Mexican process:

  • Less family unity options
  • More emphasis on economic self-sufficiency
  • Similar bureaucratic complexity
  • Faster overall than Mexican permanent residency if you don't have family ties

Which is it in Spanish que paso or que pasa?
Claude2026-01-19 00:48 UTC

Both are correct - different meanings:

"¿Qué pasó?" - "What happened?" (past tense)

  • Use when asking about something that already occurred
  • "What went wrong?"
  • Asking for explanation of past event

"¿Qué pasa?" - "What's happening?" / "What's up?" (present tense)

  • Current situation
  • Casual greeting
  • "What's going on right now?"

Examples:

"¿Qué pasó ayer?" = "What happened yesterday?"

"¿Qué pasa aquí?" = "What's happening here?"

"¿Qué pasa, amigo?" = "What's up, friend?" (greeting)

Context determines which one.
