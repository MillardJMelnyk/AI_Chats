Legal Evaluation

1. Jurisdictional Issue

Article 5 of the Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor explicitly excludes "servicios profesionales que no sean de carácter mercantil" (professional services that are not commercial in nature). However, "póliza jurídica" companies operate as commercial businesses selling standardized services rather than individualized professional legal counsel. This distinction is arguable—they may fall under PROFECO jurisdiction.

2. Weaknesses in Their Position

Their argument has three significant flaws:

A. Payment timing contradicts their claim: You state the $2,000 fee is "normally paid AT SIGNING." If true, this establishes the advance was a deposit on the complete service—not separate payment for "investigation and contract preparation." Their post-hoc characterization of what the $1,000 "covered" appears to be retroactive justification.

B. Service completion: Under the Código Civil Federal, payment for professional services is due "inmediatamente que preste cada servicio o al fin de todos, cuando se separe el profesor o haya concluido el negocio o trabajo que se le confió" (immediately after each service is rendered or at the end of all of them, when the work entrusted is concluded). The work was demonstrably NOT concluded—no contract was signed.

C. Vague causation: They claim "La situación que impidió concretar la firma del contrato es ajena a nosotros" without specifying what that situation was. This matters legally—if the tenant failed their investigation, their service arguably was completed (investigation revealed disqualification). If another reason, their claim is weaker.

Findings

1. Legally OK?

Yes, with conditions. There is no specific regulation of póliza jurídica services in Mexico. These function as private contracts for legal services, governed by the Código Civil and Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor. The lack of regulation allows companies to establish their own policies on refunds and cancellations.

Under freedom of contract principles, parties can agree to non-refundable terms. However, the agreement must be clear and explicit—ideally in writing before payment.

Key legal principle from the Código Civil Federal, Article 2610: payment for professional services is due "inmediatamente que preste cada servicio o al fin de todos, cuando se separe el profesor o haya concluido el negocio o trabajo que se le confió" (immediately after each service is rendered or at the end of all services, when the work entrusted is concluded).

2. Common Practice?

Mixed—and their model appears unusual.

Industry patterns found:

Company Structure
Tu Póliza Única "Para iniciar la investigación, se requiere... dar un anticipo de $2,000.00 (dos mil pesos, 00/100 M.N.) no reembolsables"
RentLife "$500.00 pesos por concepto de apertura de póliza jurídica" as a separate one-time fee

Querétaro firms "El costo de la póliza va desde $3,000... mismo que normalmente se deben pagar en una sola exhibición al momento de la contratación"

Pattern observed: Most companies either (a) charge a small, explicitly non-refundable investigation fee ($500-$2,000) separate from the main fee, OR (b) collect full payment at contract signing.

Critical Question for Your Case

Did they provide written documentation—before you paid—stating that the $1,000 was specifically for investigation services and non-refundable regardless of outcome?

If no written agreement exists establishing this:

  • Their retroactive characterization of what the $1,000 "covered" is weak
  • Your position that the fee is normally paid at signing establishes different expectations
  • "La empresa incurre en gastos administrativos, investigaciones y verificaciones que justifican el pago"—but they must prove these services were actually rendered

Bottom line: Legally permissible if agreed in advance. Their practice of collecting 50% upfront as non-refundable without clear written terms is aggressive but not clearly illegal. Their position weakens significantly if they cannot produce documentation showing you agreed to non-refundable terms before payment.
