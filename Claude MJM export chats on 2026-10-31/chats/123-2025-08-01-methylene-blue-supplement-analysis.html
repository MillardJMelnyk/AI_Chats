This appears to be a supplement called "Ultimate Methylene Blue 1%" by a company called Bluenova. Methylene blue is a chemical compound that has gained attention in recent years as a potential nootropic (cognitive enhancer) and longevity supplement.

Here's what you should know about methylene blue supplements:

What it is: Methylene blue is a synthetic dye that's been used medically for over a century, primarily to treat methemoglobinemia (a blood disorder). In recent years, it's been marketed as a supplement for cognitive enhancement and cellular energy.

Claimed benefits:

  • Enhanced mitochondrial function (cellular energy production)
  • Improved cognitive performance and focus
  • Potential neuroprotective effects
  • Mood enhancement

Important considerations:

  • The research on methylene blue for cognitive enhancement is still limited and mostly based on animal studies or small human trials
  • It can interact with certain medications, particularly antidepressants (especially MAOIs and SSRIs)
  • It can cause side effects like nausea, dizziness, and can temporarily turn your urine blue-green
  • The optimal dosing for cognitive benefits isn't well established
  • Quality and purity can vary significantly between supplement manufacturers

Red flags to consider:

  • The marketing language uses typical supplement hype ("zero crash, zero compromise")
  • The price point seems quite high for what it is
  • Claims about "thousands trusting" the brand without verifiable evidence

If you're considering this supplement, it would be wise to consult with a healthcare provider first, especially if you take any medications or have health conditions.

How does it interact with sertraline
Claude2025-08-01 00:51 UTC

This is a serious drug interaction that you need to be aware of.

Methylene blue and sertraline (Zoloft) have a dangerous interaction that can cause serotonin syndrome, a potentially life-threatening condition. Here's what you need to know:

How the interaction works: Methylene blue acts as a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAO-A inhibitor), which increases levels of serotonin in the brain. When combined with sertraline (an SSRI that also increases serotonin), this can lead to dangerously high serotonin levels.

FDA warning: The FDA has concluded that the concomitant administration of a serotonergic psychiatric medication with methylene blue has the potential for a drug interaction causing serotonin syndrome.

Serotonin syndrome symptoms include:

  • High fever
  • Rapid heart rate
  • High blood pressure
  • Muscle rigidity/tremors
  • Confusion/agitation
  • Profuse sweating
  • Diarrhea

Important considerations:

  • Combining methylene blue with SSRIs can lead to serotonin syndrome, a potentially life-threatening condition
  • This applies to both medical-grade methylene blue and supplements
  • The risk exists even with lower doses found in supplements
  • Research shows SRIs can interact with methylene blue, causing a serious adverse reaction consistent with serotonin syndrome

Bottom line: If you're taking sertraline, you should not take methylene blue supplements without explicit approval and monitoring from your doctor. The potential for a serious, life-threatening reaction makes this combination inadvisable for most people.

Sertraline elimination basics:

  • The elimination half-life ranges from 13 to 45 hours, with an average of 26 hours
  • Sertraline also has an active metabolite (desmethylsertraline) with a much longer half-life of 62 to 104 hours

Standard washout period: The most conservative strategy, with the lowest risk of drug interactions, is to gradually taper the dose of the first antidepressant to minimize withdrawal symptoms then start a washout period equivalent to five half-lives of the drug.

For sertraline specifically: Sertraline, even from high doses, should be largely washed-out by 5-7 days for most drug interaction purposes.

Calculating the washout period:

  • 5 half-lives of sertraline = 5 × 26 hours = 130 hours (about 5.4 days)
  • However, considering the active metabolite's longer half-life, a more conservative approach would be 7-14 days

Important caveats:

  • The half-life can vary based on your age, weight, genetics, kidney/liver function, drug interactions, and your medical conditions
  • Individual variation means some people may need longer
  • This should always be done under medical supervision

Bottom line: While sertraline may be mostly cleared in 5-7 days, given the serious nature of the methylene blue interaction and individual variation, most medical professionals would recommend waiting at least 1-2 weeks after completely stopping sertraline before considering methylene blue. This decision should absolutely be made with your prescribing physician.

Which would be considered safer for long-term use, sertraline or methylene blue?
Claude2025-08-01 00:54 UTC

Sertraline is significantly safer for long-term use compared to methylene blue supplements. Here's why:

Sertraline (established safety profile):

  • Decades of clinical research and FDA approval since 1991
  • Extensive long-term safety data from millions of patients
  • Well-understood side effect profile and monitoring protocols
  • Clear dosing guidelines and medical oversight
  • Proven efficacy for depression, anxiety, and other conditions
  • Established withdrawal protocols when discontinuing

Methylene blue supplements (limited safety data):

  • Very limited long-term human safety studies
  • No FDA approval for cognitive enhancement or mood benefits
  • Unregulated supplement industry with quality/purity concerns
  • Unclear optimal dosing for purported benefits
  • Potential for serious drug interactions (as we discussed)
  • Most research is short-term or in animal models
  • Can cause side effects like nausea, headaches, and discolored urine

Key safety considerations:

  • Sertraline has established therapeutic monitoring and medical oversight
  • Methylene blue supplements lack standardized dosing or safety monitoring
  • The supplement industry has less stringent quality controls than prescription medications
  • Long-term effects of methylene blue supplementation are largely unknown

Important note: If you're currently taking sertraline for a medical condition like depression or anxiety, it's treating a real health issue. Discontinuing it to try an unproven supplement could put your mental health at risk.

Bottom line: From a risk-benefit perspective, sertraline has a much more established safety profile for long-term use. Any changes to your medication regimen should be discussed with your healthcare provider, who can help weigh the risks and benefits based on your specific situation and medical history.

Medical vs. supplement use - key differences:

Medical methylene blue use:

  • Used for specific medical conditions (methemoglobinemia, malaria, certain poisonings)
  • Typically given in acute, short-term situations - often single doses or brief treatment courses
  • Administered in controlled medical settings with monitoring
  • Used when the immediate medical benefit clearly outweighs risks
  • Standard medical dose is usually 1-2 mg/kg intravenously

Supplement use (what's being marketed):

  • Daily, continuous use for cognitive enhancement/mood
  • Much lower doses but taken chronically over months/years
  • Self-administered without medical supervision
  • Used by healthy people seeking enhancement rather than treating disease

Why we lack long-term safety data:

  • The historical medical uses were primarily acute/short-term
  • Chronic daily supplementation for cognitive benefits is a relatively new application
  • The dosing, frequency, and population (healthy vs. sick) are completely different
  • Most people taking it medically were already seriously ill, making it hard to assess long-term effects

The gap in knowledge: You've identified a real issue - we have safety data for acute medical use, but very limited data on what happens when healthy people take it daily for months or years as a supplement. This is actually a common problem with repurposed medications being sold as supplements.

This makes your original question about safety even more relevant - we're essentially in uncharted territory regarding long-term supplemental use of methylene blue.
