what does demonology include?
Claude2025-12-14 14:57 UTC

Demonology includes:

  1. Classification systems — Taxonomies of demons by hierarchy, function, origin, or domain (e.g., Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, Ars Goetia)

  2. Historical texts and grimoires — Study of primary sources like the Lesser Key of Solomon, Munich Manual, Liber Officiorum Spirituum

  3. Theological frameworks — Religious doctrines on the nature, origin, and operation of demonic entities (varies by tradition: Christian, Islamic, Jewish, etc.)

  4. Summoning/binding practices — Ritual procedures, invocations, sigils, and protective measures described in ceremonial magic traditions

  5. Exorcism and banishment — Methods for expulsion or neutralization across religious and folk traditions

  6. Comparative mythology — Cross-cultural analysis of malevolent spiritual entities

  7. Iconography — Visual representations and symbolic attributes assigned to specific demons

  8. Etymology and naming conventions — Origins and meanings of demon names

The field spans academic religious studies, occult practice, folklore studies, and theological discourse — with significant variation depending on whether the approach is devotional, historical, anthropological, or practical.

sounds like it's focused solely on demons. What about evil gods and Satan?
Claude2025-12-14 14:58 UTC

Satan: Generally included. In Christian demonology, Satan is the chief demon or ruler of demons. Some sources distinguish "Satanology" as a specific subset, but most demonological works treat Satan as the apex of the demonic hierarchy.

Evil gods: Not typically included under demonology proper. These fall under:

  • Theology of specific traditions (e.g., Angra Mainyu in Zoroastrianism)
  • Comparative religion
  • Mythology studies

However, significant overlap exists because:

  1. Many catalogued "demons" were originally gods from rival traditions that were demonized (Baal → Bael, Astarte → Astaroth, Ba'al Zebul → Beelzebub)

  2. The demon/god boundary is tradition-dependent — what one system calls a god, another calls a demon

  3. Some traditions (Zoroastrianism, certain Gnostic systems) blur the distinction entirely

So: demonology is demon-focused by definition, includes Satan as chief demon, and intersects with evil gods primarily through the historical process of demonization of foreign deities rather than treating them as a native category.
