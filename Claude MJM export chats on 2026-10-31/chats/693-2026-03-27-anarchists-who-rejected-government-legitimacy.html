Of all the anarchists there have ever been, which ones clearly and expressly stated that their goal was to demolish every illusion of sanity underlying faith in government?
Claude2026-03-27 13:41 UTC

Max Stirner is the clearest match. In The Ego and Its Own (1845), he explicitly framed his project as exposing the state and other institutions as "spooks" (German: Spuk) or "fixed ideas" - mental phantoms that people treat as sacred realities. He directly targeted what he called the "wheel in the head" - the internalized illusions that make people subordinate themselves to abstractions including government. His entire work is an explicit assault on the psychological foundations of political authority.

Lysander Spooner also fits, though with different emphasis. No Treason: The Constitution of No Authority (1867-1870) systematically dismantles the legitimacy claims of government, particularly consent theory, arguing that belief in government authority rests on demonstrable logical fallacies. However, his framing is more legalistic/logical than psychological.

Most other prominent anarchists (Bakunin, Goldman, Kropotkin, Proudhon) critiqued government primarily on moral, economic, or power-structure grounds rather than explicitly targeting the cognitive/psychological basis of faith in government as an "illusion."

Clarification needed: Are you looking specifically for anarchists who used psychological/cognitive framing (like Stirner), or would systematic logical dismantling of government legitimacy claims (like Spooner) also qualify? The phrase "illusion of sanity" suggests the former.

Stirner described a phenomenon that could be characterized as psychosis, but did not explicitly frame it that way or state that mission.

However:

  • Stirner used metaphors (haunted, possessed, ghosts) not clinical terminology
  • He didn't explicitly say "I am exposing statism as psychosis"
  • He didn't frame his mission as demonstrating believers are insane

So: the phenomenon he described can be interpreted as psychosis (mass delusion, break from reality). But he never stated that framing or mission explicitly.
