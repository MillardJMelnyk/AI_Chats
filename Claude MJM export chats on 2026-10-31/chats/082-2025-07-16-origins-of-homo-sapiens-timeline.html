Based on the latest research, the highest estimate for when Homo sapiens first appeared is around 315,000 years ago. This dating comes from fossils discovered at the Jebel Irhoud site in Morocco that were reported in 2017.

However, there's some debate about this timeline. The scientific consensus generally points to around 300,000 years ago as when anatomically modern Homo sapiens evolved in Africa. Some researchers push the timeline back even further based on genetic evidence. Some scientists trace modern humans' origins to 1 million years ago, though this refers more to the broader evolutionary lineage rather than anatomically modern humans.

So while there's ongoing scientific discussion, the current highest estimate supported by fossil evidence is approximately 315,000 years ago, with the broader scientific consensus settling around 300,000 years ago for the emergence of Homo sapiens in Africa.

Evidence was found in 2018, dating to about 320,000 years ago at the site of Olorgesailie in Kenya, of the early emergence of modern behaviors including: the trade and long-distance transportation of resources (such as obsidian), the use of pigments, and the possible making of projectile points. Sophisticated Resource Exploitation:

By around 100,000 years ago, early Homo sapiens demonstrated advanced hunting and gathering strategies. Taphonomic change in fish skeletons from Blombos Cave have been interpreted as capture of live fish, clearly an intentional human behavior, and Humans in North Africa (Nazlet Sabaha, Egypt) are known to have dabbled in chert mining, as early as ≈100,000 years ago, for the construction of stone tools.

Brain Size and Cognitive Architecture:

Modern humans have very large brains, which vary in size from population to population, and these enlarged brains appear to have supported increasingly complex behaviors during the early period of our species' existence.

The evidence suggests that rather than a sudden cognitive "revolution," early Homo sapiens showed a gradual but consistent development of symbolic thinking, complex planning, sophisticated tool use, and social behaviors that distinguished them from their predecessors - all emerging within that crucial first 100,000 years of our species' existence.

Genetic Mutations for Human-Specific Traits (100,000+ years ago): The results, published in the journal Scientific Reports, show two moments in which mutations accumulated: one around 40,000 years ago, associated with the growth of the Homo sapiens population and its departure from Africa, and an older one, more than 100,000 years ago. Creativity and Self-Awareness Networks: Recent research has identified networks of genes distinguishing the three major systems of modern human personality and adaptability: emotional reactivity, self-control, and self-awareness, with evidence suggesting these genetic networks evolved to support uniquely human creative and adaptive capabilities.

Complex Language vs. Proto-Language: While full human language appears around 150,000-200,000 years ago, earlier forms may have existed much earlier.
