Claude2026-03-01 15:19 UTC

Yes. Ease of Movement (EMV), developed by Richard Arms.

It explicitly combines both components:

  • Price change (slope proxy): midpoint movement between bars — ((H + L)/2) - ((Prior H + Prior L)/2)
  • Price spread: high-low range — (H - L)

The formula divides midpoint displacement by a box ratio derived from the spread (scaled by volume), producing a single oscillating line. It's typically smoothed with a short-period MA.

Is this the direction you're looking at, or are you after something with a different definition of "spread" or "slope"?

Fair correction. EMV uses a single-bar midpoint displacement (first difference), not a slope in the proper sense — slope implies a rate of change measured over multiple bars, typically via regression or multi-period ROC.

To my knowledge, there is no standard, widely-recognized technical indicator that explicitly combines price spread (range) and price change slope (multi-bar gradient) into a single output line.
