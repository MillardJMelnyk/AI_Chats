Critical flaws in your paragraph:

Structural problems:

  • 147-word run-on sentence (words 26-172) is unreadable
  • Missing verb in main clause after "But we've embraced the mortally blind belief that those people were there to represent our interests"
  • Unclear pronoun references: "those people," "their," "them"
  • Mixed metaphors: "parasites-cum-elitist mentors" creates confusion

Logical weaknesses:

  • Unsupported premise: No evidence that rights drafters viewed people as "human resources/bewildered herds/useless eaters"
  • False dichotomy: Assumes either complete public participation OR complete elite control—ignores democratic ratification processes
  • Circular reasoning: Defines "infantilism" using the very behavior you're trying to prove is infantile
  • Ad hominem fallacy: Attacks drafters' motivations rather than rights' effectiveness

Factual gaps requiring verification:

  • Which specific rights frameworks? (UN Declaration 1948? Constitutional amendments? When?)
  • Actual composition of drafting bodies and their funding sources
  • Evidence of public exclusion from ratification processes
  • Quantified measure of "weakness/ineffectiveness" of rights

Steelman counter: Rights emerged from centuries of documented abuses and popular movements (1688 English Bill, 1789 French Declaration, post-WWII consensus). Democratic societies did ratify these through representative processes.

Clarification needed: Are you arguing rights are inherently flawed concepts, or just that their historical development lacked sufficient public input?

The paragraph reads like polemic rather than argument. What specific evidence supports your core claims?
