Claude2025-11-23 16:20 UTC

The premise is malformed.

Critical error: You're calculating as if 30 trillion cells each formed independently. They didn't. All cells in a body descend from one zygote through mitosis. The probability calculation collapses to: "What are the odds of one cell forming and successfully replicating?" Not 30 trillion independent formation events.

Second error: "Exactly the same fundamental architecture" misrepresents the process. Cells differentiate during development through gene expression regulation, not by repeated independent assembly. The architecture is copied, not re-formed from scratch each time.

Third error: "Hit-or-miss, undirected experimentation" mischaracterizes natural selection. Selection is non-random elimination. Random: mutation. Non-random: survival and reproduction based on fitness. The two-step process is not equivalent to pure chance.

Fourth error: The "odds" framing assumes a single trial. Abiogenesis had ~500 million years and Earth's entire surface area for chemical interactions. The relevant calculation is: probability per trial × number of trials × number of locations. Even low per-trial probabilities become near-certainties given sufficient trials.

What's the actual question?

Are you asking:

  1. Probability that life originated from non-life (abiogenesis)?
  2. Probability that evolution produced current complexity given that life exists?
  3. Something else?

Specify which probability you want quantified.
