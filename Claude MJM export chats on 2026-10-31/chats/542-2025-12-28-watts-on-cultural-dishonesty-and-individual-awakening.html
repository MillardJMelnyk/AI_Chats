Two issues:

1. Attribution problem: I cannot verify this quote as authentically Watts. The searches return only social media shares with no primary source. The style is also uncharacteristic—Watts typically avoided moralistic binaries like "terrible choice" and "disturb your soul." His characteristic mode was playful dissolution of such dilemmas, not framing them as tragic. This reads more like contemporary self-help grafted onto his name.

2. To your actual question (treating the sentiment as Watts-adjacent): Based on the verified corpus, Watts did not express expectation of collective transformation to honesty-as-norm. His framework was explicitly individual awakening within an unchanging social game. Key positions:

  • He described consensus trance as a permanent feature of social existence, not a fixable bug
  • His solution was personal "de-hypnotization"—seeing through the game while remaining in it
  • He advocated "sincere insincerity"—playing social roles consciously without being captured by them
  • He "felt such teaching could improve the world, at least to a degree"—note the modest qualifier

His Zen/Taoist framework would actually reject the premise that honesty should become the cultural norm. The "polite illusion" and authentic perception exist in necessary polarity—trying to universalize awakening would be another form of attachment. Watts was describing a perennial existential condition, not diagnosing a historically contingent problem with a solution.
