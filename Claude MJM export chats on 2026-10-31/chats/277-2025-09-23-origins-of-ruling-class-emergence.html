Based on archaeological evidence:

~6000-5500 BCE: Northern Mesopotamia shows hierarchy in Hassuna/Samarra tradition - earliest clear evidence of social stratification.

~4000-3500 BCE: Mesopotamian city-states develop formal ruling classes. Mesopotamia created earliest known social hierarchy with kings and priests at top.

~3100 BCE: Egyptian pharaonic system emerges.

Flaws in your premise:

  • "Minorities" implies numerical disadvantage - early elites often weren't numerical minorities but controlled resources/military force
  • Archaeological preservation bias - elite artifacts survive better than commoner materials
  • Dating precision decreases with age - margins of ±200-500 years common for Neolithic periods

Weakest assumption: That hierarchy = minority rule. Early stratification may have involved resource controllers (priests, warrior-leaders) who represented significant population segments, not tiny minorities.
