What's a zaibatsu
Claude2025-12-08 00:06 UTC

A zaibatsu is a Japanese industrial and financial business conglomerate that dominated the Japanese economy from the Meiji era (late 1800s) through World War II. Key characteristics:

  • Structure: Large family-controlled holding companies with subsidiaries spanning multiple industries (banking, manufacturing, trading, shipping, etc.)
  • Control: Centralized ownership, typically by a single wealthy family
  • Examples: Mitsui, Mitsubishi, Sumitomo, Yasuda

Historical timeline:

  • Rose to dominance: 1868-1945
  • Dissolved: Post-WWII by Allied occupation authorities (1947-1949) due to their role in Japan's militarization
  • Successor form: Modern "keiretsu" (企業集団) - looser corporate groups with cross-shareholdings but no single family control

The term combines 財 (zai, wealth) + 閥 (batsu, clique/faction).
