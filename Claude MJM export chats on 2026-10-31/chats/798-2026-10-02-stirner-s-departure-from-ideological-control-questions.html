Claude2026-10-02 23:09 UTC

Friday, October 2, 2026, time unavailable to me

Premise weaknesses:

  1. "Every -ism" overgeneralizes. Proudhon, Bakunin, Kropotkin, and the syndicalists fit your description because their questions are about property, the state, and the means of production. Tolstoyan anarcho-pacifism, Landauer (anarchism as a relation between people rather than a transfer of control), and post-anarchists like Saul Newman, who explicitly build on Stirner, do not.

  2. Stirner does answer "who controls the shit." His answer is whoever has the power to hold it: property is what you can make your own (Eigentum from eigen). He rejects the legitimacy layer, not the control question. That is the real gap between him and the others: they argue about who has the right to control, and he denies that rights exist ("spooks").

  3. His critique targets the other anarchists directly. In The Ego and Its Own (1844), Proudhon and the socialists are "social liberals" who replace the State with Society or Humanity, a new sovereign over the individual. On his view, they merely relocate the control question.

  4. The "different level" has a cost. If might is right, the question is not escaped but answered by force. Stirner offers no account of what stops a stronger egoist from owning you, except the "union of egoists," which holds only as long as each member's interest does.

Your characterization holds for the distribution-of-control tradition, but "different level" is accurate only for his rejection of normative grounding, not for his position on control.

Claude2026-10-02 23:25 UTC

Agreed on the substrate claim, with one correction to "completely different matters."

Agreed: Stirner's central object is the structure of possession by ideas, the fixed idea (fixe Idee). His question is what in a person makes right, State, Humanity, or God hold authority at all. That is a question about the psychology of reverence, not about who occupies the sovereign position. His treatment of liberalism as secularized religion is the clearest case: he traces the same structure of the sacred through each successive ideology, regardless of who administers it. Refusing ghosts is a consequence of that analysis, not the analysis itself.

Correction: The matters aren't completely different. His objects are the same ones the control tradition fights over (State, property, law, society). He works at the layer beneath them, asking what makes the control question possible and attractive, so he is prior to that tradition rather than parallel to it.

Weak point: The substrate analysis is not purely diagnostic. "Ownness" is a prescription (consume the ideas, don't be consumed by them), so he is partly a psychologist and partly an advocate.

Millard2026-10-02 23:27 UTC
Someone told me that Stirner believed that egoists should consider everything fair game when it comes to property. I haven't gotten that from him at all.
Claude2026-10-02 23:27 UTC

You're reading him correctly. The claim distorts a real element of his argument.

What's real: Stirner denies that anyone's holdings carry a claim on the egoist. No property is sacred to him, so he owes no deference to it as property. In The Ego and Its Own, he describes himself as seeing others' holdings as potentially his own, with nothing to "respect."

What's false: "Fair game" reads as a rule: egoists should treat everything as takeable. That is an ought, which is exactly what he rejects. He prescribes no taking. The absence of a sacred claim on you is a different thing from an instruction to violate it. Whether to take, trade, or leave something alone is decided by your interest and power in the case, not by a principle in either direction. He explicitly allows for agreements and associations (the union of egoists) when they serve you.

Likely source of the distortion: someone converted "no obligation to respect" into "license to take," which smuggles a normative frame back in.

Weak point: the text gives no restraint either. It's silent on what an egoist will do, which is why both the "predator" and the "peaceful trader" readings get attributed to him.
