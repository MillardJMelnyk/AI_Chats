Claude2025-09-24 19:04 UTC

Yes, inserting "as" would allow you to avoid the commas around "Semiotic relationships." Here are a couple of ways you could revise it:

Option 1: "I've titled this part of the larger diagram as "Semiotic relationships" because it represents the symbolic mapping that goes on completely inside our heads."

Option 2: "I've titled this part of the larger diagram as semiotic relationships because it represents the symbolic mapping that goes on completely inside our heads."

In Option 1, you keep the quotation marks around the title, which makes it clear you're referring to the exact label you've used. In Option 2, you remove both the quotation marks and any need for commas by making it flow as a direct object of the preposition "as."

The "as" construction works because you're no longer using an appositive (which requires commas) but instead creating a complement that explains what you titled it.

I've titled or I've entitled?
Claude2025-09-24 19:05 UTC

"I've titled" is the better choice here.

"Title" as a verb means to give a name or title to something, which is exactly what you're doing - you're giving a name to this part of your diagram.

"Entitle" primarily means to give someone a right or claim to something ("This coupon entitles you to a discount") or, less commonly, to give a title to a creative work like a book or article.

While "entitled" can technically be used for naming things, "titled" is more natural and direct when you're simply giving something a descriptive label or heading, especially in academic or technical contexts like your diagram.

So your sentence works well as: "I've titled this part of the larger diagram as 'Semiotic relationships'..."
