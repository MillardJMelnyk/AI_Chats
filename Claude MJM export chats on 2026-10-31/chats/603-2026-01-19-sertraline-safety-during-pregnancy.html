First Trimester:

  • Small increased risk of cardiac septal defects (ventricular/atrial septal defects). Absolute risk increase approximately 0.5-1% above baseline.
  • Most recent meta-analyses show conflicting results on whether this association is causal or due to confounding factors.

Third Trimester:

  • Neonatal adaptation syndrome in 20-30% of exposed newborns: irritability, jitteriness, respiratory distress, feeding difficulties, hypoglycemia. Usually self-limiting, resolves within 2 weeks.
  • Possible increased risk of persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN). Estimated absolute risk 3-4 per 1000 births vs. 1-2 per 1000 baseline. Causality disputed.

Throughout Pregnancy:

  • Increased risk of preterm birth and low birth weight (may be confounded by underlying maternal depression).
  • Postpartum hemorrhage risk increased (SSRIs affect platelet function).

Developmental Outcomes:

  • No consistent evidence of major neurodevelopmental deficits in long-term studies.
  • Some studies show minor motor delays in toddlerhood that typically resolve.

Sertraline is generally considered one of the lowest-risk SSRIs during pregnancy when treatment is necessary. Untreated depression carries its own fetal/maternal risks.

FETAL/NEONATAL RISKS:

First Trimester:

  • Cardiac septal defects (baby): Small increased risk, absolute increase ~0.5-1% above baseline. Causality uncertain.

Third Trimester exposure effects on newborn:

  • Neonatal adaptation syndrome (baby): 20-30% of exposed newborns. Symptoms include irritability, jitteriness, respiratory distress, feeding difficulties, hypoglycemia. Self-limiting, resolves within 2 weeks.
  • Persistent pulmonary hypertension/PPHN (baby): Absolute risk 3-4 per 1000 births vs. 1-2 per 1000 baseline. Causality disputed.

Throughout pregnancy:

  • Preterm birth (baby): Increased risk, possibly confounded by maternal depression.
  • Low birth weight (baby): Increased risk, possibly confounded by maternal depression.
  • Long-term neurodevelopment (baby): No consistent evidence of major deficits. Some studies show minor motor delays in toddlerhood that typically resolve.

MATERNAL RISKS:

  • Postpartum hemorrhage (mother): Increased risk due to SSRI effects on platelet aggregation.
