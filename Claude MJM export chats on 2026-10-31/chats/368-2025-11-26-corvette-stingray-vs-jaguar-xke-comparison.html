PERFORMANCE

C2 Corvette Stingray (1963-1967)

  • 327 ci V8: 250-375 hp depending on spec
  • 0-60: 5.6-6.5 seconds (base), 4.5-5.8 seconds (high-performance)
  • Top speed: 130-150 mph
  • Quarter mile: 13.8-14.5 seconds

C3 Corvette Stingray (1968-1982)

  • 327-454 ci V8: 165-425 hp (power dropped significantly post-1972 due to emissions)
  • 0-60: 5.3-8.0 seconds (degraded badly in mid-70s)
  • Top speed: 112-140 mph
  • Quarter mile: 13.3-16.5 seconds

E-Type Series 1 (1961-1968)

  • 3.8L/4.2L inline-6: 265 hp
  • 0-60: 6.9-7.6 seconds
  • Top speed: 150 mph
  • Quarter mile: 14.9-15.1 seconds

E-Type Series 2 (1968-1971)

  • 4.2L inline-6: 246 hp (detuned for US emissions)
  • 0-60: 7.4-8.0 seconds
  • Top speed: 135-142 mph

E-Type Series 3 (1971-1975)

  • 5.3L V12: 272 hp
  • 0-60: 6.4-7.0 seconds
  • Top speed: 146 mph

Advantage: C2 Corvette and early C3 (pre-1973) for straight-line acceleration. E-Type Series 1 competitive. All degraded in the 1970s.

MAINTENANCE

Corvette

  • Parts: Abundant and cheap (Chevy small-block ubiquitous)
  • Labor: Simple American V8 design, any mechanic could work on it
  • Service intervals: Oil changes every 3,000 miles, basic tune-ups
  • Estimated annual maintenance (1970s-era): $300-600

E-Type

  • Parts: Expensive, many UK-sourced
  • Labor: Required Jaguar specialists; complex DOHC inline-6/V12
  • Service intervals: More frequent than Corvette; carburetors required constant adjustment
  • Estimated annual maintenance (1970s-era): $1,200-2,500
  • Series 3 V12: Notoriously difficult to work on (tight engine bay)

Advantage: Corvette by massive margin. E-Type maintenance costs 2-4x higher.

RELIABILITY

Corvette

  • Engine: Highly reliable Chevy small-block/big-block
  • Electrical: Simple but prone to typical 1960s-70s wiring issues
  • Common problems: Fiberglass body cracks, vacuum system leaks (C3), overheating in traffic
  • Overall: Well above average for the era

E-Type

  • Engine: Series 1/2 inline-6 reasonably durable; Series 3 V12 problematic (overheating, complexity)
  • Electrical: Notorious Lucas electrical system failures
  • Common problems: Chronic overheating, carburetors out of sync, rust in unibody structure, differential failures
  • Cooling system inadequate for US climate
  • Overall: Significantly below average, even for British cars of the era

Advantage: Corvette dramatically more reliable. E-Type ownership required tolerance for frequent breakdowns.

Bottom line: Corvette superior in all three categories. E-Type advantage was styling and prestige only.

Compare just the C2 to the series 1 in terms of handling

SUSPENSION & CHASSIS

C2 Corvette (1963-1967)

  • Front: Independent, unequal-length A-arms, coil springs
  • Rear: Independent, transverse leaf spring (new for 1963)
  • Frame: Ladder-type steel with fiberglass body
  • Weight: 3,020-3,150 lbs
  • Weight distribution: ~52/48 front/rear

E-Type Series 1 (1961-1968)

  • Front: Independent, torsion bars, double wishbones
  • Rear: Independent, lower wishbones, trailing links, coil springs
  • Frame: Steel monocoque center with front/rear subframes
  • Weight: 2,520-2,800 lbs (300-400 lbs lighter)
  • Weight distribution: ~51/49 front/rear

HANDLING CHARACTERISTICS

C2 Corvette

  • Oversteer tendency with aggressive throttle (rear weight bias with powerful engine)
  • Stiff suspension for American standards; still body roll in hard cornering
  • Wide track (56.8" front/57.0" rear) provided stability
  • Heavy steering at low speeds; good feedback at speed
  • Contemporary Road & Track (1963): "Understeer at moderate speeds, oversteer when pushed hard"

E-Type Series 1

  • Neutral to slight understeer in most conditions
  • Superior weight distribution and ~400 lbs less weight gave better turn-in
  • Rack-and-pinion steering (vs Corvette's recirculating ball) - more precise
  • Softer suspension than Corvette; more compliant over rough surfaces
  • Contemporary Motor (1961): "Handles as well as anything we've driven"
  • Rear suspension prone to camber changes under load

TRACK PERFORMANCE

Skidpad (lateral g's, period tests)

  • C2 Corvette: 0.82-0.86g
  • E-Type Series 1: 0.85-0.88g

Slalom/transient response

  • E-Type quicker due to lighter weight and better steering
  • Corvette more stable at limit but slower to change direction

Advantage: E-Type Series 1 for handling precision and feedback. C2 Corvette more dramatic/challenging at limit but objectively less capable in corners.

Verified Fuller concepts from the image:

  1. "Humanity is taking its final examination" - Fuller's actual words: "Humanity is in a final exam as to whether or not it qualifies for continuance in Universe"

  2. "The present evolutionary crisis of humans on planet Earth is that of a final examination for their continuance in universe"

  3. "doesn't have to be you or me. There is enough for all" / "We need not operate competitively any longer" - Attributed to Fuller in 1970s context but presented as paraphrase, discussing distribution problems and having "enough for all"

  4. "youth, truth, and love" - Appears in same 1970s paraphrase: "If we succeed, it will be because of youth, truth and love"

Attribution percentage: ~70%

The core concepts (final exam for humanity, sufficiency of resources, ending competition, youth/truth/love as success factors) are Fuller's. However, the simplified wording and sentence structure don't match his actual prose style, which was typically more technical and verbose. Fuller wrote things like "continuance in Universe" not "doesn't have to be you or me."

The image quote packages Fuller's ideas into digestible social media language rather than preserving his actual phrasing.
