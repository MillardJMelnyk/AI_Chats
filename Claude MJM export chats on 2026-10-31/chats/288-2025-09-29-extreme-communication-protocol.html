Claude2025-09-29 16:10 UTC

Attacking your premise:

Definitional flaws:

  • "Evil is intent to harm" - circular with "harm" undefined. Intent vs. outcome distinction creates measurement impossibility.
  • "Deprive first, sharing optional" mischaracterizes current economics - most transactions are voluntary exchanges, not deprivations.
  • "Authority" definition conflates legitimate coordination with violence.

Implementation impossibilities:

  • "Commons-managed consensus" - who enforces consensus? Scale problems: 8 billion people can't reach consensus on resource allocation.
  • "No ownership" - eliminates investment incentives, maintenance responsibilities, innovation rewards.
  • "Share first" - who determines sharing ratios? What prevents free-riding?

Logical contradictions:

  • Eliminating "classes" while requiring consensus management creates new hierarchy (consensus-makers vs. non-participants).
  • "Deprive only if no option" requires someone to judge "no option" - creates authority you've eliminated.
  • Without property rights, no mechanism to prevent stronger groups taking resources from weaker ones.

Historical evidence:

  • All commons-without-property experiments failed: Soviet collective farms (1920s-1991), Israeli kibbutzim (90% failed by 2000s), Jamestown colony (1607-1610).

Scale problems:

  • Consensus mechanisms break down >150 people (Dunbar's number).
  • No examples of consensus-based resource allocation working beyond small communities.

Assumption challenge: Your "Share first" principle assumes scarcity isn't real. Physical laws create genuine scarcity requiring allocation mechanisms. Without prices/ownership, no information system exists for efficient allocation.

What specific mechanism replaces price signals for resource allocation at scale?
