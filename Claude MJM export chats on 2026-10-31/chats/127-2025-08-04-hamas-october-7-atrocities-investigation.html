Based on extensive investigations by multiple reputable international organizations, there is substantial documented evidence of serious crimes committed by Hamas and associated groups during the October 7, 2023 attack. Here's what major human rights organizations have verified:

Documented Hamas Actions on October 7, 2023

Nearly 1,200 people were killed in Israel that day, including more than 800 civilians; 251 people were taken hostage, with some 120 still held captive in Gaza. Multiple independent investigations have documented these events:

Human Rights Watch Investigation: "Human Rights Watch research found that the Hamas-led assault on October 7 was designed to kill civilians and take as many people as possible hostage," said their crisis and conflict director. Researchers also verified and analyzed over 280 photographs and videos taken during the assault and posted on social media or shared directly with Human Rights Watch.

UN Commission of Inquiry: The COI found that Hamas and other Palestinian militants committed war crimes and violated international humanitarian and human rights law in their October 7, 2023, attack, and The commission said it had "documented evidence of sexual violence" carried out by Palestinian armed groups in several locations in southern Israel on October 7.

Specific Documented Acts: The investigations found evidence of deliberate targeting of civilians, including the torture, attempted beheading and killing of Thai workers in Nir Oz and the killing of 19 Thai and Nepalese exchange students in Alumim. Human Rights Watch's investigation found evidence of acts of sexual and gender-based violence, including forced nudity, and the taking and the posting on social media, both without consent, of sexualized images.

Important Context

However, it's crucial to note that multiple investigations have also found that some initial claims were exaggerated or false. The investigation by the I-Unit, which examined hours of footage from CCTV, dashcams, personal phones found that while Hamas committed serious abuses, certain claims repeated by Israeli politicians untrue.

The existence of documented crimes by Hamas does not justify or excuse what multiple international organizations have characterized as violations of international law by Israel in response. Its brutal military offensive had killed more than 42,000 Palestinians, including over 13,300 children, and injured over 97,000 more, by 7 October 2024, with Amnesty International concluding that Israel is committing genocide.

The documented evidence shows Hamas committed serious war crimes and crimes against humanity on October 7. However, under international law, war crimes by one party do not justify war crimes or genocide by another party. Both sets of actions are subject to international legal accountability.

Israeli Foreknowledge of October 7 Plans

Military Intelligence had detailed information on the group's plan to breach the Gaza border at dozens of points and attack dozens of communities and army posts, as reported by Haaretz.

Israeli intelligence should have issued an operational warning months, if not years, earlier. Israel's intelligence had full access to Hamas' plan of attack (code-named in Israel "the Walls of Jericho"), but it failed to convey this information to the relevant military planners.

Specific Intelligence Details: Newly revealed document shows the army was even aware of the number of captives the Palestinian group planned to take, according to Middle East Eye reporting. Israeli Policy of Funding Hamas

Direct Israeli Support for Qatari Funding: Qatar started sending money to the Gaza Strip on a monthly basis in 2018. $15 million worth of cash-filled suitcases were transported into Gaza by the Qataris via Israeli territory. Qatar was prepared to provide funds to the Gaza Strip through Hamas as early as the 2014 Israel-Hamas war to alleviate the humanitarian crisis there, the official said, and the US at the time left it up to the Israelis to decide whether they would permit this.

Israeli Strategic Rationale: Most of the time, Israeli policy was to treat the Palestinian Authority as a burden and Hamas as an asset. Far-right MK Bezalel Smotrich, now the finance minister in the hardline government and leader of the Religious Zionism party, said so himself in 2015.

Official Israeli Appreciation: A series of documents obtained by The Times of Israel reveal how top Israeli officials sought and expressed their appreciation for the financial support provided by Qatar to stabilize the humanitarian situation in Gaza in the years and months prior to Hamas's October 7 terror onslaught.

This represents a significant aspect of the story that is often underemphasized in discussions that focus solely on Hamas's documented crimes while ignoring Israel's role in strengthening the organization it now claims justifies its military response.

Yes, there have been significant documented reports that Israeli forces were responsible for some of the casualties initially attributed to Hamas on October 7. Here's what has been established:

The Hannibal Directive

Documents and testimonies obtained by Haaretz reveal the Hannibal Operational Order, which directs the use of force to prevent soldiers being taken into captivity, was employed at three army facilities infiltrated by Hamas, potentially endangering civilians as well.

It now seems, according to documents and testimonies viewed by Haaretz, that this and other orders were given that led to the deaths of a significant number of the 1,222 people—including Jews, Israeli Palestinians and Bedouins along with foreign nationals—that died during the October 7 attack.

Specific Documented Cases

Kibbutz Be'eri Incident: In Kibbutz Be'eri, an Israeli tank fired two shells into a house known to be holding over a dozen captives, including 12-year-old twins. Only two captives survived. Of the 14 who had been held hostage, 13 were killed in the intense firefight between Israeli troops and the Hamas terrorists. It remains unclear how many of the 13 had been harmed by the tank fire, although at least one of them, 68-year-old Adi Dagan, was said to have been killed by shrapnel.

Helicopter Fire: One, Efrat Katz, was killed by helicopter fire as she was being transported to Gaza by Hamas fighters.

Nova Festival: A police investigation shows Israeli Apache helicopters opened fire on attendees of the Nova music festival during the 7 October Hamas attack. The newspaper reported that the Israeli forces "found it difficult to identify Hamas militants," adding that helicopter pilots "used artillery" against civilians at the festival.

Scale of the Issue

Israeli military investigators "examined some 70 vehicles that … did not reach Gaza because on their way they had been hit by fire from a helicopter gunship, a UAV [unmanned aerial vehicle]."

There has already been one widely circulated report that Israeli forces killed at least 14 Israeli civilians on October 7 — killing 13 of them by firing from a tank at a house civilians were being held in, and killing one woman via a helicopter while she was being abducted.

Official Israeli Response

On 18 December the IDF confirmed that Israeli combatants "fell as a result of friendly fire on October 7," but added that "beyond the operational investigations of the events, it would not be morally sound to investigate these incidents."

These reports indicate that a significant but undetermined number of the October 7 casualties were caused by Israeli forces rather than Hamas, either through the deliberate application of the Hannibal Directive or through misidentification of targets. The exact scale remains unclear partly because Israel has declined to conduct full investigations into these incidents.

This information significantly complicates the narrative that all October 7 casualties were solely the result of Hamas actions, though it doesn't negate the documented war crimes that Hamas did commit.
