When I do it on the stove, I always put in about an inch or sometimes inch and a half of water above the material, rice or beans. How do you do it with a pressure cooker ?
Claude2026-10-25 22:23 UTC

It depends on the food. Beans work much like your stovetop method, but rice takes measured water, and less than you'd use on the stove, because a sealed pot loses little water to steam.

  • Beans: Cover soaked beans by about 2 inches of water. For unsoaked beans, a common guide is about 3 cups of water per cup of dry beans. Stovetop pressure cookers with a weighted regulator need about 1 tablespoon of oil per cup of dry beans to control foaming.
  • Lentils: About 2 cups of water per cup of lentils, or cover them by about 2 inches.
  • Rice: Measure instead of going by inches. One cooker manual's chart gives 1.5 cups of water per cup of white rice and about 1.6–1.7 cups per cup of brown rice.
  • Barley: I didn't find a source for barley, so check your cooker's chart.

In every case, the total of food plus water stays at or below the half line.

These ratios come from recipe sites, a pulse-industry guide and another brand's manual, not Presto's. Use your cooker's own chart first, since times and water amounts vary by model.
