Key NT Passages on Testing of Faith

James 1:2-3, 12

  • v.2-3: "Count it all joy when you fall into various πειρασμοῖς (peirasmois), knowing that the δοκίμιον (dokimion) of your faith produces endurance."
  • v.12: "Blessed is the man who endures πειρασμόν (peirasmon)..."

1 Peter 1:6-7

  • "...though now for a little while, if necessary, you have been grieved by various πειρασμοῖς (peirasmois), so that the δοκίμιον (dokimion) of your faith..."

Romans 5:3-4

  • "...tribulation produces endurance, and endurance produces δοκιμήν (dokimēn)..."

Hebrews 11:17

  • "By faith Abraham, when πειραζόμενος (peirazomenos), offered up Isaac..."

The Two Word-Groups

1. πειρασμός / πειράζω (peirasmos / peirazō)

Root meaning: To make trial of, to attempt, to test by experience.

Classical Greek usage:

  • Herodotus, Thucydides: used for testing enemy strength, attempting an enterprise, proving someone's loyalty or capacity through ordeal.
  • Neutral in classical usage—no inherent negative connotation. Context determined whether the "testing" was hostile (temptation to evil) or probative (proving quality).

LXX usage: Translates Hebrew נָסָה (nasah)—God "testing" Abraham (Gen 22:1), Israel "testing" God (Ex 17:2), etc.

NT semantic range:

  • External trial/affliction (James 1:2; 1 Pet 1:6)
  • Temptation to sin (Matt 4:1; 1 Cor 10:13)
  • The context determines which sense applies. James 1:13-14 explicitly disambiguates: God does not peirazō anyone toward evil.

2. δοκίμιον / δοκιμή / δοκιμάζω (dokimion / dokimē / dokimazō)

Root meaning: From δόκιμος (dokimos) = "approved," "genuine," "tested and found true."

Classical Greek usage:

  • Technical term in metallurgy and coinage: the dokimion was the assay, the crucible test that proved metal genuine.
  • Plato, Aristotle: used metaphorically for testing character, arguments, or claims.
  • Strong connotation of proving authenticity—not merely subjecting to trial, but demonstrating genuineness through the process.

NT usage:

  • James 1:3, 1 Peter 1:7: dokimion of faith = the means/process by which faith is proved genuine, like gold refined by fire.
  • Romans 5:4: dokimē = proven character, the result of endurance.
  • 2 Cor 8:2: dokimē = testing that revealed the Macedonians' genuine commitment.

Functional Distinction

Term Focus Analogy
πειρασμός The trial itself, the ordeal experienced The furnace
δοκίμιον The testing process that proves genuineness The assaying
δοκιμή The proven quality that results The certified gold

James 1:2-3 and 1 Peter 1:6-7 use both terms together: one falls into peirasmoi (trials), and these function as the dokimion (proving mechanism) of faith.

Cultural Context

Greek culture valued demonstrated virtue over claimed virtue. The gymnasium, the agōn (contest), the battlefield—all were dokimasia settings where character was publicly proved. The NT appropriates this framework: faith is not a static possession but a quality that must be—and can be—demonstrated genuine through ordeal.
