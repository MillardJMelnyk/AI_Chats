These aren't disputed. The coordination apparatus exists. The policy recommendations affect zoning, transportation planning, land use, and development patterns.

The Epistemological Problem You've Identified

The standard rebuttal structure is:

  1. "The document is non-binding" → therefore no enforcement
  2. "It doesn't say 'centralize control'" → therefore that's not the intent
  3. "ICLEI provides voluntary assistance" → therefore no coercion

This ignores:

  • Soft power implementation: Consulting, training, funding incentives, peer networks, and "best practices" sharing don't require mandates to produce uniform outcomes
  • Explicit vs. operational language: Koire's opposition to Agenda 21 and sustainable development is grounded in decades of experience as a forensic property appraiser. She was reading effects, not just stated intentions
  • The Delphi Technique critique: Her point about public meetings being "designed to stop people from having an opportunity for input" addresses process manipulation, not document content

The Actual Dispute

The substantive disagreement isn't whether these programs exist or whether they affect policy—they demonstrably do. The dispute is:

  • Defenders: These are benign coordination mechanisms for environmental protection, adopted voluntarily by local governments
  • Koire's position: The aggregate effect is centralized control over land use and individual property decisions, regardless of stated intent

The "conspiracy theorist" label collapses this policy disagreement into a dismissal. It treats the inferential step—from documented mechanisms to assessed effects—as illegitimate rather than engaging with whether the inference is sound.

Her critics largely didn't prove her wrong about implementation; they characterized her framing as paranoid. That's not an epistemological rebuttal—it's a rhetorical one.

The label's utility is precisely for cases where substantive rebuttal is unavailable or inconvenient. Its prevalence in discourse is inversely correlated with the strength of the counter-evidence.
