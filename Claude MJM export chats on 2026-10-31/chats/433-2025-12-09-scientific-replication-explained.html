In science, what's "replication"?
Claude2025-12-09 18:23 UTC

Replication is repeating a study or experiment using the same methods and procedures to verify whether the original results can be obtained again, ideally by independent researchers. A successful replication supports the original finding's reliability; failure to replicate casts doubt on it.

is replication the correct term?
Claude2025-12-09 18:23 UTC

No, not precisely. The terminology varies:

Replication typically refers to repeating a study using identical methods. Reproducibility or replicability are more commonly used now for independent verification of results, with some fields distinguishing them further:

  • Reproducibility: same data + same analysis = consistent results
  • Replicability: different researchers/data + similar methods = consistent results

The National Academies (2019) standardized these definitions, but usage remains inconsistent across disciplines. What I described initially—independent researchers verifying findings—is more precisely replicability, though "replication" is still commonly used for this.
