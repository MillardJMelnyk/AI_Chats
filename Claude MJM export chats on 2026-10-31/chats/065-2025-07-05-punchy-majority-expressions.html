Here are some more punchy alternatives to "overwhelming majority":

For formal/professional contexts:

  • "vast majority"
  • "decisive majority"
  • "supermajority"
  • "landslide majority"

For more impactful/dramatic effect:

  • "crushing majority"
  • "sweeping majority"
  • "resounding majority"
  • "commanding majority"

For maximum punch:

  • "lion's share"
  • "nearly all"
  • "virtually all"
  • "all but a few"

The best choice depends on your context and tone. "Vast majority" is crisp and professional, while "crushing majority" or "lion's share" pack more emotional weight. "Nearly all" is often the most concise and impactful option.

Millard2025-07-05 17:17 UTC
which is more common, incontestably or indisputably?
Claude2025-07-05 17:18 UTC

Indisputably is more common.

"Indisputably" gets used more frequently in both formal and casual contexts - you hear it in everyday speech, journalism, and academic writing. "Incontestably" sounds more formal and less conversational, so it appears less often in general usage.

Both mean essentially the same thing, but "indisputably" has that everyday familiarity that makes it the go-to choice for most people.

Millard2025-07-05 17:18 UTC
which is stronger?
Claude2025-07-05 17:18 UTC

Incontestably is stronger.

"Incontestably" means it literally cannot be contested or challenged - there's no possible argument against it. It's absolute.

"Indisputably" means it's not being disputed, but theoretically could be. It's about the current state of agreement rather than the impossibility of disagreement.

So while "indisputably" is more common, "incontestably" carries more force - it shuts down the possibility of challenge rather than just noting the absence of current challenge.
