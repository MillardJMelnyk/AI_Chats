Draft Pitch Letter:

Dear Grace,

Your work in Vulture Capitalism demonstrates that the "free market" is a fiction—that capitalism actually operates as a planned system serving corporate and elite interests. I'd like to propose that the planning mechanism has a name: ownership. The assumption that withholding is default and sharing requires justification isn't incidental to corporate capture; it's the operating principle that makes capture inevitable.

You've shown that monopoly power, backdoor bailouts, and democratic deficit aren't aberrations but features of how capitalism actually works. My 30+ years researching psychological mechanisms of social control—originally through cult studies—suggests these features trace to something deeper than institutional capture: the ownership paradigm itself. The same dynamics enabling authoritarian groups (manufactured scarcity, externalized authority, trained dependency) appear structurally wherever exclusive control over resources becomes the organizing principle.

"Share-first economics" inverts the burden of proof: instead of asking why we should democratize the economy, we ask what justifies concentrating control in the first place. This isn't idealism—it's recognizing that sharing is our biological default before ownership ideology trains it out of us. Children share naturally; commons functioned for millennia; open-source outperforms proprietary in specific domains. The question becomes: what maintains the ownership default against observable cooperative efficiency?

Your call for economic democracy addresses institutional arrangements. Share-first thinking addresses the conceptual frame that makes those institutions seem natural. The "death of freedom" you diagnose may be less about corporate power corrupting markets than about ownership logic making domination the default relationship between people and resources.

I'd welcome exploring how these frameworks might intersect—whether through correspondence, your Substack, or Tribune.

Respectfully, Millard J. Melnyk
