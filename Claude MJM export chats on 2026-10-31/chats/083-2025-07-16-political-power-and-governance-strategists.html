Several notable thinkers have written influential works on statecraft and governance, similar to Machiavelli's approach:

Ancient and Classical:

  • Sun Tzu wrote "The Art of War," which applies strategic thinking to both military and political leadership
  • Kautilya (Chanakya) authored the "Arthashastra," an ancient Indian treatise on statecraft, economic policy, and political strategy that's often compared to "The Prince"
  • Han Feizi developed Legalist philosophy in ancient China, emphasizing strict laws and centralized power

Modern Political Theorists:

  • Thomas Hobbes in "Leviathan" argued for absolute sovereign power to maintain order
  • Carl Schmitt wrote extensively on political theology and the nature of sovereignty, including "The Concept of the Political"
  • Leo Strauss examined the relationship between philosophy and politics, influencing neoconservative thought

Practical Strategists:

  • Henry Kissinger has written numerous works on diplomacy and realpolitik, including "Diplomacy"
  • Robert Greene authored "The 48 Laws of Power," which applies Machiavellian principles to modern contexts
  • Saul Alinsky wrote "Rules for Radicals," focusing on community organizing and political activism

These authors share Machiavelli's interest in the practical mechanics of power, though they approach it from different philosophical, cultural, and historical perspectives. Many emphasize the tension between moral ideals and political effectiveness that Machiavelli famously explored.
