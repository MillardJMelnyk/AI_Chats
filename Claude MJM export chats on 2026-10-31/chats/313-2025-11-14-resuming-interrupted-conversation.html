What the data actually show:

What you're arguing:

The imbalance reflects a deeper epistemological orientation: outside-in (normative/third-person) vs. inside-out (experiential/first-person).

The gap:

  • Pathology research can be studied experientially (phenomenological approaches to depression, lived experience of trauma)
  • Well-being research can be studied normatively (objective metrics of flourishing, behavioral correlates of happiness)

The actual correlation is probabilistic, not deterministic:

  • Pathology research tends toward outside-in orientation (DSM criteria, observable symptoms, third-person diagnosis)
  • Well-being research tends toward inside-out orientation (subjective experience, meaning-making, first-person reports)

But these are tendencies, not identities.

For ICSA:

You can argue the DSM expansion exemplifies the outside-in orientation (classification from observer perspective, symptom checklists, normative deviation). But you cannot claim DSM = outside-in and well-being = inside-out as perfect equivalents.

The imbalance in subject matter (pathology vs. well-being) is evidence suggestive of but not identical to the imbalance in epistemological stance (outside-in vs. inside-out).
