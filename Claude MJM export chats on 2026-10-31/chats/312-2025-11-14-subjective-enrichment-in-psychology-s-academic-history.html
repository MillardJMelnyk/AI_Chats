Summarize the work of each, Csikszentmihalyi and Diener.
Claude2025-11-14 15:55 UTC

Csikszentmihalyi (Flow Research)

Core concept: Flow = optimal experience state characterized by complete absorption in challenging activity matched to skill level.

Key features:

  • Clear goals, immediate feedback
  • Merging of action and awareness
  • Loss of self-consciousness
  • Distorted time perception
  • Intrinsic motivation

Method: Experience Sampling Method (ESM) - participants carry beepers, report experience when signaled throughout day. Real-time data, not retrospective.

Findings: Flow occurs across cultures, activities (work, sport, art, etc.) when challenge-skill balance achieved. Predicts performance, creativity, well-being.

Major works: Beyond Boredom and Anxiety (1975), Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience (1990).

Diener (Subjective Well-Being Research)

Core concept: Subjective well-being (SWB) = how people evaluate their lives, comprising:

  • Life satisfaction (cognitive judgment)
  • Positive affect (pleasant emotions)
  • Negative affect (unpleasant emotions)

Method: Psychometric scales, particularly Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS, 1985). Cross-sectional and longitudinal surveys.

Findings: SWB partially stable (set-point theory), influenced by personality, circumstances, adaptation. Income correlation weaker than expected. Cultural variation in predictors.

Major contribution: Operationalized and standardized measurement of happiness/life satisfaction, enabling large-scale empirical research.

Major works: Extensive journal publications 1980s-present; edited Well-Being: The Foundations of Hedonic Psychology (1999).
