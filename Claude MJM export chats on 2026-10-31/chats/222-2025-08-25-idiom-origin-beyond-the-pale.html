Etymology: Irish "Pale" = English-controlled territory around Dublin, 14th-18th centuries. Physical boundary marked by wooden stakes/fences.

Original meaning: Literally outside English jurisdiction/civilization in Ireland.

Current meaning: Unacceptable behavior that violates social/moral boundaries.

First recorded use: 1720s literal, 1860s figurative.

Usage shift: Geographic → behavioral/moral transgression.

Literal parsing: "Simple human decency violates acceptable political boundaries."

Logical flaw: Misuses idiom. "Beyond the pale" = unacceptable/wrong. Statement claims decency is unacceptable.

Intended meaning (likely): "Simple human decency is absent/excluded from politics" or "Politics has moved beyond caring about decency."

Correct phrasing: "Simple human decency is beyond the pale of politics" or "In politics, abandoning decency is beyond the pale."

Your statement: Grammatically suggests decency itself is wrong, not that politics rejects decency.
