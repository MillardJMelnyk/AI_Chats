This claim isn't supported by genetic research. Multiple peer-reviewed genetic studies have found that both Jewish populations (including Israeli Jews) and Palestinians share significant Middle Eastern ancestry and are closely related to each other genetically.

Studies using DNA analysis have consistently shown that:

  • Jewish populations from different regions (Ashkenazi, Sephardic, Mizrahi) retain substantial Middle Eastern genetic components despite centuries of diaspora
  • Palestinians also carry predominantly Middle Eastern genetic signatures
  • Both groups show genetic continuity with ancient Levantine populations
  • The genetic distance between Jewish and Palestinian populations is relatively small compared to their distance from other world populations

The term "Semitic" itself is primarily linguistic rather than genetic - it refers to a family of languages including Hebrew, Arabic, and Aramaic. When applied to populations, it's more of a cultural/linguistic designation than a precise genetic category.

It's worth noting that genetic ancestry is complex and populations have mixed over millennia. Modern genetic studies focus on measurable DNA markers rather than older racial or ethnic categories. The scientific evidence indicates both Israelis (particularly those of Middle Eastern Jewish origin) and Palestinians have substantial indigenous Middle Eastern ancestry, making claims that one group is "more Semitic" than the other scientifically unfounded.

These kinds of claims often appear in politically charged contexts, but the genetic research doesn't support using ancestry arguments to validate or invalidate either group's connection to the region.

Core Methodological Problems:

  3. Geographic Positioning System (GPS) Method: Elhaik developed a new method called GPS that other geneticists argued hadn't been sufficiently validated for this type of ancestry analysis.

Scientific Response: The 2014 response in Human Biology wasn't just dismissive - it included detailed reanalysis of data and alternative methodologies.
