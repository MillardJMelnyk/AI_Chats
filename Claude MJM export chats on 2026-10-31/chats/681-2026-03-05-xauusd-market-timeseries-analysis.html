STAGE 1 — "Should I enter this trade?"

Entry method — Three pre-built recipes for deciding if a reversal is real. Mix 1 is the most permissive (fires most often, more wrong calls). Mix 2 is the middle ground. Mix 3 is the pickiest (fires least, fewer false alarms but misses some real reversals).

G2 Climax threshold (1.991) — How big was the last bar of the run compared to the average bar in the run? Only used by Mix 3. Higher = you only enter when the run ended with a dramatic blowoff candle. Lower = you'll accept quieter endings. At 1.991, the last bar needs to be roughly twice the average — very selective.

G3 b1 BRR threshold (0.551) — How "decisive" is the first opposing bar? A bar that's all body and no wicks scores near 1.0. A bar that's all wicks and no body scores near 0.0. Higher = you demand a committed, full-bodied opposing bar before entering. Lower = you'll accept wicky, uncertain bars. Used by Mix 2 and Mix 3.

G7 Opposing count threshold (2) — How many bars in a row have gone against the run before you act? Higher = you wait for more confirmation (safer but later entry, worse price). Lower = you act faster (riskier but better price). At 2, you need at least two consecutive opposing bars. Used by Mix 2 and Mix 3.

G8 Pullback threshold (0.487) — How far has price retraced into the run's range? Measured as a fraction: 0.487 means price has pulled back roughly half the distance the run covered. Higher = you demand a deeper retracement before calling it a reversal (fewer signals, more confident). Lower = you'll act on shallow pullbacks (more signals, less certain). Used by all three mixes.

G9 Extension threshold (0.068) — Has price made new ground beyond where the run topped/bottomed? At 0.068, you're saying "if price has already pushed more than ~7% past the run's extreme, this isn't a reversal, skip it." Higher = you're more tolerant of some extension before dismissing the signal. Lower = any new ground at all and you pass. Used by Mix 1 and Mix 3.

STAGE 2 — "Was I wrong? Should I bail?"

These fire AFTER you've entered a reversal trade. Each tier is a different test for whether the reversal is actually a feint (fake-out) and you should flip back.

Tier 0 Geometric (bar 4) — The earliest possible "I was wrong" check. Compares the biggest resumption bar's body to the biggest opposing bar's body. MBR of 0.615 means: if a bar in the original trend direction already has a body 62% as large as the biggest reversal bar, get out. Lower = more sensitive (bails sooner on less evidence). Tier C MBR+Ext (bar 10+) — Combines max body ratio with whether price is extending. The extension threshold of -0.025 means "price doesn't even have to make new ground — just getting close to the run's extreme is enough." More negative = even easier to trigger.
