Saltwater lamps: real or fake?

Created 2025-12-22 15:33 UTC · Last updated 2025-12-22 15:34 UTC · 2 messages

Millard2025-12-22 15:33 UTC
I just saw a video of so-called saltwater lamps which have two copper plates and a light bulb between them that lights up as soon as you pour salt water into them. Is this real?
Claude2025-12-22 15:34 UTC

Yes, the core phenomenon is real. It's a galvanic cell—two dissimilar metals in an electrolyte (saltwater) generate electrical current through oxidation-reduction reactions.

Critical constraints:

  1. Voltage output: A single copper-zinc or copper-aluminum cell produces ~0.5-1V. Standard bulbs need significantly more. These lamps either use multiple cells in series or low-voltage LEDs (not incandescent bulbs).

  2. Current capacity: Extremely limited. Enough to light a small LED, not a meaningful light source. Power output typically under 1 watt.

  3. Duration: Electrodes corrode. The anode (zinc/aluminum) dissolves into the salt solution. Lifespan depends on electrode mass—hours to days, not indefinite.

  4. Replacement requirement: You must replace the consumed electrode and refresh the electrolyte periodically.

What marketing obscures:

  • "Just add saltwater" omits that electrodes are consumables
  • Actual light output is minimal
  • It's chemistry demonstration packaged as functional lamp

The physics works. The utility claims are oversold.
