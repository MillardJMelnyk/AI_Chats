A native american friend of mine distinguishes European-style civilizations from those in the Americas pre-invasion. Here's what he wrote: There were quite a number of large-scale civilizations on the western supercontinent built without these things. Cahokia and its contemporaries existed on the northern continent as large cities with advanced agricultural advancements and intertribal trade. In what is now Mexico, Teotihuacan and Tenochtitlan operated in much the same way- and in the South there were Cusco and Quito. This is only to name a few, and trade existed even between them over thousands of miles. These were places with irrigation, sewage, metallurgy, economic systems and even forms of electricity. Astrological, scientific and medical (even surgical) knowledge on this continent was thousands of years ahead of Europe. Our oral traditions even speak to knowledge of energies akin to nuclear power and some families still carry oral traditions that include the details of that. These things were done through observation, cooperation and humility. Not slavery, genocide, colonization or theft of land and resources. People who come from cultures which teach us to remain always a student of nature and those who come before us are less held back by silly arrogance that has kept “western society” so inefficient and destructive. Looking at the specific technological claims against archaeological evidence:

Societies with claimed technologies:

Cahokia (900-1350 CE)

  • Population: Peak ~10,000-20,000
  • Had: Irrigation (limited), trade networks, mound construction
  • Lacked: Sewage systems, metallurgy, electricity
  • Governance: Hierarchical chiefdom with elite burial mounds
  • Warfare: Evidence of palisades, violent deaths
  • Human sacrifice: Yes (retainer sacrifice documented)

Teotihuacan (100 BCE-550 CE)

  • Population: Peak ~125,000-200,000
  • Had: Urban planning, limited drainage, obsidian working
  • Lacked: True sewage systems, advanced metallurgy, electricity
  • Governance: Unclear but hierarchical (priest-kings likely)
  • Warfare: Military iconography, weapons production
  • Human sacrifice: Yes (archaeological evidence)

Tenochtitlan (1325-1521)

  • Population: ~200,000-400,000
  • Had: Chinampas (agriculture), causeways, limited copper/bronze
  • Lacked: Electricity, iron metallurgy
  • Governance: Monarchical empire with nobility
  • Warfare: Constant (flower wars, expansion)
  • Human sacrifice: Yes (extensively documented)

Cusco/Inca (1438-1533)

  • Population: ~150,000 (empire: 10-12 million)
  • Had: Irrigation, road systems, bronze metallurgy
  • Lacked: Electricity, iron, wheel technology
  • Governance: Divine monarchy, strict hierarchy
  • Warfare: Imperial expansion across Andes
  • Human sacrifice: Yes (capacocha rituals)

Critical corrections:

  • No pre-Columbian society had electricity
  • European medicine by 1500s included anatomy, surgery, and pharmacology - not "thousands of years behind"
  • All large-scale societies listed practiced warfare and had ruling elites
  • No archaeological evidence supports nuclear energy knowledge

The claimed distinction between cooperation-based and coercion-based civilizations doesn't hold up to archaeological evidence.
