April 2-3, 2025:

Trump announced "Liberation Day" tariffs on April 2, 2025: a 10% baseline tariff on all imports starting April 5, with higher reciprocal tariffs (up to 54% on China) planned for April 9, and 25% auto tariffs that took effect at midnight on April 3.

The Plunge (April 3-4):

The announcement triggered the largest global market crash since 2020, with the S&P 500 falling nearly 5% on April 3 and another 6% on April 4. This was a severe risk-off event. NZD, as a commodity/risk currency, plunged as investors fled to safety.

The Immediate Retracement:

Contrary to standard models, the USD sharply depreciated rather than appreciated on Liberation Day, with the dollar temporarily switching from a safe-haven to a "risk-on" currency. The dollar gapped down on April 3.

USD weakness offset NZD's risk-off decline, causing NZDUSD to rebound. The unusual dollar behavior—falling during a crisis instead of rising—dominated the pair's movement and drove the immediate retracement.

Mechanism: Risk-off panic → NZD falls, but USD falls harder (lost safe-haven status) → NZDUSD rebounds.
