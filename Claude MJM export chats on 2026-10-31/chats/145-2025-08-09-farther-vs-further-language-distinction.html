Yes, there is a traditional distinction between "farther" and "further," though it's becoming less rigid in modern usage.

Farther is traditionally used for physical distance - things you can measure:

  • "The store is farther down the road"
  • "I can throw the ball farther than you"
  • "We walked farther into the woods"

Further is traditionally used for figurative, abstract, or metaphorical distance:

  • "Let's discuss this further" (more extensively)
  • "Further research is needed" (additional)
  • "Nothing could be further from the truth" (more distant conceptually)

The key memory trick: "farther" has "far" in it, which relates to physical distance.

That said, "further" is increasingly used in both contexts in everyday speech, and many dictionaries now list them as largely interchangeable. "Farther" being used for abstract concepts is less common, though.

In formal or academic writing, maintaining the traditional distinction is generally preferred, but in casual conversation, using "further" for both contexts is widely accepted and understood.

I start with Harry Frankfurt's classic definition—assertions made in complete disregard for truth, with the aim of persuading or impressing rather than representing reality—and take it a step further. I want to develop a crisp, practical, empirically identifiable characterization:

Bullshit is any information offered as fact or truth when nothing has been done to ascertain how representative it is (or is not) of what's really going on.

The main changes:

  • Split the long sentence into two parts for better readability
  • Made "I want to develop" explicit rather than implied
  • Added "when" to make the conditional relationship clearer
  • Put "(or is not)" in parentheses to reduce the awkward double negative construction
  • Removed the colon after "characterization" since it was separating the setup from the definition too abruptly
