Need more specific data on separation methods.

Flaws in premise: "Justice" ≠ always separation. Many historical systems used corporal punishment, fines, restitution without separation. Modern restorative justice explicitly avoids separation.

Separation methods (least to most violent):

  1. Restraining/protective orders (modern, ~1970s+): Legal distance maintenance, no physical confinement
  2. Probation/parole supervision (1840s+): Restricted movement, monitored freedom
  3. House arrest/electronic monitoring (1960s+): Home confinement
  4. Shunning/social exile (ancient+): Community ostracism
  5. Internal exile (ancient+): Banishment within homeland boundaries, like Russian Katorga system
  6. Banishment/exile (ancient+): Complete removal from territory, practiced in ancient Greece (10-year political exile), widespread in early civilizations
  7. Transportation (1600s-1800s): Forced relocation to colonies, practiced 16th-19th centuries with "inhumane conditions"
  8. Imprisonment (widespread post-1800s): Physical confinement in institutions
  9. Life imprisonment (1800s+): Permanent incarceration
  10. Capital punishment (ancient+): Execution, permanent biological separation

Assumption attack: Primary ancient/medieval punishment was banishment/exile, not separation universally—incarceration rare until 19th century
