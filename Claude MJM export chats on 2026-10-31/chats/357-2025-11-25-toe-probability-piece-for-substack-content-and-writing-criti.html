Major Problems:

1. Probability Calculation Fallacy The cascading probability argument commits the "lottery fallacy" - calculating the odds of a specific outcome rather than any functional outcome. Evolution doesn't aim for predetermined targets; it filters whatever works. The calculation also treats each step as independent when selection is cumulative, preserving gains. A math whiz in 1900 who understood statistics did exist - they didn't debunk evolution because the calculation framework is category-error.

2. Category 1/2 Distinction is Epistemologically Incoherent By this standard, no historical science survives. Geology, cosmology, forensics, archaeology - all rely on inferring past events from present evidence. The demand for "actually did" evidence apart from convergent lines of inference would require direct observation of past events, which is impossible for any historical claim. This isn't a problem unique to evolution; it's a problem with demanding impossible epistemic standards selectively.

3. Misrepresentation of Evidence The table claims all evidence is Category 1. This ignores observed speciation (Lenski's E. coli experiment, apple maggot flies), ERV insertions at identical genomic loci in related species (statistically inexplicable without common descent), and successful phylogenetic predictions confirmed by subsequent fossil finds.

4. Non-Sequitur Core Argument "If they lied about ToE, they lied about everything" does not follow. Each claim requires independent evaluation.

5. Source Quality The document cites AI chatbot outputs as authoritative confirmation. This is circular - you prompted the calculations, then cite the outputs as independent validation.
