Chronic piriformis dysfunction from prolonged sitting.

Sitting for extended periods:

  • Keeps piriformis in shortened position
  • Reduces blood flow to the muscle
  • Weakens it from disuse
  • Creates adhesions/trigger points in the tissue

When you suddenly load it (weight-bearing + rotation), the weakened, tight muscle spasms because it can't handle the demand.

What to do:

  1. Strengthen it - clamshell exercises, side-lying hip abduction, resistance band external rotation
  2. Stretch it - piriformis stretch (figure-4 position), hip external rotation stretches
  3. Break up sitting - stand/move every 30-60 minutes
  4. Release trigger points - lacrosse ball or foam roller directly on the spot, or get it manually released

Strengthening is more important than stretching for this pattern. Weak + tight needs strength first.

What are the most effective exercises targeting the piriformis?
Claude2026-01-19 10:48 UTC

Strengthening (priority for your situation):

Clamshells - side-lying, knees bent, feet together, lift top knee while keeping feet touching. Resistance band above knees increases load.

Side-lying hip abduction - same position, straighten top leg, lift it directly upward.

Fire hydrants - hands and knees, lift one knee out to the side while keeping it bent 90°.

Single-leg bridge - lie on back, one foot planted, other leg extended, drive through planted foot to lift hips. Forces piriformis to stabilize.

Resistance band external rotation - seated or standing, band around knees or ankles, push knees/feet outward against resistance.

Stretching (you're already doing):

Figure-4 stretch - ankle on opposite knee, pull knee toward chest.

Pigeon pose - front leg bent 90°, back leg extended behind.

Clamshells and resistance band external rotation are most direct piriformis activation. Single-leg bridges integrate it functionally under load, which matches your problem pattern.

Setup:

  • Lie flat on your back on the floor
  • Bend one knee, place that foot flat on the floor near your butt
  • Other leg: either keep it bent with foot off the ground, or straighten it and hold it in the air

Movement:

  • Push through the foot that's on the floor

  • Hold briefly at the top
  • Lower back down
  • Repeat

The glutes and piriformis on the planted leg side have to work hard to lift and stabilize your entire body weight on one leg.
