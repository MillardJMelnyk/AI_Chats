I'm a social researcher, experimenter, change agent, theorist, and writer. How do I convey that without sounding pretentious?
Claude2025-07-19 21:28 UTC

Here are some approaches that feel more grounded and accessible:

Focus on what you do, not what you are:

  • "I research how communities change and test ideas for social impact"
  • "I study social patterns and experiment with new approaches to create change"
  • "I research, write, and test theories about how society works"

Use concrete examples:

  • "I'm researching how neighborhoods adapt to economic shifts and testing community-based solutions"
  • "I study social movements and write about what makes change efforts succeed or fail"

Combine roles naturally:

  • "I do social research and write about it, often testing new theories through small experiments"
  • "I research social change, develop theories about what works, and write about the results"

Lead with your most relatable role: If you're introducing yourself to academics, lead with "researcher." For practitioners, maybe "I work on social change initiatives." For general audiences, "writer" or "I study how communities solve problems" might land better.

Show uncertainty and curiosity:

  • "I'm trying to understand how social change really happens"
  • "I research and experiment with different approaches to community problems"

The key is matching your language to your audience and letting your actual work speak for the breadth of what you do, rather than listing impressive-sounding titles upfront.
