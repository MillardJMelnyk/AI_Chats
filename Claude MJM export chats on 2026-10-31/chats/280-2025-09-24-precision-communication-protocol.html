Claude2025-09-24 16:31 UTC

Flaws in your definition:

  1. Circular reasoning - "epistemic work" undefined. What counts? How much? Who decides?

  2. False binary - Ignores spectrum between rigorous verification and zero effort. Most human knowledge sits in middle ground.

  3. Impossible standard - Requires omniscience about speaker's verification process. You can't know what someone "never tried."

  4. Contradicts established usage - Frankfurt's influential definition focuses on indifference to truth, not verification effort. Your redefinition lacks justification.

  5. Practical absurdity - Makes most conversational statements "bullshit" (weather comments, casual observations, inherited knowledge).

  6. Intent irrelevant - Someone stating verified facts while lying about their verification process wouldn't be "bullshitting" under your definition.

  7. Verification paradox - How do you verify someone "deliberately avoided" verification without being guilty of your own definition?

Counter-steelman: Could be useful for academic/scientific contexts where verification standards are explicit and measurable.

Bottom line: Definition is internally inconsistent, practically unworkable, and solves no problem that existing terms don't already address.
