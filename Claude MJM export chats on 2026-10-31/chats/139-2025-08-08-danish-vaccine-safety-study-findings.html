Yes, this appears to be accurate based on a major study published in July 2025. The study examined medical records of 1.2 million children born in Denmark from 1997 through 2018, following them for over two decades until the end of 2020.

The research found no statistical association between aluminum content in vaccines and increased risk of developing autism, autoimmune diseases, asthma, or allergic conditions. The study examined 50 different health conditions and found no link between vaccines and any of them, including autism and asthma.

This was specifically focused on aluminum-containing vaccines, which use aluminum as an adjuvant (a substance that helps boost immune response). Denmark's meticulous government health record-keeping system allowed researchers to compare children who received different amounts of aluminum in their vaccines.

Experts have labeled this the "largest and most definitive ever conducted" study of its kind. The findings were published in the journal Annals of Internal Medicine and add to the substantial body of scientific evidence showing vaccines are safe and do not cause autism or other chronic conditions.

This study is particularly significant because of Denmark's comprehensive healthcare registry system, which allows for exceptionally thorough long-term tracking of health outcomes across an entire population.
