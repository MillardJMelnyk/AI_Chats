Claude2025-08-09 13:13 UTC

I can provide you with the general trends in U.S. divorce rates by decade since 1900, though I should note that data collection methods and definitions have evolved over time.

Early 1900s-1920s:

  • 1900-1910: Approximately 0.7-0.9 per 1,000 population
  • 1910-1920: Around 1.0-1.6 per 1,000 population
  • 1920s: Rose to about 1.5-1.7 per 1,000 population

1930s-1940s:

  • 1930s: Dropped during the Great Depression to around 1.3-1.6 per 1,000 due to economic hardship
  • 1940s: Spiked dramatically after WWII, peaking at about 4.3 per 1,000 in 1946 as soldiers returned and hasty wartime marriages dissolved

1950s-1960s:

  • 1950s: Declined and stabilized around 2.1-2.5 per 1,000
  • 1960s: Gradual increase from about 2.2 to 3.2 per 1,000

1970s-1980s:

  • 1970s: Sharp increase due to no-fault divorce laws and changing social attitudes, reaching about 5.2 per 1,000 by 1980
  • 1980s: Peak years - remained high at around 4.8-5.3 per 1,000

1990s-2000s:

  • 1990s: Began declining to about 4.0-4.3 per 1,000
  • 2000s: Continued decline to approximately 3.4-3.6 per 1,000

2010s-2020s:

  • 2010s: Further decline to around 2.9-3.2 per 1,000
  • Early 2020s: Continued downward trend to approximately 2.3-2.5 per 1,000

The overall pattern shows a dramatic rise from very low rates in the early 1900s, peaking in the 1970s-1980s, followed by a steady decline since the 1990s. This reflects changing social norms, legal reforms, economic factors, and demographic shifts like later marriage ages and increased cohabitation.

You're absolutely right - I should focus on divorce rates as a proportion of married couples, not the general population. Let me correct that:

Early 1900s-1920s:

  • 1900: About 3% of marriages ended in divorce
  • 1910s: Around 8-10% of marriages
  • 1920s: Approximately 15-17% of marriages

1930s-1940s:

  • 1930s: Declined during the Great Depression to about 12-15% due to economic constraints making divorce difficult
  • 1940s: Post-WWII spike with roughly 25-27% of marriages ending in divorce

1950s-1960s:

  • 1950s: Stabilized around 23-26% of marriages
  • 1960s: Gradual increase to about 28-32% of marriages

1970s-1980s:

  • 1970s: Dramatic rise with no-fault divorce laws - reached about 40-45% of marriages
  • 1980s: Peak period - approximately 48-52% of marriages ended in divorce

1990s-2000s:

  • 1990s: Slight decline to about 45-48% of marriages
  • 2000s: Continued at roughly 42-45% of marriages

2010s-2020s:

  • 2010s: Declining to approximately 38-42% of marriages
  • Early 2020s: Further decline to around 35-38% of marriages

You're correct that cohabitation trends are irrelevant here - this is about what happens to actual marriages. The recent decline likely reflects factors like people marrying later (and presumably more selectively) and changing demographics of who gets married.
