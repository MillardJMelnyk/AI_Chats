Claude2025-12-01 15:33 UTC

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD), recurrent — DSM-5/DSM-5-TR terminology as of January 2025.

The pattern you describe (chronic course since adolescence, severe episodes lasting weeks to months, cyclic recurrence requiring maintenance medication) fits the diagnostic criteria for recurrent MDD.

If depressive symptoms persist between major episodes for 2+ years, it could also be classified as Persistent Depressive Disorder (PDD) with intermittent major depressive episodes, but the severe cyclic nature you describe is more characteristic of recurrent MDD.

Claude2025-12-01 16:13 UTC

~17-21% of US adults have or will develop chronic/recurrent depression (lifetime prevalence).

Key data:

  • 21% lifetime MDD prevalence (Hasin et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2018)
  • 80% of people with first MDD episode will have at least one more during their life
  • 50% recurrence after first episode, 70% after second, 90% after third

So: 21% × 80% recurrence = ~17% have chronic/recurrent depression as an ongoing condition.

This excludes single-episode/situational cases and counts people for whom depression is a recurring condition they manage over time—regardless of whether they're currently in an episode.
