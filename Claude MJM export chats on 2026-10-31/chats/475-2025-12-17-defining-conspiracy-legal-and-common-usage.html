What's the most widely recognized definition of conspiracy
Claude2025-12-17 07:53 UTC

Legal definition (most codified):

An agreement between two or more persons to commit an unlawful act or to accomplish a lawful act through unlawful means.

Dictionary definition (Merriam-Webster):

"An agreement among conspirators" or "a secret plan made by two or more people to do something that is harmful or illegal."

The legal definition is more widely recognized in formal/institutional contexts. The dictionary definition captures common usage. Both center on: (1) multiple actors, (2) agreement/plan, (3) unlawful or harmful intent.

I often use the metaphor of smoke billowing from death camp chimneys while Germans in the nearby villages pretended not to see. Is that actually inaccurate?
Claude2025-12-17 08:09 UTC

Accurate for specific camps.

Documented facts:

  • Auschwitz-Birkenau: Smoke from crematoria visible from surrounding areas, particularly 1943-1944 during peak operations. Aerial reconnaissance photos confirm this.

  • Dachau, Buchenwald, Mauthausen: Located near/within sight of German towns. Multiple survivor and local testimonies describe visible smoke and pervasive odor of burning bodies.

  • Post-war testimony: Interrogations of local residents show claims of ignorance contradicted by physical impossibility of not noticing smoke, smell, ash fallout, and emaciated prisoners used as labor.

Limitation: Not universal. Some camps were more isolated or lacked crematoria. But your metaphor accurately describes conditions at major camps near population centers.

Historical consensus: Widespread willful ignorance among nearby populations is well-documented, particularly 1942-1945. The "pretending not to see" characterization is supported by evidence, not just inference.

Your metaphor is empirically grounded for the camps where it applies.
