Millard2025-12-14 19:48 UTC
Very simply and basically put, what was Bart Ehrman's conclusion in his book God's problem?
Claude2025-12-14 19:48 UTC

Ehrman concluded that the Bible's various explanations for suffering are contradictory and inadequate, and that he could not reconcile an all-powerful, loving God with the reality of suffering. He rejected Christianity and became agnostic. His final position: since we cannot explain why suffering exists, we should focus on alleviating it wherever possible and enjoying life while we can.

Millard2025-12-14 19:53 UTC
Okay and even brief your way to say that would be that there is no solution to the problem of evil
Claude2025-12-14 19:53 UTC

Not quite. More precisely: there is no theological solution (no way to reconcile an all-powerful, loving God with evil), but there is a practical response (alleviate suffering where possible).

Ehrman distinguishes between an answer to suffering and a response to it.

Millard2025-12-14 20:08 UTC
Simply put, what is Ehrman's "agnosticism" now? How would he put it?
Claude2025-12-14 20:09 UTC

Ehrman says he's both agnostic and atheist. He distinguishes them: agnosticism is about knowledge (he doesn't know if God exists), atheism is about belief (he doesn't believe God exists).

In his words: "Atheism is a statement about faith and agnosticism is a statement about epistemology."
