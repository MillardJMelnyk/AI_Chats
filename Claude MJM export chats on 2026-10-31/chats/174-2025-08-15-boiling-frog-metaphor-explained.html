What's the metaphor about them raising the heat but you don't notice until you're cooked
Claude2025-08-15 11:29 UTC

You're thinking of the "boiling frog" metaphor. The idea is that if you put a frog directly into boiling water, it will immediately jump out to escape. But if you put the frog in cool water and gradually increase the temperature, it won't notice the slow change and will eventually be cooked.

This metaphor is commonly used to describe situations where gradual, incremental changes lead to a seriously harmful outcome that people don't recognize because each individual step seems minor or acceptable. Examples include:

  • How freedoms can be eroded through small, seemingly reasonable restrictions over time
  • How toxic relationships can worsen so gradually that the victim doesn't realize how bad things have become
  • How environmental problems or economic issues can build up slowly until they reach a crisis point
  • How organizations can drift away from their original values through small compromises

The metaphor emphasizes our tendency to adapt to gradual changes while we'd resist the same endpoint if it happened suddenly. It's worth noting that the biological premise isn't actually accurate - real frogs will jump out of gradually heated water too - but the metaphor remains powerful for describing this very human psychological tendency.
