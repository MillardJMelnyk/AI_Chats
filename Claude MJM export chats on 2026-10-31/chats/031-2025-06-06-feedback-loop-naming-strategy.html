Looking at the diagram, I can see two feedback loops at the bottom levels:

Orange feedback loop (between subconsciousness and physical sensory systems):

  • Sensorimotor Integration Loop or Perceptual Processing Loop

This loop represents the continuous cycle where sensory systems gather raw data from the environment, the subconscious processes and filters this information, and then influences how the sensory systems attend to and interpret subsequent inputs.

Green feedback loop (real-world interaction):

  • Environmental Interaction Loop or Embodied Action-Perception Loop

This loop captures how our physical body interacts with the external world - our actions change the environment, which in turn provides new sensory information that feeds back into our perceptual systems, creating a continuous cycle of action and perception that grounds our embodied experience in physical reality.

These names reflect the functional roles each loop plays in the embodied cognition framework shown in your diagram.

Are any of those terms recognized in relevant fields like cognitive science, epistemology, neuroscience, etc?
Claude2025-06-06 15:28 UTC

Yes, several of these terms are well-established in the relevant fields:

Orange Loop - Recognized Terms:

  • "Sensorimotor integration" is a standard term in neuroscience referring to "the complex process in the central nervous system where sensory information from multiple sources is selectively and rapidly integrated to produce task-specific motor output"
  • "Sensorimotor loop" appears frequently in neuroscience literature, particularly in motor control research
  • There's even a specific model called "SMILE" (Sensory-Motor Integrative Loop for Enacting) used in dystonia research

Green Loop - Recognized Terms:

  • "Perception-action loop" and "action-perception loops" are established terms in embodied cognition and ecological psychology
  • "Perception-action relationship" is fundamental to enactivism, where "organisms obtain knowledge or develop their cognitive capacities through a perception–action relationship with a mutually determining environment"
  • "Embodied action" is a core concept in enactivist approaches

Most Appropriate Academic Terms:

  • Orange loop: "Sensorimotor Integration Loop" or simply "Sensorimotor Loop"
  • Green loop: "Perception-Action Loop" or "Action-Perception Loop"

These terms are widely recognized across cognitive science, neuroscience, embodied cognition theory, and enactivism - making them the most academically appropriate choices for your diagram.

So, Sensorimotor Integration deals with the interface between subconscious perception assembly/management of sensorymotor signals?
Claude2025-06-06 15:31 UTC

Yes, exactly! You've captured it well. Sensorimotor integration specifically refers to that interface where:

On the sensory side: Raw sensory signals are filtered, translated, and assembled into coherent perceptual information at the subconscious level

On the motor side: Motor commands and responses are coordinated and refined based on that processed sensory information

The "integration" happens at that interface between the physical sensory systems and subconsciousness, where:

  • Multiple sensory inputs are combined and interpreted
  • Sensory information is matched with motor planning
  • Feedback from motor actions refines sensory processing
  • The brain creates coherent representations from disparate signals

This process is largely subconscious - we don't consciously experience the complex neural computations that integrate visual, proprioceptive, vestibular, and other sensory streams, or that coordinate the precise muscle activations needed for smooth movement.

So in your diagram, the orange feedback loop represents this continuous cycle where sensorimotor integration processes incoming sensory data, influences motor responses, and then uses the sensory consequences of those motor actions to further refine the integration process. It's the fundamental loop that allows embodied beings to navigate and interact with their physical environment before that information ever reaches conscious awareness.

  • Green loop: Physical Interaction (body ↔ environment)

It's appropriately basic and doesn't presuppose any cognitive apparatus, just the fundamental fact of embodied beings existing in and exchanging with their physical environment.
