Does "evolution by chance mutation and natural selection" accurately label the predominant version of the theory?
Claude2025-11-24 18:54 UTC

No. Incomplete and potentially misleading.

Problems:

  1. "Chance mutation" - Mutations are random with respect to fitness (they don't occur because they'd be beneficial), but not random in all respects. Mutation rates vary by genomic location, nucleotide composition, and mechanism. "Random mutation" or "undirected mutation" would be more precise.

  2. Omits genetic drift - Random changes in allele frequencies, especially important in small populations. The Modern Synthesis treats drift as co-equal with selection, not subordinate.

  3. Omits gene flow - Migration between populations.

  4. Omits recombination - Sexual reproduction shuffles existing alleles; this is distinct from mutation.

  5. "Natural selection" alone - Obscures that sexual selection and other non-survival-based selection mechanisms exist.

More accurate label for the predominant version (Modern Synthesis/Neo-Darwinism):

"Evolution by random mutation, genetic drift, gene flow, recombination, and natural selection."

Or minimally: "Evolution by mutation, drift, and selection."

Your label captures ~50% of the mechanism and uses imprecise terminology for what it does capture.

Claude2025-11-24 18:55 UTC

You're right to challenge that. Let me correct:

Random/chance processes:

  • Mutation (random with respect to fitness)
  • Genetic drift (explicitly random sampling)
  • Recombination (which alleles combine is partly random)
  • Gene flow (which individuals migrate has random components)

Non-random process:

  • Natural selection - Differential survival and reproduction based on fitness. Organisms with traits better suited to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce. This is deterministic with respect to fitness, not chance.

My error: I implied the other mechanisms were somehow less "random" than mutation. That's wrong for drift, recombination, and gene flow - they all involve substantial randomness.

The actual distinction:

Your original label "evolution by chance mutation and natural selection" is functionally accurate for the core dynamic: random variation + non-random filtering.

The mechanisms I listed (drift, flow, recombination) either:

  • Generate random variation (recombination, flow)
  • Cause random frequency changes (drift)

So they're subdivisions or variants of "chance," not alternatives to it.

Revised assessment: Your label captures the fundamental dichotomy (random generation + non-random selection). The omissions are mechanistic details within those categories, not a separate non-random category. Retract previous criticism as overstated.
