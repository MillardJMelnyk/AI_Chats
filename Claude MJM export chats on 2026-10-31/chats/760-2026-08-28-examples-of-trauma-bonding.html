Well-known examples of trauma bonding (cyclical abuse + intermittent reinforcement producing attachment to the abuser):

  1. Domestic violence cycles — Lenore Walker's "cycle of violence" model (tension-building → acute battering → reconciliation/honeymoon) is the canonical clinical reference.
  2. Stockholm syndrome hostage cases — Patty Hearst (SLA, 1974); the 1973 Norrmalmstorg bank robbery (origin of the term).
  3. Cult leader–follower dynamics — Jonestown (Jim Jones), NXIVM (Keith Raniere), Children of God — alternating punishment/love-bombing to bind members.
  4. Human trafficking / pimp-controlled prostitution — "trauma bond" or "Romeo pimp" model: coercive control interspersed with affection.
  5. Child abuse attachment — abused children forming strong attachment to abusive parents (documented in attachment literature, e.g., Bowlby-derived studies on disorganized attachment).
  6. Celebrity/public relationship cases cited in pop psychology — Ike and Tina Turner's relationship is frequently invoked as a trauma-bonding example in clinical and media literature.

Note: "trauma bonding" is a popular-psychology term, not a DSM/ICD diagnostic category — usage varies across sources.

what is it that actually kills severe diabetes sufferers?
Claude2026-08-28 22:07 UTC

Leading causes of death in severe/long-term diabetes (both types), by mechanism:

  1. Cardiovascular disease — the dominant killer. Diabetics have ~2-4x the risk of MI and stroke vs non-diabetics; CVD accounts for roughly 50% of diabetes-related deaths (WHO/ADA figures, varies by cohort and year — check current WHO diabetes fact sheet for latest %).
  2. Diabetic nephropathy → end-stage renal failure — chronic hyperglycemia damages glomeruli; progresses to dialysis-dependence or death without transplant.
  3. Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — acute, mostly Type 1: insulin deficiency → ketone accumulation → metabolic acidosis, cerebral edema, death if untreated. Can also occur in Type 2 under severe stress.
  4. Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) — acute, mostly Type 2 elderly patients: extreme hyperglycemia → severe dehydration → coma, vascular collapse. Higher mortality rate than DKA (up to ~15-20% in some cohorts vs ~1-5% for DKA — figures vary by study/year).
  5. Severe hypoglycemia — from insulin/sulfonylurea overtreatment: arrhythmia, seizure, death, particularly in elderly or those with hypoglycemia unawareness.
  6. Infection/sepsis via diabetic foot ulcers — peripheral neuropathy + peripheral arterial disease → unnoticed wounds → gangrene → amputation → sepsis.
  7. Silent MI — autonomic neuropathy can mask cardiac ischemia symptoms, delaying treatment and increasing mortality.

Net: chronic vascular damage (macro- and microvascular) is the underlying mechanism behind most deaths; acute metabolic crises (DKA, HHS, hypoglycemia) are the direct killers in a smaller but significant subset.

"Diabetic complications" is the standard umbrella term.

If you want it split: acute diabetic complications (DKA, HHS, severe hypoglycemia) vs. chronic diabetic complications — the latter further divided into microvascular (nephropathy, retinopathy, neuropathy) and macrovascular (CVD, stroke, peripheral arterial disease).
