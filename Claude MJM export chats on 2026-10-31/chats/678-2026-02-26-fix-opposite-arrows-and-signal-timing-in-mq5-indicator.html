Your instinct on option 3 (no reversions, first cross commits for the bar) is the right one. Here's why:

The cocking mechanism already does the heavy lifting. It requires a structural setup: extreme → retrace past reference open. That's the conviction filter. The trigger cross is confirmation of that conviction. Once both conditions are met, the structural thesis is established. A brief retrace back through the trigger line doesn't invalidate the structure — it's just tick noise around a moving level.

Option 1 (threshold beyond the trigger) fails for exactly the reason you identified — it's turtles all the way down. You'd need a threshold for the threshold.

Option 2 (no reversions, first cross locks the signal for the bar) is clean because:

  • The cocking prerequisite prevents spurious first-crosses. You can't accidentally trigger on random noise because the cock hasn't been set.
  • The EA's natural execution boundary becomes bar close or next bar open, which is when MT5 gives you reliable price data anyway (no requotes on a closed bar).
  • Blackout applies normally from that committed bar forward.
  • It eliminates the entire provisional/snapshot/revert machinery, which is the source of at least three of the bugs we've been tracing.

The one scenario to consider: price briefly pierces the trigger on a wick but closes back on the wrong side. Under "first cross commits," you're in. But given the cock prerequisite, this is a legitimate signal that happened to retrace within the bar — which is normal price action. The trade is still structurally justified. Waiting for the next bar's execution price means you're not chasing the wick anyway.

The provisional signal machinery then becomes dead code that can be stripped entirely — along with the snapshot state, RevertProvisionalSignal(), and the uncock block. That eliminates bugs #2 and #5 by removing the mechanism rather than patching it.
