Codependence and Humanness: A Self-Test

Codependence and humanness differ in one thing: who you rely on to enable you. The litmus question is: who do I depend on to enable me?

Codependence is a posture of can't-unless-and-until. Your ability and capacity to act, feel, self-stabilize, or self-initiate are made to depend critically on circumstances, events, or other beings. Whatever those sources supply is all you allow yourself. You treat this dependence as the way to success, and you see no failure in it. It is a self-staged, self-operated simulation, and so inauthentic by definition. Its root is suggestibility induced by earlier trauma, so in the moment it is not an explicit choice.

Humanness is a posture of can-and-will-even-though. You enable yourself whether circumstances, events, or other beings cooperate or oppose you. Illness, exhaustion, or injury may cut your capacity, but you still treat yourself as the enabler: you act within what remains, adjust, and ask for help without surrendering your agency. You are a free, mature adult in possession of your humanness.

Three things to check in yourself:

  1. Source. When I act, or fail to, am I waiting on something or someone to enable me, or am I enabling myself?
  2. Valuation. When I depend critically on others, do I count it as a failure, or as the way to succeed? A fully human person who lapses or is coerced into dependence counts it as failure. A codependent counts it as the way.
  3. Location of power. Do I regard myself as a failure who cannot succeed without an outside power? Or is the power integral to my own being, so that relying on it is relying on myself? Submission to an outside power as a precondition of my success is codependence. Power that is organically mine, such as God's power in a believer who embodies it as a member of Christ's body, is not.

It comes and goes. Codependence operates in some situations and not others, the way PTSD episodes are triggered by certain events. A person can be competent and self-reliant in one setting and revert to childlike dependence in another, like a business owner who is commanding at work and submissive at mass. So the test classifies moments, not whole people, and it applies to each area of your life separately.

Opting out. Because the posture was imposed by prior trauma, nothing in the moment will end it for you. Choosing humanness takes an explicit, conscious act each time.
