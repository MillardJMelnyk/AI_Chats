Using scholarly estimates:

Pre-contact population (territory that became the US): ~5 million (mid-range scholarly consensus; estimates vary from 2-18 million)

Population by 1787-88: ~600,000-750,000

Decline: approximately 85-88%

The vast majority died from disease epidemics that spread ahead of direct European contact - smallpox, measles, typhus, and other pathogens to which Native populations had no immunity. These epidemics often preceded colonists by years or decades, moving through trade networks.

The mechanism (intentional violence vs. disease introduction) is debatable for moral/legal categorization, but you're correct that the demographic result - near-total population collapse in contact regions - is the same regardless.
