BoBo Balance (Primary Competitor)

Strengths:

  • Created by two physiotherapists in 2015 specifically for rehabilitation
  • Used in over 10,000 physical therapy clinics worldwide
  • 400 hours battery life (vs GoBalance's 200 hours)
  • Progress tracking with scoreboards and performance records
  • Testimonials from users with EDS, ataxia, and other conditions using it for PT

Weaknesses:

  • App does not support multiple user profiles
  • Reviewer noted "limited app features" and frustration with inability to track/compare performance over time
  • Price: 2.3x–3.5x more expensive than GoBalance products

Stealth Core Trainer (Different Use Case)

Key Difference: Designed exclusively for planking, not standing balance exercises

Strengths:

  • Reviewer reported increased core strength after 4 weeks, improved balance, lower back, arms, shoulders, glutes
  • Works 29 different muscles according to company
  • 60-day free trial available
  • Featured in Men's Health

Weaknesses:

  • User complaint: "only 2 free games and the rest are $25 per year"
  • Reviewer called it "a one-trick pony" and "absurdly expensive for what it is"
  • Only ~7 of 20+ premium games rated as engaging by one reviewer
  • Ongoing subscription cost for full game access

Summary Verdict

BoBo Balance is purpose-built for balance rehabilitation and training by physiotherapists, has documented clinical adoption, requires no subscription, but has an app that users report is underdeveloped for tracking progress.

Stealth Core Trainer is purpose-built for plank-based core workouts by a fitness entrepreneur, has no clinical validation, requires a subscription for full game access, but users consistently report it makes planking more engaging and extends workout duration.

They are not substitutes for each other. BoBo trains standing balance; Stealth trains prone core strength. Buying one expecting the other's benefits would be a category error.

Recommendation

For children, BoBo is the better fit. Reasons:

  1. Designed with children in mind — explicit age range starting at 4, documented pediatric PT use
  2. Lower supervision burden — standing exercise has lower form-related injury risk than planking
  3. Developmentally appropriate — balance and coordination training aligns with childhood motor development needs
  4. No subscription — full game access included
  5. Documented child engagement — multiple parent reports of kids enjoying the games

Stealth is not recommended for children unless:

  • The child is athletic/mature enough to maintain proper plank form
  • An adult can actively monitor form during every session
  • The goal is specifically core strengthening (not balance)
