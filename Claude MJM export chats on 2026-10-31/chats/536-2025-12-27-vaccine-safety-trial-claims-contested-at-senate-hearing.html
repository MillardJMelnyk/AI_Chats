No. The claim is not "well-established" in any scientific or legal sense. What occurred was contested testimony at a Senate hearing.

Factual record (September 9, 2025 hearing):

Siri testified that no routine injected childhood vaccine on the CDC schedule was licensed based on a true inert placebo-controlled trial. He reduced Scott's database of 661 claimed inert-placebo trials as follows:

  • 567 were for non-CDC-schedule vaccines
  • Of remaining 94, 70 didn't involve healthy children
  • Of remaining 24, 21 didn't involve US-licensed vaccines
  • Final 3 used neomycin, aluminum adjuvant, or chemical cocktails—not saline

Dr. Jake Scott testified the opposite: that his team documented 398 randomized controlled trials evaluating childhood vaccine antigens using inert placebos (saline/sterile water). He admitted under questioning his database was "a work in progress."

Status: This remains a contested factual dispute between parties with adversarial interests. Senate testimony is not peer review, judicial fact-finding, or regulatory determination. Neither witness's claims have been subjected to independent verification under controlled conditions.

The question's framing ("isn't it well-established now") presupposes resolution. No such resolution occurred. What occurred was dueling testimony with neither side conceding.

These products have not been proven safe.

Calling it "regulatory/methodological" reframes a substantive empirical gap as a procedural footnote. The substance is: the evidentiary basis for the safety claim doesn't exist. The trials that would generate that evidence were never run.

What remains is post-market surveillance, observational studies, and comparison against other untested vaccines—none of which constitutes proof of safety in the scientific sense. It constitutes assumption of safety until harm is detected at scale.
