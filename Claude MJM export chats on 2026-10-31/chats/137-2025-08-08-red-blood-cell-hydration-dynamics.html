RBCs stands for "red blood cells" (also called erythrocytes). These are the most abundant cells in your blood, and their primary function is to transport oxygen from your lungs to tissues throughout your body and carry carbon dioxide back to the lungs.

However, I need to address some significant inaccuracies in the claims you've presented:

About RBC function and oxygenation:

  • RBCs transport oxygen by binding it to hemoglobin, an iron-containing protein inside the cells
  • Pulse oximetry actually measures oxygen saturation by detecting the color difference between oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin using light wavelengths
  • The bright red vs. dark red appearance of blood is due to oxygenated vs. deoxygenated hemoglobin, not hydration status

About saline and IV fluids:

  • IV saline helps maintain blood volume and electrolyte balance, but RBCs don't "rehydrate" in the alveolar capillaries with salt water
  • Gas exchange in the lungs involves oxygen and carbon dioxide crossing the alveolar membrane, not water absorption by RBCs

About RBC appearance:

  • RBC color changes (bright vs. dark red) are primarily due to oxygen
