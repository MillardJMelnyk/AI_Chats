Claude2025-12-13 18:51 UTC

He's asking a fair question and deserves a direct answer. The weakness in his framing is this: Who decides whether a breach occurred?

His description assumes the breach is legitimate and undisputed. But the abuse potential is precisely in disputed cases:

  • Carlos or FPJ claims a breach (late payment, unauthorized guest, whatever)
  • You dispute it
  • Under normal legal process, a court decides who's right
  • Under this clause, there's no neutral arbiter—the pagaré gets executed and the lawyer enters, based solely on their determination

The specific abuse scenario: a landlord (or their póliza jurídica) fabricates or exaggerates a breach, and the tenant has already signed away their right to have a judge determine if the breach is real.

How I'd respond:

  1. Answer his direct question honestly and specifically—tell him what I just told you.

  2. Clarify the pagaré confusion with Claudia. Were you OK with pagarés for rent (normal) but not for the inflated furniture/damages amount? Or has your position changed? He deserves to know what happened there.

  3. Acknowledge his point that landlords also face risks—he's right, and you're not dismissing that. You're asking for a contract where both parties rely on legal process for disputes, not one where you've pre-signed your eviction.

  4. Keep the door open—he got outside legal opinions, he's engaging seriously, this is someone trying to work with you.

Want me to draft something?
