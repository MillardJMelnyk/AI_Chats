Bitcoin's response to market crashes

if there was a severe crash in the markets, how would bitcoin be affected?
Claude2025-11-22 18:13 UTC

Historical evidence (severe crashes):

March 2020 (COVID crash): Bitcoin fell ~50% in 48 hours (from ~$9,000 to ~$4,500), correlating strongly with equities during the liquidity crisis. It recovered faster than equities but crashed with them initially.

2022 (Fed tightening + crypto contagion): Bitcoin fell ~65% alongside risk assets. Correlation with NASDAQ reached 0.6–0.8 during peak stress.

Mechanism driving this:

  1. Liquidity demand — In severe crashes, margin calls and redemptions force selling of liquid assets. Bitcoin is highly liquid 24/7, making it an early liquidation target.

  2. Risk-off behavior — Institutional holders treat Bitcoin as a risk asset, not a hedge. Deleveraging hits it hard.

  3. No yield, no floor — Unlike bonds or dividend stocks, Bitcoin has no cash flow to establish a valuation floor during panic.

Counterpoint (longer horizon):

Post-crash, Bitcoin has historically outperformed in recovery phases due to monetary policy response (rate cuts, QE). The "digital gold" thesis has weak support during acute stress but some support over multi-year cycles.

Summary: In a severe, sudden crash, expect Bitcoin to fall at least as much as equities, potentially more, due to leverage in crypto markets and 24/7 liquidity access. Decorrelation from traditional assets has not materialized during actual crises.
