Simple, 1-on-1 friendly:

Predict your partner's week — Student guesses five things about your next seven days using might / probably will / definitely won't. You confirm or correct. Then swap.

Weekend plans, three levels — Student names one thing they'll definitely do, one they'll probably do, one they might do. Follow up with "why only might?"

What'll happen in 10 years? — Cars, phones, their city, their job. Push for the adverb: possibly, probably, definitely.

The suitcase — They're packing for a trip somewhere you choose (beach, mountains, London in November). Each item justified: "I might need an umbrella."

Fortune teller — You play the fortune teller, they ask may/might questions about their future. Then reverse roles.

Agree or disagree — You say a prediction ("Mexico will probably win the next World Cup"), they respond with their own probability level and a reason.

Empty fridge — Look in an imaginary fridge together. "I might have some eggs... I'll probably order food." Good for the present-possibility use in the left column.

Photo reactions — Show a picture of someone mid-action. "What might happen next?" Three guesses, ranked by likelihood.
