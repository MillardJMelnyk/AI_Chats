How the needle detector works, step by step:

Setup. For every tick, I use the mid-price — the average of the bid and the ask — so the two-line chart becomes one price line to work with.

Step 1 — Draw a local "normal range" around each tick. For the tick being examined, I look at the 10 ticks immediately before it and the 10 ticks immediately after it — 20 neighbors, the tick itself excluded. The highest and lowest of those 20 define a band: the price territory that's normal for that little stretch of time. This band is the local jitter envelope — the $0.30–0.50 waffle you pointed at. Its size isn't fixed; it breathes with the market, wider in active stretches, tighter in calm ones.

Step 2 — Ask whether the tick pokes out of its band. If the tick's price is above the top of the band, it's poking up. Below the bottom, poking down. Inside the band, it's ordinary jitter and I move on. Just poking out isn't enough on its own — the next two checks decide if it's a real needle.

Step 3 — Measure how far it pokes, and reject small pokes. I measure the gap between the tick and the edge of the band. A needle has to clear that edge by at least $0.15. Less than that, it's just a tick brushing the boundary of normal noise, not a true stab — discarded. The $0.15 came from the data: most pokes are tiny, but there's a clear shoulder in the distribution where genuine stabs begin, and $0.15 sits at that shoulder.

Step 4 — Measure how wide the poke is, and reject wide ones. I count how many ticks in a row stay outside the band. A needle is narrow — one or two ticks. If price stays outside for three, five, ten ticks in a row, that's not a stab, it's the start of a real move or a trend leg — discarded. This is the check that separates a needle from a breakout.

Step 5 — Confirm it snaps back. After the poke, I check whether price returns inside the band within 8 ticks. If it does, the move was temporary — the defining trait of a needle: out and back. If price never returns, it didn't snap back; it went somewhere and stayed. That's a breakout, not a needle — discarded.

Step 6 — Label and record. Anything that survives all four checks is a needle. Poking up = up-needle, poking down = down-needle. I record its time, price, how far it poked, its width, and how fast it snapped back.

The core idea. A needle is defined entirely by contrast with its own neighborhood, not by any absolute size. It must (a) stick out of the local band, (b) stick out by a meaningful amount, (c) be narrow, and (d) come right back.
