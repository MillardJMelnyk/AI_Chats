Invocation context — how every exit gets called

A signal fires and is recorded with an entry bar and entry price. The next time a new signal arrives, build_trades treats the bars between the two signals as the window available for an early exit. It calls _choose_earliest_exit, passing the entry bar, entry price, direction, and the window that runs from the bar immediately after entry up to (but not including) the new signal's bar. Every one of the 15 exit functions is called against that same window. Each one independently looks for its own trigger condition and returns either the earliest bar on which it would exit, or nothing. After all 15 have been evaluated, whichever returned the earliest bar wins. If none triggered, the trade closes at the new signal's bar and price instead. At end-of-file, if no new signal ever arrived, the same arbitration runs over the remaining bars, and if still nothing triggers, the trade closes at the final bar's close.

That is the full surrounding machinery. The 15 functions themselves are described below.

1. Early Stop

A fixed-distance stop loss measured from entry. A stop price is placed a configurable dollar amount behind the entry. The function scans forward bar by bar looking for any bar whose range touches or crosses that stop level. Each touch increments a hit counter; a configurable number of initial touches can be skipped before the stop is considered actionable. On an actionable hit, up to three additional confirmation requirements can be applied individually or in combination: the bar must close beyond the stop level, the bar must still show a loss relative to entry, or the MA relationship must no longer favor the trade direction. The first bar that touches the stop and satisfies all active requirements triggers the exit at the stop price.

2. Immediate T2E Revalidation

Targets the case where the signal fired on a bar where the fast MA was already on the wrong side of the slow MA — meaning the momentum relationship did not actually confirm the trade direction at entry. The function checks whether the MA relationship at the entry bar itself was unfavorable for the trade. If it was not unfavorable at entry, the exit passes. If it was unfavorable at entry, the function then checks the MA relationship at a fixed number of bars after entry. If momentum has still not turned in favor of the trade by that target bar, the trade closes at that bar's close.

3. Two-Bar Adverse Continuation

Looks for a sustained adverse close sequence of sufficient magnitude over a fixed bar window. Every bar in the window must close at a loss relative to entry — a single profitable close anywhere in the window disqualifies the exit. Additionally, the loss at the final bar of the window must exceed a minimum dollar threshold. Both conditions must hold simultaneously: unbroken adverse closes throughout the entire window, and the final close must be deeply enough in the red. If both are satisfied, the trade closes at that final bar's close.

4. Three-Bar Dead Start

Looks for a trade that never even momentarily moved in the favorable direction. Every bar in the window must close at a loss — any profitable close at all disqualifies the exit. Unlike the Two-Bar Adverse Continuation, there is no minimum loss magnitude requirement; the requirement is simply that the trade produced zero favorable closes across the entire window, and that the final bar's close is still a loss. If that pattern holds, the trade closes at the final bar's close.

5. Three-Bar Heat Cap

A worst-case intrabar adverse excursion stop. The function places a stop a fixed dollar amount behind entry, exactly as the Early Stop does, but applies it only within a short post-entry window. Instead of waiting for a close beyond the stop, it triggers the moment any bar's range reaches the stop level — measured as the maximum adverse intrabar move against entry on that bar. The exit fires at the stop price on the first bar within the window whose range extends far enough in the wrong direction.

6. KC Midline Rejection

Requires that every bar in a short post-entry window closes on the wrong side of the Keltner Channel midline — below it for longs, above it for shorts. If even one bar in the window closes on the correct side of the midline, the exit is disqualified. If all bars in the window consistently close against the trade on the midline, the trade closes at the final bar's close.

7. Five-Bar Still Red

A simple patience check: after a configurable number of bars have elapsed since entry, is the trade still losing? The function looks only at the final bar of the window and asks whether that bar's close is still below the entry price for longs, or above it for shorts. There is no requirement about the intermediate bars. If the trade has simply failed to reach breakeven by the target bar, the trade closes at that bar's close.

8. Adverse Close Dominance

Counts how many bars within a window closed at a loss relative to entry. A minimum number of adverse closes is required for the exit to trigger. If the adverse-close count reaches or exceeds that minimum within the window, the trade closes at the final bar's close. The window and the minimum count are both configurable, making this a vote-based exit: the majority of bars must have closed against the trade.

9. Early Bounce Failure

Targets trades that showed some favorable intrabar movement early on but never converted it into a favorable close. The function scans the window bar by bar, and the moment any bar closes profitably the exit is disqualified entirely. Simultaneously it tracks the maximum favorable intrabar excursion seen across all bars. If the window ends with no profitable close anywhere, but the trade did reach some minimum favorable intrabar distance at some point during the window, the trade closes at the final bar's close. The logic captures the pattern of a brief poke into profit that immediately reversed and never recovered.

10. Entry Impulse Rejection

Examines only the first two bars after entry, focused on what happened on bar one. It measures how far bar one moved in the favorable direction and how far it moved in the adverse direction on an intrabar basis. If the adverse intrabar move on bar one is large enough relative to the favorable intrabar move — specifically, adverse must equal or exceed a configurable multiple of favorable — the entry impulse is considered rejected. If that rejection condition holds and bar two's close is still a loss, the trade exits at bar two's close.

11. Wrong-Side Structure

Requires that every bar in a short window simultaneously closes on the wrong side of two independent reference lines: the Keltner Channel midline and the fast EMA of the HLCC4 price. Both conditions must be true on every bar in the window; a single bar where either reference is on the correct side disqualifies the exit. If the price structure has consistently remained below both references for longs, or above both for shorts, throughout the entire window, the trade closes at the final bar's close.

12. Weakness Confirmed Adverse

A condition-stacked stop that requires three things to be true simultaneously on the same bar before triggering. The function scans bar by bar looking for any bar whose range touches a stop level placed a fixed dollar amount behind entry. For each such bar, it then checks whether that bar's close is also at a loss relative to entry, and whether the MA relationship on that bar no longer favors the trade direction. All three — range touch, adverse close, and adverse momentum — must coincide on the same bar. The first bar satisfying all three exits the trade at the stop price.

13. Time Failure

Looks backward over the entire period from the bar after entry through a fixed number of bars after entry and asks: did the trade ever reach a minimum favorable intrabar distance during that entire span? The function is not interested in closes or in any specific bar — it accumulates the maximum favorable intrabar excursion seen anywhere in the window. If that lifetime peak favorable move never reached the minimum threshold, the trade closes at the target bar's close. This identifies trades that simply stalled without ever showing meaningful momentum.

14. Adverse Recovery Requirement

A two-phase test that monitors what happens after the trade suffers a significant adverse move. Phase one watches for the trade's intrabar adverse excursion to first reach a configurable threshold. Once that threshold is breached, phase two begins: the trade is given a fixed number of bars to show a meaningful recovery. Recovery is defined as price reaching a level that is a configurable fraction of the way back from the stop toward entry. If the recovery level is touched within the bar window, the exit is cancelled. If the bar limit expires without the recovery level being touched, the trade closes at that bar's close. Trades that took a serious hit and then went quiet — neither recovering nor hitting a stop — are closed out.

15. Spike vs. Cocked Separate Management

Applies different time-and-minimum-profit standards depending on whether the entry signal was generated by a spike reversal or by a conventional cocked setup. The logic is the same as the Time Failure exit: look over a fixed post-entry window and check whether the maximum favorable intrabar move ever reached a minimum threshold. The difference is that spike entries are given a shorter window and a lower profit minimum, while cocked entries are given a longer window and a higher profit minimum. If the applicable minimum favorable move was never achieved within the applicable window, the trade closes at the target bar's close.
