A few distinctions matter:

Your description of the 1997 Bess and Gluschankof papers is substantially correct. Both found that density-gradient “purified HIV” preparations contained large amounts of cellular vesicles with overlapping size, density, and cellular components. Gluschankof explicitly reported an excess of 50–500-nm cellular vesicles relative to the ~100-nm particles classified as HIV.

Therefore, density ≈1.16 g/ml plus particle morphology is not sufficient by itself to identify a particle as HIV. Subsequent methods use combinations of viral RNA sequence, p24/gp120, antibodies, infectivity, protease sensitivity, immunocapture, etc., rather than morphology alone.

HIV-associated material has nevertheless been obtained directly from patient plasma without first generating it by transfection. For example, infectious virus was recovered from cell-free patient plasma in clinical studies; another study physically purified plasma material from eight patients and immunocaptured particles bearing HIV-associated and host-envelope markers.

Modern work has sequenced near-full-length ~8.3-kb HIV RNA genomes directly from plasma-derived particles of untreated patients. On causation, there has never been an intentional experiment infecting healthy humans with purified HIV and waiting to see whether they develop AIDS. That experiment would be unethical.

The strongest human causal evidence instead comes from natural transmission experiments. For example, blood from a donor who was HIV-negative on screening during the window period infected a recipient; donor and recipient HIV sequences were >99% identical. Other transfusion investigations have independently established donor-recipient transmission by phylogenetic sequencing. Longitudinal transfusion cohorts then documented recipients progressing to AIDS.
