CLAUDE2026-05-18 21:01 UTC

The defect underneath your description: the test triggerSlopeSignNow == -g_trade.direction is a level test on current state, but you need an edge test — "did something new happen?" Both reference-point switches (momentum sign flips, new MA selected, that MA happens to already be sloping the other way) and the trade-direction flip itself can make an unchanged slope condition look freshly eligible. That's the mechanical bug.

But your stated goal cuts deeper than the mechanical fix. You want reversal justified by adverse market action, not by mechanical state shuffling. A clean slope flip while the trade is winning 50 points isn't worth a reversal; a 30-point adverse run on a quiet slope is. That reframes the slope-vs-trade test as potentially the wrong test, not just a misimplemented one.

Solutions, ranked by how directly they address your stated goal of avoiding significant losing movements:

A. Adverse-excursion-driven reversal. Drop slope-against-trade as the trigger. Reverse when the open trade's adverse excursion (from entry, or from MFE) exceeds a threshold X. Slope serves only as a direction-confirming gate — we don't reverse into a slope that disagrees with the reversal direction. This is the direct expression of "reverse to avoid significant losing movement." Threshold metric is the open question: fixed points, ATR multiple, or a percentile of historical excursion.

B. Entry-anchored trigger MA. Separate two roles the trigger MA is currently playing: (i) "which MA originates a new trade" and (ii) "which MA's slope governs the current trade's reversal." Lock the originating MA at entry. Momentum can still re-elect the trigger MA for future first-trade purposes, but reversal evaluation uses the entry-anchored MA's slope. This kills the reference-point class of false reversals cleanly. Doesn't address the P/L question.

C. Edge-triggered slope crossing. Latch slope sign at entry; only fire reversal when slope sign actually crosses from with-trade to against-trade after entry. On any trigger MA change, the latch resets and at least one full slope period must elapse before reversal becomes eligible again. Minimal mechanical fix. Doesn't address the P/L question either, but cheap and additive.

D. Hybrid A+C. Reversal requires both adverse excursion above threshold and a fresh slope event in the reversal direction. Most conservative; double-confirmation. Risk: missed reversals on fast moves where slope hasn't flipped yet.

Three decisions before any of these gets built:

  1. Substantive or mechanical: is the goal P/L-justified reversal (A or D), or is killing the reference-point spurious-fire alone enough (B or C)?
  2. If A or D: what defines "significant" — fixed points, ATR multiple, or excursion percentile?
  3. Is the trigger MA locked at trade entry (B's stance) or does it flow with momentum throughout the trade (current behavior, modulo whatever fix)?

CLAUDE2026-05-18 21:39 UTC

Restated in trader terms:

Right now, when the bar closes and the trigger MA fires a trade, the indicator immediately flips its internal trade direction. The slope-against-trade logic then runs again on the same bar and sees the freshly-flipped direction as evidence that we should reverse — even though no new price action happened. That's the bug.

Your fix: once any trade fires on a bar, no more trigger-MA entries on that bar. Period. The only thing that can fire as a second trade on the same bar is a stop-loss-style reversal — meaning the open trade is hurting badly enough that we want out and going the other way.

So two separate jobs:
- Trigger MA opens and reverses trades the way it does now. One trigger-MA fire per bar maximum.
- Stop-loss reversal is a different mechanism that can fire as a second trade on the bar, only when the current trade is bleeding.

Three things to decide, and my picks:

1. What defines "bleeding enough to bail."
Use adverse excursion measured in ATR multiples, evaluated on the open trade. Fixed points fail on gold because volatility varies; percentile of history needs a calibration pass; ATR adapts automatically and gold traders already think in ATRs. Specifically: trigger when current price is N×ATR(14) against entry. Start with N somewhere between 0.75 and 1.5, walk it with a simulator. ATR computed bar-close, so it's stable within the bar.

2. When to evaluate the stop-loss path.
Bar close only, same as trigger-MA evaluation. Tick-level evaluation introduces a different signal cadence, makes the simulator's job harder, and invites slippage modeling questions you don't want. Bar-close keeps the entire signal model on one clock. The cost is up to one bar of extra adverse drift before reversal — acceptable, because you're already eating that on every trigger-MA reversal.

3. Can stop-loss fire alone on a bar, or only as second-fire?
It can fire alone. Restricting it to second-fire-only means a slow grinding loss escapes review until the trigger-MA happens to fire something else on the bar — and the whole point of stop-loss reversal is that the trigger MA isn't reacting fast enough. Treat it as an always-on second check after trigger-MA evaluation completes: if no trigger-MA fire and the open trade is past the ATR threshold, fire the stop-loss reversal.

Two additional suggestions you didn't ask about but should know:

  • Anchor the ATR threshold at entry, not running. Compute the required adverse distance from entryPrice using ATR as of the entry bar. Otherwise ATR rising mid-trade makes the stop loosen on you exactly when volatility is hurting you most.
  • Block stop-loss reversal in the same direction as the just-closed trade. Already covered by g_lastSignalDir, but worth being explicit: a stop-loss reversal is a reversal — same-direction is incoherent.
