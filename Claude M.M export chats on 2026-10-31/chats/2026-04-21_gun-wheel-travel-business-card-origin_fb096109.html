Where did the have gun wheel travel business card originate

CLAUDE2026-04-21 03:57 UTC

The business card comes from the CBS Western TV series Have Gun – Will Travel, which aired from 1957 to 1963. The card belonged to the show's protagonist, Paladin, and read "Have Gun Will Travel / Wire Paladin / San Francisco," with an engraving of a white chess knight on it.

There's also a real-world origin claim with legal teeth: a rodeo performer named Victor De Costa had been dressing in black, calling himself "the Paladin," and handing out hundreds of business cards featuring a chess piece and the phrase "Have gun will travel" — before the TV show existed. He sued CBS, and in 1991 a federal jury awarded him $3.5 million from Viacom (CBS's distributor) for trademark infringement.

As for the card's meaning within the show, the name "Paladin" referred to a lawful knight of Charlemagne's court, which explains the chess knight on the card and the theme song's lyric calling him "a knight without armor in a savage land." The word "Wire" on the card is not a name — it's a verb meaning "send a telegram."
