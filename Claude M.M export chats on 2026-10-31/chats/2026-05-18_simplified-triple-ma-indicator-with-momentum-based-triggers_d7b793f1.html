CLAUDE2026-05-18 16:31 UTC

XAUUSD M1 chop, e.g., Asian session 03:00–05:00 UTC.

Three consecutive bars: small bull (+1.5 pip body), small bear (-2 pip), small bull (+1 pip). Momentum MA (period 7) absorbs them. With InpMomentumSlopePeriod = 2:

  • Bar i (after bull): momentum slope = +0.4 → trigger = MA b
  • Bar i+1 (after bear): slope = -0.6 → trigger flips to MA a
  • Bar i+2 (after bull): slope = +0.2 → trigger flips back to MA b

At bar i+1, the trigger just became MA a, but its slope-now and slope-then values are measured over MA a's own history — which has nothing to do with whether that history meant anything as a trigger. If MA a's slope happens to have a sign flip during those bars (likely, in chop), the pivot detector fires. False short. Then i+2 flips back, MA b's slope may also have flipped during chop, false long. Two losing trades in three bars.

With InpMomentumSlopePeriod = 6 and trigger slope period left at 2:

  • The 3-bar oscillation averages to ~+0.05 → stays inside sideways band → trigger preserved through the chop.
  • Real breakout when it comes: momentum builds past +0.20 cleanly, trigger assigned and stays.
  • Trigger slope (still period 2) detects MA b's actual pivot sharply when it arrives.

Asymmetry has a direction here. Longer momentum period buys stability of trigger assignment (fewer false reassignments → fewer false pivots on freshly-assigned MAs). Shorter trigger period preserves pivot responsiveness. The opposite asymmetry (short momentum, long trigger) is strictly worse — fast trigger reassignment with sluggish pivot detection.

Caveat: this is a hypothesis worth testing against your dlog, not a guarantee. The actual edge depends on how often trigger flips during chop fire signals on freshly-assigned MAs in your data. Could be 6 false trades per session, could be 1. Your tick dump will tell.
