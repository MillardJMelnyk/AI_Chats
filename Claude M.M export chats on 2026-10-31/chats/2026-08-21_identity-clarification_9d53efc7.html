Tell me briefly about Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla

CLAUDE2026-08-21 15:50 UTC

Catholic priest from Guanajuato, born 1753. On September 16, 1810, in the town of Dolores, he rang his church bell and called the people to rise against Spanish rule — the Grito de Dolores, still reenacted every year on the night of the 15th.

He raised an army of tens of thousands, mostly indigenous and mestizo farmers, and took Guanajuato and Guadalajara. His forces were huge but untrained; after a crushing defeat at the Battle of Calderón Bridge in January 1811 he fled north, was betrayed and captured, and was executed by firing squad in Chihuahua in July 1811. His head was hung in a cage on the Alhóndiga in Guanajuato for a decade.

Independence didn't come until 1821, but he's remembered as the father of the nation. The Querétaro connection is direct: the 1810 conspiracy was plotted here, and when it was discovered, La Corregidora's warning is what pushed Hidalgo to launch early.
