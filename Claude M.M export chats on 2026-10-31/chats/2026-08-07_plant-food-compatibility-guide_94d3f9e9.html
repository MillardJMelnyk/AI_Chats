what type of plants is this plant food ok to be used for?

CLAUDE2026-08-07 21:09 UTC

This is fertilizer for shade plants ("Plantas de Sombra") — indoor/low-light foliage plants. It's an inorganic soluble fertilizer, N-P-K of 5.80-7.56-0.35, enriched with iron to promote growth and healthier appearance.

MILLARD2026-08-07 21:09 UTC

is it ok for basil?

CLAUDE2026-08-07 21:09 UTC

Not ideal. This is formulated for ornamental shade/foliage plants, not edible herbs — and basil actually wants more light and a different nutrient balance than a shade-plant fertilizer provides. It's also not labeled as approved for use on edible crops, so I wouldn't use it on something you're going to eat unless the label explicitly says it's safe for food plants (yours doesn't appear to, based on what's visible). Better to use a fertilizer labeled for vegetables/herbs.

MILLARD2026-08-07 21:10 UTC

what about lavender?

CLAUDE2026-08-07 21:10 UTC

Not a great match either. Lavender is a full-sun, drought-tolerant, low-nutrient herb that actually does poorly with too much fertilizer (especially nitrogen) — it can push leafy growth at the expense of flowers/oils and make the plant leggy. A "shade plant" formula is the wrong profile on both counts: wrong light-plant type and too rich in nitrogen for lavender's needs. If you fertilize lavender at all, it should be sparingly, with something low-N (or none), not this.
