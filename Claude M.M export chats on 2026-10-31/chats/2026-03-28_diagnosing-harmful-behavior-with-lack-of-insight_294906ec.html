What's DSM-5 diagnosis covers someone who does heinous harm to other people but insists that others are the monsters?

CLAUDE2026-03-28 13:15 UTC

The diagnosis that best fits this description is Antisocial Personality Disorder (ASPD), often associated with psychopathy or narcissistic traits. But more precisely, the combination you're describing points to a few overlapping DSM-5 categories:

Antisocial Personality Disorder (301.7)
The core diagnosis for people who repeatedly harm others without remorse. Key features include disregard for others' rights, deceitfulness, impulsivity, and lack of guilt. Crucially, many with ASPD use externalization — they genuinely perceive themselves as justified or victimized.

Narcissistic Personality Disorder (301.81)
The "others are the monsters" inversion is a hallmark narcissistic defense. It involves projecting blame onto others, a grandiose sense of entitlement, and inability to tolerate being seen as the wrongdoer. Narcissistic injury often triggers this kind of moral reversal.

The key psychological mechanism: Projection
This is where someone unconsciously attributes their own unacceptable qualities (cruelty, malice) to others. Combined with DARVO — Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender — it explains exactly why an abuser insists they are the real victim.

Psychopathy (not a standalone DSM-5 diagnosis, but coded within ASPD)
Hare's psychopathy model adds shallow affect, charm, and deliberate manipulation — meaning the moral inversion may not be defensive self-deception but a calculated strategy.

In short: The DSM-5 diagnosis most squarely covering this pattern is ASPD, often comorbid with NPD. The "I'm the real victim" dynamic is so characteristic that it's sometimes called the abuser's script.
