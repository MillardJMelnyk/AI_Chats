Duration 9:27, -0:32 — you slept 9 hours 27 minutes, 32 minutes short of your set sleep goal.

Hypnogram — the timeline of those phases across the night, 8:57 PM to 6:27 AM. Bars low = deep, higher = light, highest = awake. You dropped into deep sleep early and clustered most of it before 3 AM, which is the normal pattern. The back half is mostly light sleep with brief surfacings.

Actigraph — raw movement detected by the phone or watch. The spikes are where you moved; that's the input the app uses to guess which phase you were in. Everything above is derived from this, so treat the phase percentages as estimates, not measurements.

Two things that would actually matter:

How you feel. One night's numbers say nothing. If you wake up tired despite 9.5 hours and 100% efficiency, that's a signal worth following — and it points at things this app can't see, like breathing interruptions. If you wake up fine, the numbers are irrelevant.

The 9:27 also deserves a question: is that your normal, or catch-up from a short night before? Regularly needing more than nine hours is worth noticing.

MILLARD2026-08-17 12:48 UTC

How can I reduce snoring?

CLAUDE2026-08-17 12:48 UTC

Things that actually work, roughly in order of payoff:

Side sleeping. Back sleeping lets the tongue and soft palate fall backward. Biggest single lever for most people. A body pillow or a tennis ball sewn into the back of a shirt keeps you off your back.

Weight, if it applies. Fat around the neck narrows the airway. Even modest loss can cut snoring noticeably.

Alcohol in the evening. Relaxes the throat muscles. Cutting it within 3–4 hours of bed makes a real difference.

Nasal congestion. If you're a mouth-breather from a blocked nose, treating that — saline rinse, allergy control, nasal strips — helps. Querétaro's dry air and dust can be a factor.

Head elevation. Raising the head of the bed a few inches, not just stacking pillows, which kinks the neck and can make it worse.

Sedatives before bed. Same mechanism as alcohol.

The thing worth ruling out: snoring plus daytime tiredness, morning headaches, or witnessed pauses in breathing points at sleep apnea, which none of the above fixes. Your 9.5 hours with 100% efficiency and still asking about sleep quality is the kind of pattern that makes it worth a look. Sleep as Android's snore recording will show you whether it's continuous rumbling or interrupted by silences — the silences are the tell.

MILLARD2026-08-17 12:49 UTC

Head elevation. Raising the head of the bed a few inches, not just stacking pillows, which kinks the neck and can make it worse.

Explain

CLAUDE2026-08-17 12:49 UTC

Two different geometries.

Stacking pillows raises your head but not your torso. Your chin drops toward your chest and the neck bends forward. That bend narrows the throat at exactly the point where snoring originates — so you can end up worse off than flat.

Raising the bed tilts your whole body as one unit. Head, neck, and chest stay in a straight line, but the whole line is on an incline. Gravity pulls the tongue and soft palate down and away from the back of the throat instead of backward into it. Airway stays open, no kink.

How to do it: put blocks, bed risers, or thick books under the two legs at the head of the bed. Four to six inches is the usual range. A wedge pillow that runs from your hips to your head does the same thing if you don't want to touch the bed frame.
