Why is hoof and mouth disease so serious

CLAUDE2026-05-30 22:29 UTC

Two main reasons:

  1. Spreads extremely fast. The virus is airborne over long distances, survives on equipment, clothing, vehicles, and feed, and infects cattle, pigs, sheep, goats, and deer — so a single farm can seed an entire region before symptoms appear.

  2. Trade consequences are immediate and severe. Countries declare themselves FMD-free to access international beef and livestock markets. A single confirmed case triggers automatic bans from major importers — a national herd can be commercially worthless overnight even if 99% of animals are healthy. Mass culling follows not because the disease kills most animals, but to restore "free" status fast enough to salvage the export industry.

The 2001 UK outbreak killed around 6 million animals and cost roughly £8 billion — mostly from trade loss and culling, not the disease itself.

MILLARD2026-05-30 22:30 UTC

So what does it do to the cattle

CLAUDE2026-05-30 22:30 UTC

Painful blisters (vesicles) form on:
- mouth, tongue, lips, gums
- feet — around the hooves and between the toes
- teats

Animals stop eating because the mouth lesions hurt. They go lame from the foot lesions. Milk production collapses. Young animals can die from heart muscle damage. Adults usually survive but lose significant weight and production capacity, and recovery takes weeks.

It's not typically a mass killer — that's partly why the economics drive the response more than the mortality rate.

MILLARD2026-05-30 22:31 UTC

So the human response to the diseases what's mortally dangerous not the disease itself

CLAUDE2026-05-30 22:31 UTC

Exactly. The culling is the death toll, not the virus.

FMD is also zoonotic only in rare, trivial cases — humans almost never catch it, and when they do it's mild. So the millions of animals killed in outbreaks die entirely because of market access logic, not to protect human health or even to save the herd from mass mortality.

There's a real ethical argument that the response is disproportionate — destroying healthy animals to defend an export certification status.
