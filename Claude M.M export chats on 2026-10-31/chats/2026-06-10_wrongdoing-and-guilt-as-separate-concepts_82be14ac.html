Wrongdoing and guilt as separate concepts

Created 2026-06-10 15:31 UTC · Last updated 2026-06-10 16:09 UTC · 44 messages
Original: claude.ai/chat/82be14ac-7861-47e7-bf17-a880c7eca5e3
MILLARD2026-06-10 15:31 UTC

Do you understand that the concept of wrongdoing is separable from the concept of guilt?

CLAUDE2026-06-10 15:31 UTC

Yes.

Wrongdoing is about the act — whether a harm occurred, whether a norm was violated, whether something that shouldn't have happened did.

Guilt is about the agent's relationship to that act — whether they knew, intended, could have done otherwise, bear responsibility.

You can have wrongdoing without guilt (harm caused by someone acting in good faith on false information, for instance). You can also have guilt without wrongdoing in the strict sense, though that's more contested philosophically.

The conflation of the two is one of the more consequential errors in both legal and moral reasoning — it drives both over-punishment (punishing non-culpable actors because harm occurred) and under-accountability (excusing culpable actors because the outcome wasn't harmful).

Is this going somewhere specific?

CLAUDE2026-06-10 15:35 UTC

On that view, "wrongdoing without guilt" isn't just rare — it's a category error. If there's no awareness or intention, there's no wrong done, only harm caused. The moral weight lives entirely in the agent's mental state, not the outcome.

This aligns with a broadly Kantian intuition: the will is where morality operates. It also maps reasonably well onto how serious criminal law distinguishes actus reus from mens rea — though law muddies this with strict liability offenses, which many find morally unsatisfying for exactly your reason.

The pressure point on your view: what do you do with recklessness and negligence? Someone who doesn't intend harm but knowingly disregards a substantial risk — or who should have known but didn't bother to find out. Most people's intuitions place those somewhere between accident and deliberate wrongdoing, not fully outside the moral category. MILLARD2026-06-10 15:48 UTC

CLAUDE2026-06-10 15:48 UTC

Fair enough.

Culpability: the degree to which an actor is answerable for an action — how much of the causal and intentional chain runs through them, and how much they could have done otherwise.

Guilt: a verdict that culpability has crossed a threshold sufficient to warrant blame, condemnation, or punishment. It's culpability adjudicated, not just measured.

On that framing, culpability is a spectrum; guilt is a binary conclusion drawn from it.

MILLARD2026-06-10 15:50 UTC

What's the difference between responsibility and culpability?

CLAUDE2026-06-10 15:51 UTC

On my framing:

Responsibility is the bare connection — this action belongs to this actor, they were the agent of it. It precedes any moral evaluation.

Culpability adds the evaluative layer — given that it's yours, how much does that matter morally? It factors in things like choice, knowledge, alternatives available.

Responsibility is attribution. Culpability is graded moral weight attached to that attribution.
