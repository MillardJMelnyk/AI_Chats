Understanding: at a short-term trend reversal (up→down or down→up), tick-to-tick bid travel shrinks — the ratio of consecutive single-tick travels (travel at tick t vs travel at tick t−1) trends down through the reversal. Travel = |bid_t − bid_t−1|.

Three variables named: per-tick travel (the bid/bid spread itself), tick density (ticks per time unit), net price spread per time unit (signed net move over that window).

Ambiguous: is "the ratio" literally tick-over-tick travel (travel_t / travel_t−1), standalone — or travel normalized against tick density and/or net spread (e.g. net move ÷ sum of travels, efficiency-ratio style)? Different formulas. Which one?

CLAUDE2026-06-30 13:38 UTC

Net travel needs to drop MathAbs — your code has it absolute, the spec wants it signed. Beyond that, this needs new state outside the buffers: live bar timestamp for rollover detection, last live tick price plus whether one's been recorded yet, and a running live-segment sum — reset every new bar, not seeded from the prior close, matching your "first tick, no data" spec. Total travel on the live bar becomes every historical segment in the window except the last one, replaced by that running sum.

One thing to confirm: with real history loaded, the live bar's 2nd tick won't actually publish 1.0 — Window1's other 28 historical segments are still in total travel alongside that one live segment, so it comes out small, not 1. You mean the live-tick bootstrap in isolation, not the literal published value at that moment — right?
