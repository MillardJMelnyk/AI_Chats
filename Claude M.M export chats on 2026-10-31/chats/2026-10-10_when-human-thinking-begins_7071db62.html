When do neuroscientists say that human thinking begins?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 15:37 UTC

Saturday, October 10, 2026, 9:37 AM CST

If you mean in an individual's development, most put the start of any conscious experience around 24–30 weeks of gestation, when the thalamus wires into the cortex (Likely), and some, like Hugo Lagercrantz, place it at birth. Reflective thinking about one's own thoughts doesn't appear until roughly 18–24 months.

CLAUDE2026-10-10 15:38 UTC

Have you weighed that sedation evidence, and does it change your read?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 15:45 UTC

Most researchers agree with you; the dominant view now is gradualist, with a dim, flickering awareness building from around 24–30 weeks and switching on and off with sleep-wake cycles, rather than a single on-switch at birth. Lagercrantz himself, writing with Changeux, softened it to "minimal consciousness" possibly present just before birth, which concedes the line isn't sharp.

MILLARD2026-10-10 15:46 UTC

So what's the earliest credible point?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 15:46 UTC

The earliest serious claim is around 12–15 weeks, from Stuart Derbyshire and John Bockmann (2020), who argued that early thalamic wiring into the developing cortex could support raw felt experience like pain (Unlikely by mainstream consensus, but credible and notable because Derbyshire previously held the 24-week line). The mainstream earliest point remains about 24 weeks, once thalamus-to-cortex connections are in place (Likely).
