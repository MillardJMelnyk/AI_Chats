Section 16 runs one way: you indemnify them for injuries, losses, claims and damages, plus costs and attorneys' fees, arising from any action or omission by you, or any breach or alleged breach of your warranty that the work was professional. No matching obligation from them, and no dollar ceiling.

Section 18 covers the same ground but symmetrically — you hold them harmless for your negligent or intentional acts, they hold you harmless for theirs.

Two problems with the pair. First, 16 is broader than 18 on your side: 18 needs negligence or intent, 16 triggers on any action or omission, including a merely alleged breach. So a claim you'd win could still cost you their defense bill. Second, having both means 16 quietly swallows the balance 18 appears to give you.

If you want it fixed, the cleanest ask is to strike the indemnity sentence from 16 and leave the warranty, letting 18 govern indemnity for both sides. Failing that, ask that 16 be limited to your gross negligence or willful misconduct and capped at the fees they've paid you.

Realistically, on a $10/hour contract they're unlikely to redline anything, and the practical exposure is low — teaching English over video rarely generates claims. Exhibit D is still the clause with real teeth.
