Germinating strawberry seeds in wet napkin

Created 2026-09-23 13:53 UTC · Last updated 2026-09-23 13:57 UTC · 18 messages
Original: claude.ai/chat/cba169ff-e28f-4e7f-b50c-dcd760d07e29
MILLARD2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

can i germinate strawberry seeds in a wet napkin?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

Yes.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

is it a good method?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

No.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

why?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

Strawberry seeds take 1–6 weeks to sprout, so the napkin usually goes moldy first. They also sprout better with light, and the hair-thin sprouts tear when you peel them off the napkin to plant. Sowing them on the surface of damp seed mix under a clear cover avoids all three problems.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

so i should just sow them directly into soil?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:53 UTC

Yes, pressed onto the surface of the soil, not buried.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:54 UTC

ok i put them in the fridge for a month and took them out, they have been out of the fridge for a week, are they still ok?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:54 UTC

Yes, a week out doesn't undo the cold treatment, so sow them now.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:54 UTC

how long does undo the cold treatment?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:54 UTC

There's no published number for strawberry seeds; seeds kept dry generally hold the cold treatment for weeks to months, but nobody has measured exactly when it wears off (Likely).

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:55 UTC

if i plant them directly into soil, how far apart should they be? how much water? ect

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:55 UTC

Sow them about 1–2 cm apart on the surface of seed-starting mix, pressed in but not covered.

Water by misting or bottom-watering so the surface stays damp all the time but never soggy, and keep a clear lid or plastic wrap over the tray to hold moisture in. Give them bright light out of harsh direct sun, at roughly 18–24°C.

Once the seedlings have 3 true leaves, move each one to its own pot; one plant per cut-down 10L jug, or two at most.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:56 UTC

what about planting them directly into outside soil?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:56 UTC

It works, but badly. The seeds are dust-sized and sit on the surface, so rain washes them away, one dry afternoon kills them, ants and birds carry them off, and weeds smother the seedlings during the 1–6 weeks they take to come up.

If you do it anyway, pick a spot with morning sun and afternoon shade, sow thickly on loosened, weeded soil, and keep the surface damp daily with a fine spray. Covering the spot with a clear plastic sheet or cut-off bottle until they sprout helps with the moisture.

MILLARD2026-09-23 13:57 UTC

should i do the plastic wrap thing with tomato seeds also?

CLAUDE2026-09-23 13:57 UTC

Optional. Tomato seeds sit about 6 mm deep and sprout in 5–10 days, so they don't need the cover the way strawberries do. If you use it anyway, take it off as soon as they sprout.
