CLAUDE2026-09-01 22:51 UTC

Ranked by what can actually cost you money:

1. Clause 18 — the 2-month penalty is a trap. It applies not just if you leave early, but if you trigger any rescission ground in Clause 16. A late utility bill or a neighbor complaint = 2 months' rent penalty plus eviction.

2. Clause 8 — unpaid utilities count as unpaid rent. Rent is not considered paid until water, gas and electricity are settled. A CFE bill you didn't know about puts you in default, which cascades into Clause 6 penalties and Clause 16 rescission.

3. Clauses 2 and 12 — extinción de dominio liability. You pay the landlord's lawyers and damages if the property is ever investigated or seized. Clause 12-VI makes mere suspicion by any authority a rescission ground, including for crimes committed outside the property, and including acts by any guest you let in. You carry that risk, the owner carries none.

4. Clause 6 — collection starts on day one. From the first day of non-payment the landlord triggers SBI's legal policy against you. No grace period, on top of the 2/4/6% escalating penalty.

5. Clause 16 — rescission without a judge, on grounds including "escándalos or inappropriate attitudes with neighbors" (undefined, subjective) and breach of a condominium internal rulebook that is not attached to this contract. Ask for it before signing.

6. Clause 10 — 48 hours to report hidden defects, in writing, and you need written authorization before any repair. Miss the window and the defect becomes yours. Combined with Declaration II(c), where you certify everything works and waive withholding rent over any defect.

7. Clause 15 — póliza jurídica at your cost, forever. You pay for it, you must renew it 30 days before expiry, and if you don't the landlord renews it and bills you without asking. Not renewing is itself grounds for rescission.

8. Clause 7 — rent runs until formal handover. Every month is payable in full even if you're not living there and even if the property hasn't been formally handed back. Get the handover documented the day you leave.

9. Clause 4 — deposit is one month, held 30 days after you leave, returned without interest, and cannot be applied to your last month's rent. So exiting early costs 2 months penalty plus a month of rent you can't offset.

10. Obligado solidario. You need a co-signer who is 100% liable for everything above. In Querétaro this normally means someone with property in the state.

11. Clause 20 — anything the agent told you verbally is void. Only what's written in this document counts.

Asymmetries worth naming: there is no clause obligating the landlord to maintain the property, no penalty on the landlord for anything, and no way for you to terminate for the landlord's breach without paying the 2-month penalty yourself.

Before signing: fill every blank (amounts, dates, bank details, addresses) — never sign with blanks. Get the photographic inventory in Clause 9 done thoroughly and keep a copy, since it's your only defense on the deposit. Get the condominium rulebook. You'll need CURP and RFC, which requires resident status, not a tourist visa.

I'm not a lawyer, and this is a Mexican contract under Querétaro law — for a 12-month commitment with a co-signer on the hook, an hour with a local abogado is worth it, particularly on items 1, 3 and 5.

Two things to be aware of when you assemble the full set of amendments:
