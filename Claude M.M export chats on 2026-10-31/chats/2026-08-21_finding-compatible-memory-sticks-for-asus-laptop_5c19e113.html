Finding compatible memory sticks for Asus laptop

MILLARD2026-08-21 16:55 UTC

I need to identify the memory sticks that fit in my laptop but I forgot me the model of the laptop, it's something like an Asus nj47v or something like that

CLAUDE2026-08-21 16:55 UTC

Don't guess the model — pull it and the installed sticks in one shot. Open PowerShell and run:

Get-CimInstance Win32_ComputerSystem | Select-Object Manufacturer,Model
Get-CimInstance Win32_PhysicalMemory | Select-Object Manufacturer,PartNumber,Capacity,Speed,SMBIOSMemoryType
Get-CimInstance Win32_PhysicalMemoryArray | Select-Object MaxCapacity,MemoryDevices

That gives the exact model, what's in each slot, the memory type (DDR3/DDR4), speed, how many slots exist, and the max the board takes — which is everything needed to buy a matching stick.

If Windows won't boot, the model is printed on the sticker on the underside of the laptop.

CLAUDE2026-08-21 16:56 UTC

Best match for "nj47v" is an Asus N46VJ or N56VJ — the 2012–13 N-series with the GT 635M/740M. Same platform either way, so the RAM answer is the same.

Buy: DDR3L SO-DIMM, 204-pin, PC3-12800 (1600 MHz), 8 GB per stick, 2 slots, 16 GB max.

DDR3L runs in both 1.35 V and 1.5 V boards, so it's the safe buy if the model guess is off by a letter. Two things that would break this: if the machine is actually a 2015-or-later Asus, it's DDR4 and the sticks won't fit; and if you're mixing with an existing stick rather than replacing both, match the speed or the pair drops to the slower one.

Cheapest verification without the laptop: the model is on the box, the receipt, or the original order email if you bought it online.

CLAUDE2026-08-21 16:56 UTC

No — Asus N-series goes N46VJ (14"), N56VJ (15.6"), N76VJ (17"); there's no N47. The 47 you're remembering is probably the U-series, where U47A and U47VC do exist.

Doesn't change the answer: U47 and N46/N56 are all the same 2012–13 Ivy Bridge platform. DDR3L SO-DIMM 204-pin PC3-12800, 8 GB per stick, 2 slots, 16 GB max.

CLAUDE2026-08-21 16:57 UTC

N76VJ takes the same sticks: DDR3L SO-DIMM, 204-pin, PC3-12800 (1600 MHz), 8 GB per stick.

Difference from the 14"/15" models — the N76 has 4 slots, so max is 32 GB, and the two extra slots sit under the keyboard, not the bottom hatch. If you only want the easy upgrade, do the two under the hatch and leave the others.

Buy 8 GB sticks in pairs. Any brand — Crucial, Kingston, Corsair — the spec is what matters, not the label.
