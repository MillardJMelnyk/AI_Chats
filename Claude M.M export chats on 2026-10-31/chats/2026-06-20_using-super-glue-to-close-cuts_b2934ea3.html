Can you actually use super glue to close a cut

CLAUDE2026-06-20 23:29 UTC

Yes, but with real caveats.

What works: Medical-grade cyanoacrylate adhesives (the same chemical family as super glue) are legitimately used to close minor wounds — this is what Dermabond and similar products are. They work by bonding skin edges together and forming a flexible seal as a substitute for a few stitches.

Why regular super glue isn't the same thing:
- Hardware-store super glue isn't sterile and often contains additives (like added solvents or different stabilizers) that aren't meant for skin contact and can irritate tissue or cause a stronger, more painful exothermic reaction as it cures.
- Medical cyanoacrylate (usually octyl- or butyl-cyanoacrylate) is formulated to be more flexible and less irritating than the methyl-cyanoacrylate in most super glue.

When it's reasonable as a stopgap: For a small, clean, shallow cut with edges that easily come together, on intact skin, in a pinch — it's not crazy, and some people do use it this way. Hold the edges closed, apply a thin layer along the top of the seam (not inside the wound), hold for a minute.

When you shouldn't:
- Deep cuts, anything gaping wide, wounds near eyes, joints, or genitals
- Puncture wounds, animal bites, or anything visibly dirty/contaminated (glue traps bacteria inside)
- Anything still bleeding heavily — glue won't hold under blood/moisture
- If you can see fat, muscle, or bone — that needs actual stitches

If a cut is gaping, deep, or won't stop bleeding, that's a "go get it actually closed" situation, not a glue situation.

How do you get the medical grade kind

CLAUDE2026-06-20 23:30 UTC

Medical-grade cyanoacrylate (Dermabond, SurgiSeal, LiquiBand, etc.) is mostly sold to clinicians, but a few paths work for regular people:

  • Pharmacy/drugstore — Some carry over-the-counter versions marketed for minor cuts (brand names like "Skin Glue," "New-Skin" liquid bandage variants, or generic "liquid stitches"). CVS, Walgreens, Walmart usually stock something in this category near the bandages.
  • Online retailers — Amazon and similar sell consumer cyanoacrylate skin adhesives explicitly labeled for wound closure. Look for "topical skin adhesive" or "liquid bandage," not just "super glue."
  • Outdoor/camping/first-aid suppliers — Companies selling backcountry first-aid kits often include a skin adhesive applicator, since stitches aren't an option in the field.
  • Veterinary suppliers — Some vet-supply sites sell cyanoacrylate skin adhesive in bulk; it's the same chemistry, sometimes cheaper, though obviously not FDA-labeled for human use.

A few things worth checking when buying: the listing should specify it's for topical/skin use (not general purpose), and ideally name the active ingredient as octyl or butyl cyanoacrylate — that's the more flexible, lower-irritation formulation actually used in clinics.
