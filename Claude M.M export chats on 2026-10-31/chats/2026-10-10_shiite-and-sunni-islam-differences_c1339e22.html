Shiite and Sunni Islam differences

What's the difference between Shiite and Sunni Islam?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:28 UTC

Saturday, October 10, 2026, (exact time unavailable) CST

The split began over who should lead after Muhammad: Sunnis (about 85–90% of Muslims) accepted the community's chosen caliphs starting with Abu Bakr, while Shiites held that leadership belonged to his cousin and son-in-law Ali and Ali's descendants, the Imams.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:28 UTC

Imams are unique to Shiites

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:28 UTC

Not the word: Sunnis also call prayer leaders and major scholars "imam" (Abu Hanifa, al-Shafi'i). What's uniquely Shiite is the Imamate, a line of divinely appointed, infallible leaders descended from Ali.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:29 UTC

They hold them to be infallible ?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:29 UTC

Yes, Twelver and Ismaili Shiites (the large majority) hold the Imams to be ma'sum, free from sin and error; Zaydi Shiites generally don't.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:29 UTC

What about the Sunnis? Do they also have a caste that's infallible ?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:29 UTC

No, Sunnis hold that no one after Muhammad is infallible; the closest equivalent is ijma, the belief that the community's scholars as a whole can't agree on an error.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:30 UTC

So Muhammad was infallible and then the scholars are collectively infallible

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:30 UTC

Broadly yes, though Sunni infallibility for Muhammad covers conveying revelation and avoiding major sin, not every worldly judgment (the hadith on date-palm pollination has him admitting a mistake). Ijma's infallibility applies only to a genuine consensus of qualified scholars, which in practice has been reached on relatively few questions.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:31 UTC

Are there major differences in their beliefs apart from which lineage gets to be infallible ?

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:31 UTC

Core theology (one God, Muhammad, the Quran, prayer, fasting, pilgrimage) is shared; the real differences flow from the succession dispute: Shiites use their own hadith collections and reject many narrated by companions who opposed Ali, follow the Ja'fari legal school, and allow temporary marriage (mut'ah), which Sunnis forbid. Twelvers also believe the Mahdi is their 12th Imam, alive in hiding since 874, and give senior clerics (marja') a binding authority that Sunni scholars don't have.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:32 UTC

What predominates in Iran Sunni or Shiite

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:32 UTC

Shiite, about 90% of Iranians, overwhelmingly Twelver; Sunnis are roughly 5–10%, mostly Kurds, Baluch, and Turkmen.

MILLARD2026-10-10 20:32 UTC

So is Sharia law specific to Shiites

CLAUDE2026-10-10 20:32 UTC

No, sharia is Islamic law for both; Sunnis interpret it through four schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) and Shiites through the Ja'fari school, and Sunni Saudi Arabia applies it as strictly as Iran does.
