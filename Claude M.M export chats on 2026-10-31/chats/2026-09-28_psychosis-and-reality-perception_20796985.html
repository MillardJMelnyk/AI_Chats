how true is this:

Psychosis results from severing one's grasp of reality from reality itself.

CLAUDE2026-09-28 14:18 UTC

As a description it's accurate; as an explanation it's circular: losing touch with reality is the definition of psychosis, so saying psychosis "results from" that is like saying fever results from high body temperature. The actual causes, such as brain chemistry, sleep deprivation, drugs, trauma, or illness, are what produce the break.

When it comes to Munchausen by proxy, do we know of cases where children later are reconciled with the offending parent ?

CLAUDE2026-09-28 14:35 UTC

Rarely, and I found no documented case of full reconciliation: survivor accounts overwhelmingly end in estrangement, like the adult who broke off contact with her mother and her entire family, since they had always backed the mother up, or the survivor who was advised by a doctor to have nothing to do with her.

The main obstacle is that the offending parent almost never admits it, so there's nothing to reconcile with. One survivor describes a mother who still initiates all the contact while the rest of the family refuses to believe she deliberately made her ill. That's contact without reconciliation. It also cuts against your abuse-versus-divorce contrast: the door you said abuse leaves open is, in this case, usually shut by the abuser's denial.

Grade: Likely that genuine reconciliation is rare. Missing evidence: Libow's 1995 study of adult victims, the main systematic look at how their relationships with the offending parent turned out, which I didn't read.
