Jewish ethnic diversity beyond Ashkenazi and Sephardic

Created 2026-09-29 17:39 UTC · Last updated 2026-09-29 17:40 UTC · 4 messages

MILLARD2026-09-29 17:39 UTC

I just heard a claim that there are only two kinds of Jews Ashkenazi and Sephardic.

CLAUDE2026-09-29 17:39 UTC

That's wrong. Ashkenazi and Sephardic are the two largest groups, but there are several others: Mizrahi (Middle East and North Africa, often lumped in with Sephardim), Yemenite (Teimanim), Ethiopian (Beta Israel), Romaniote (Greece), Italian (Italkim), Indian (Bene Israel, Cochin, Baghdadi), Bukharan (Central Asia), Mountain Jews (Caucasus), Georgian, and the small Kaifeng community in China.

MILLARD2026-09-29 17:39 UTC

Give me proportions for each group

CLAUDE2026-09-29 17:40 UTC

Rough global shares of ~15.8 million Jews (graded Likely; no census tracks these categories, heavy intermarriage in Israel blurs them, and Sephardi vs. Mizrahi is often counted as one bloc):

Group Approx. people Share
Ashkenazi 11–12 million ~70–75%
Sephardi + Mizrahi combined 3.5–4 million ~22–25%
Yemenite (usually counted inside Mizrahi) 350–450k ~2.5%
Ethiopian (Beta Israel) ~170k ~1%
Bukharan ~150–200k ~1%
Mountain Jews ~100–150k ~0.8%
Georgian ~80–100k ~0.6%
Indian (all three groups) ~80–90k ~0.5%
Italian (Italkim) a few thousand <0.1%
Romaniote a few thousand <0.05%
Kaifeng descendants ~1,000 ~0.01%

The overlap is why the shares don't add neatly. In Israel alone, roughly half of Jews are Sephardi/Mizrahi, not a quarter.
