Removing baked-on stovetop grease

Created 2026-10-11 22:14 UTC · Last updated 2026-10-11 22:16 UTC · 6 messages
Original: claude.ai/chat/d3f93412-a061-4a2a-80d4-a2c7345fc4ea
MILLARD2026-10-11 22:14 UTC

It's been a long time since I clean my stove top so I have a lot of big done grease and that kind of thing on the surface. What products can I use to coat the crap, let it sit and soften it up so that it doesn't take a bunch of scrubbing?

CLAUDE2026-10-11 22:15 UTC

Sunday, October 11, 2026, 4:14 PM CST — Spray it with Easy-Off oven cleaner (sold at Walmart/Soriana), or slather on a paste of baking soda and Salvo dish soap, cover with hot wet rags, and let it sit 30–60 minutes before wiping. If it's a glass top, skip the oven cleaner and use only the baking soda paste.

MILLARD2026-10-11 22:15 UTC

I've tried using that baking soda mixture it doesn't work

CLAUDE2026-10-11 22:15 UTC

Then go to lye: spray Easy-Off Heavy Duty or Mr. Músculo Horno (both sold locally), cover it with plastic wrap, and let it sit a few hours or overnight so the grease dissolves instead of just softening. On a glass top, use a razor scraper on the softened crud instead of scrubbing.

MILLARD2026-10-11 22:16 UTC

This is a stainless steel top

CLAUDE2026-10-11 22:16 UTC

Easy-Off or Mr. Músculo Horno works on stainless, but test a small hidden spot first because long soaks can leave dull or discolored patches. Rinse it off thoroughly and wipe with the grain.
