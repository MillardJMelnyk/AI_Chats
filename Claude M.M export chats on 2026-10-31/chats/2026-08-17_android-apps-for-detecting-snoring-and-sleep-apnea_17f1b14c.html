MILLARD2026-08-17 02:44 UTC

Do any of the Android apps for monitoring sleep do a good job of detecting snoring or possible sleep apnea problems?

CLAUDE2026-08-17 02:44 UTC

Snoring: yes, several do it well. Sleep apnea: no — none of them detect it reliably, and none diagnose it.

Best Android picks:

  • SnoreLab — the strongest dedicated snore tracker. Records audio clips, gives a nightly snore score, tracks trends over months, and lets you test remedies (position, nasal strips) against your own baseline. Clean and simple to use.
  • Sleep as Android — Android-only, good if you want full sleep tracking with snore detection built in. Also has a sonar mode that uses the phone speaker/mic to track breathing rate.
  • SnoreClock — free, exports raw audio, no scoring gimmicks. Useful if you want to hand a technician unprocessed recordings.

What they actually catch: loudness, frequency, timing, and audible silence-then-gasp patterns. What they miss: blood oxygen drops, which is what apnea severity is measured by. Phone-mic apps regularly miss quiet apneas and flag ordinary snoring as suspicious.

Diagnosing apnea takes a home sleep test or in-lab study. If you want a real number at home, a fingertip pulse oximeter that logs overnight SpO2 (~$40) tells you far more than any app — repeated desaturations below 90% is the signal doctors care about.

So can you have concerning sleep apnea without snoring?

CLAUDE2026-08-17 02:46 UTC

Yes.

Two ways it happens:

  • Central sleep apnea — the brain stops signaling the breathing muscles. No airway vibration, so usually no snoring at all. Common with heart failure, opioid use, or at altitude.
  • Quiet obstructive apnea — the airway still collapses, but the person doesn't produce loud noise between events. More common in women, thin people, and older adults. Partners often report silence-then-gasp rather than rumbling.

Given all concerning degrees of sleep apnea, what percentage of cases have no snoring?

CLAUDE2026-08-17 02:47 UTC

No solid single number exists — it depends on whether snoring is measured or self-reported, and self-report is unreliable.

The ranges that get cited:

  • Obstructive apnea without habitual snoring: roughly 10–20% of diagnosed cases. Higher in women, where under-reporting is worse.
  • Central sleep apnea (no snoring by mechanism): about 5–10% of all apnea cases, higher in heart-failure and opioid populations.

So somewhere around 15–25% of concerning apnea comes without meaningful snoring. That figure is soft — studies that objectively record sound find fewer true non-snorers than studies relying on a partner's report.
