A few C2-appropriate topics that actually challenge advanced learners (basic tense/aspect stuff is too easy at this level):

Inversion for emphasis/formality — "Not only did she refuse, but she also demanded an apology." "Rarely have I seen such dedication." Great for formal writing and rhetorical flair.

Subjunctive mood (mandative + hypothetical) — "It's essential that he be informed" vs. "If I were to reconsider..." Also nice to contrast British/American differences here.

Cleft sentences (it-clefts and wh-clefts) — "What surprised me was his silence." "It was the delay that caused the problem." Useful for teaching emphasis and information structure in writing.

Nuanced modal verbs for speculation/deduction — "She might well have known," "He can't possibly have finished already," "You needn't have worried" — subtle differences in certainty and retrospective judgment.

Ellipsis and substitution in discourse — how fluent speakers/writers omit repeated elements ("I'd love to, but I can't" / "She has, hasn't she?") — good for natural-sounding speech.

Fronting and marked word order — moving elements to the front for cohesion or emphasis: "Down came the rain." "Such was his anger that..."

Discourse markers and hedging language — for academic/professional register: "arguably," "it could be argued that," "not least because," "insofar as."

If you want one with strong engagement potential: inversion + cleft sentences together works well as a lesson — you can build it around persuasive writing or op-eds, have students rewrite plain sentences for rhetorical effect, then produce their own paragraph using at least 3 structures.
