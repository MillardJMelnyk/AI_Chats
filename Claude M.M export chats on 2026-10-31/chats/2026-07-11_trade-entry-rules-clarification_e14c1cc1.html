7 explained:

What it measures. Condition 7 isn't about Indy1's slope being negative — that's not checked here at all. It's about the slope falling: this sample's slope minus the previous sample's slope is negative. For a short, Indy1's momentum must be moving shortward right now — accelerating down if the slope is already negative, or at least decelerating if it's still positive. It's the freshness check: conditions 3–6 can all be true late in a move that's already stalling; #7 requires the move to still be gaining, not coasting.

What the sampling spec says. Two cases:

  • Historical bars (replay, or any already-closed bar): slope at bar n's close minus slope at bar n−1's close. Clean, fixed values.
  • The current live tick: slope at this tick minus slope at the last tick of the most recent closed bar. Not tick-minus-previous-tick, and not tick-minus-anything-inside-the-current-bar.

Why the reference point is the last closed bar. The current bar is unfinished. If the delta were tick-vs-previous-tick, it would flip sign on every wiggle of tick noise — the condition would chatter uselessly. If it were tick-vs-some-earlier-point-in-the-current-bar, the reference would move as the bar builds, and the same market picture would give different answers depending on when ticks happened to arrive. Anchoring to the last closed bar gives one fixed reference all through the current bar: the question every tick answers is "is momentum shortward of where it stood when the last bar finished?" — stable, and it converges exactly to the closed-bar-to-closed-bar comparison the moment the bar closes, so replay and live measure the same thing.
