Barely: by the paper's own numbers, kids who got only a flu shot and kids who got only a COVID shot showed nearly identical, modest autism odds versus kids with neither (1.11 and 1.12), which points at which families get their kids shots and diagnosed, not at the COVID vaccine.

Grade: Likely that the association comes from who gets vaccinated and when diagnoses happen, not from vaccine harm. It's not Highly likely because the authors also report that COVID-only children had higher autism odds than flu-only children in a direct comparison (1.45), which doesn't square with those two near-equal numbers, and the abstract doesn't resolve it.

Missing evidence: whether each child's autism diagnosis came before or after the first shot. The authors concede the survey doesn't record diagnosis dates, so it can't show that vaccination preceded diagnosis. Their one timing subgroup rests on 9 exposed cases, and it dates the shot, not the diagnosis.
