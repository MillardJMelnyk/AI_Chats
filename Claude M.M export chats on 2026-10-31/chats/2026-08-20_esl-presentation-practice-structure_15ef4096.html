CLAUDE2026-08-20 23:45 UTC

Presentation structure (6 blocks)

  1. Hook + frame — who he is, what he does, one sentence on why the audience should care.
  2. Context/problem — the situation his work addresses.
  3. What he does — the process or solution, 3 steps max.
  4. Evidence — one number, one concrete example.
  5. Takeaway/ask — what he wants the listener to remember or do.
  6. Q&A — 3 questions he expects, 1 he dreads.

Language he needs (this is the teaching part)

  • Signposting: First I'll cover… / Moving on to… / To wrap up…
  • Highlighting: The key point here is… / What this means in practice is…
  • Data: This accounts for roughly… / We saw a drop of…
  • Hedging: It tends to… / In most cases… / Broadly speaking…
  • Stalling in Q&A: That's a good question — let me put it this way… / If I understand you correctly…
  • Deflecting: I don't have that figure with me, but I can follow up.

Class flow (about 60–75 min)

  • 5 min — he picks the topic and the audience (colleague? client? new hire?). Audience choice changes the register.
  • 10 min — he fills the 6 blocks in note form, keywords only, no full sentences.
  • 10 min — you feed him the signposting phrases above, he rewrites his transitions using them.
  • 10 min — silent rehearsal from notes.
  • 7 min — full delivery, no interruptions. You take notes, don't correct live.
  • 5 min — you ask 3 questions, including one hostile or vague one.
  • 10 min — feedback, then he re-delivers just the weakest block.

Feedback rubric (score 1–5 each)

Structure and signposting · fluency and pausing · grammatical accuracy under pressure · precision of vocabulary (does he use the actual terminology of his field in English) · pacing and eye contact · Q&A recovery.

At his level the two things worth hammering are transitions and Q&A recovery — those are where fluent speakers still sound rehearsed or fall apart.

CLAUDE2026-08-22 21:49 UTC

The article's phrases are organized by meeting stage, not by presentation section — so the way to put them in context is to stage the whole thing as a meeting where he presents, rather than a monologue. He plays chair and presenter; you play the room.

Layout — 8 stages, phrases attached to each

Stage What he does Phrase function to pull from the article
1. Pre-meeting email Writes 2 lines inviting you Proposing a meeting, stating purpose, asking people to review the agenda
2. Open Welcomes, thanks, states purpose Greeting, appreciating attendance, "the purpose of today is…"
3. Frame Introduces himself and his role Self-introduction, explaining his function to people unfamiliar with it
4. Present His work: problem → what he does → evidence Speaking from expertise ("from my perspective as…", "based on my work…")
5. Discussion You push back; he responds Agreeing, disagreeing politely, counterproposing
6. Manage the room You interrupt / go off-topic Redirecting to the agenda, deferring a question, handing the floor to someone
7. Close Recaps and ends Summarizing, three takeaways, thanking, wrapping up
8. Follow-up Writes 2 lines after Touching base, checking in, next agenda

Stages 5 and 6 are where the real value is — that's the language he can't fake, and it's the half of the article most learners skip.

How to run it

Give him the phrase list before, not during. He picks three phrases per stage and marks them on his notes. During delivery he must use them; you tick them off. Anything he doesn't use in speech, he doesn't own.

Your job as the room: agree once, disagree once, ask one vague question, interrupt once with something irrelevant. That forces stages 5 and 6 to actually happen instead of him just narrating.

Difficulty dial, if the first pass is too easy: strip his notes to keywords only; make yourself hostile rather than neutral; require a different phrase each time he transitions, no repeats.

neighbourhood watch for an apartment complex, what is it called

CLAUDE2026-08-22 22:05 UTC

Apartment Watch or Building Watch — both are used. Community Watch or Resident Watch is the broader HOA/complex term.

In Mexico, the equivalent body in a condominio is the comité de vigilancia, and the street-level version is vigilancia vecinal.

Branch off someones idea synonyms

CLAUDE2026-08-22 22:40 UTC

Build on — the standard business one. "Building on what Ana said…"

Others by shade:

  • Piggyback on — informal, common in meetings
  • Expand on / Extend — take it further
  • Pick up on — grab one thread of it
  • Run with — take it and go
  • Spin off — head in a new direction from it
  • Springboard off — use it as a launch point
  • Riff on — loose, creative

Meeting phrasings: "To build on that…" / "Just picking up on your point…" / "Adding to what you said…" / "That connects to something I was thinking…"
