Now let me check what happens on the live bar specifically — the provisional vs. committed distinction:

The MQ5 indicator's real-time signal price logic is identical to what the Python port does. The key detail is the provisional vs. committed distinction:

On the live bar (the still-forming candle), a normal cocked-trigger signal is provisional — isProvisional = true because isNewBar is false. In that case, execPrice falls through to close[iIX] (line 2227), which is the current tick's close — i.e., the live price at the moment the condition is met. The signal can still be reverted if close moves back above/below the cock level before the bar closes.

Once the bar closes and the next bar opens (isNewBar becomes true), the provisional signal gets committed. At that point the execution price has already been recorded as the live close at the moment the trigger fired.

For committed-on-arrival signals — backstops, spikes, force-committed aggressive flip-checks — the price is computed immediately with no provisional phase. In AGG_EMA mode that's ema_tema + direction × sensitivity, clamped to the bar's high/low range.

So in real-time operation, the actual execution price for a normal trigger is the tick price (close) at the instant the cocked condition fires on the live bar. The MOCK_PRICE_* modes are only relevant for simulating what that price would have been on historical bars where the exact intra-bar tick isn't available.
