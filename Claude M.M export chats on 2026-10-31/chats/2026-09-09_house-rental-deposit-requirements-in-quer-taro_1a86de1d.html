House rental deposit requirements in Querétaro

Created 2026-09-09 19:06 UTC · Last updated 2026-09-09 19:11 UTC · 4 messages
Original: claude.ai/chat/1a86de1d-4e27-43ef-8fc7-ade74af8fd64
MILLARD2026-09-09 19:06 UTC

My son is trying to rent a house in Querétaro. He asked the poliza people to show him a draft of the contract so that he could see the terms. They want him to put a deposit on the house equivalent to one month's rent before they'll even show him the contract. I could understand a good faith deposit. I cannot understand a deposit of that amount, 8300 pesos. is this standard for polizas here?

CLAUDE2026-09-09 19:06 UTC

No. One month's deposit is a standard amount, but paying it before seeing the contract is not standard and is backwards.

How it normally runs here: the póliza company investigates the tenant (credit, income, references, guarantor), 3–7 business days, then the contract draft goes to both sides for review, and money — deposit plus the póliza fee — moves at signing. The póliza's whole selling point is that both parties see terms in writing first.

What is common is an apartado to hold the property off the market. That's usually a partial amount, always backed by a written receipt stating what it covers and under what conditions it's returned or applied to the deposit. A verbal "give us 8,300 and then we'll show you the paper" is not that.

Two things to check before he pays anything: who is actually asking — the póliza despacho, the inmobiliaria, or the owner — and whether they'll put the apartado terms in writing. If they refuse to hand over a draft contract or a signed receipt, that's a walk-away.

MILLARD2026-09-09 19:11 UTC

When you say one month's rent deposit is standard, what kind of deposit are you saying is standard for?

CLAUDE2026-09-09 19:11 UTC

The depósito en garantía — the security deposit paid at signing, held by the landlord for the whole lease and returned at the end minus damages or unpaid amounts. One to two months is normal for that.

Not the apartado (holding money, usually partial and applied to the deposit later) and not the póliza fee itself, which typically runs 30–40% of one month's rent as a one-time charge that nobody gets back.
