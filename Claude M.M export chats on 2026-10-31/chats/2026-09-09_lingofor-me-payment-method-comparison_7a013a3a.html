MILLARD2026-09-09 19:10 UTC

i am trying to get paid through lingofor.me, would it be better to do so through payoneer or through paypal?

CLAUDE2026-09-09 19:11 UTC

Payoneer.

PayPal Mexico takes a bigger bite on both ends: the commercial receiving fee is 3.95% + $4 MXN, and all PayPal fees in Mexico carry 16% IVA on top, plus an extra percentage for cross-border payments, and 3.50% plus IVA above the base exchange rate when the money is converted to pesos. Call it roughly 8-10% gone by the time it hits your bank.

Payoneer: about 1% to receive, $1.50 flat to withdraw USD to a USD account, or around 2% conversion if you withdraw to a peso account. Two to three percent total.

Two things that can flip it: Payoneer charges $29.95 a year, waived only if you receive over $2,000 in twelve months — under that, on small teaching income, the flat fee eats the advantage. And roughly 75% of Payoneer's negative reviews are about account freezes or blocked funds, often during verification, so start verification before your first invoice is due.

MILLARD2026-09-09 19:55 UTC

is payoneer allowed to make me verify myself through id after 2 years of holding the account and already providing verification just cause i was sent payment? They are withholding my payment

CLAUDE2026-09-09 19:55 UTC

Yes. Their terms and their KYC/AML obligations let them re-verify you at any point in the account's life, and a first incoming payment after a quiet stretch is one of the standard triggers — new payer, new corridor, dormant account waking up. Prior verification doesn't grandfather you.

They're also allowed to hold the funds until it clears. The money isn't gone; it sits in limbo.

Fastest path: upload exactly what the Verification Center asks for, ID and any source-of-funds document showing what the payment is for — your Lingofor.me contract or invoice — in one go. Mismatched name spelling or an address that doesn't match your ID is what stalls these.

If it drags past a couple of weeks with no movement, Payoneer Inc. is a US-licensed money transmitter, so a complaint to the New York Department of Financial Services and the CFPB tends to unstick cases that support tickets won't.

i dont have a valid id, is there another way?

CLAUDE2026-09-09 19:56 UTC

No. A valid, unexpired government photo ID is the hard requirement, and expired documents are rejected outright. Proof of address doesn't substitute for it.

What counts, any one of: passport, driver's license, national ID card. Living in Mexico, your residente temporal or permanente card is a government-issued photo ID and works if you hold one. So does a Mexican driver's license — Querétaro state issues those to foreigners with residency.

If the only gap is an expired US passport, renewal from Mexico goes through the embassy in Mexico City or a consulate; check their page for whether they're taking mail-in adult renewals, since that varies by post and changes.

Meanwhile the payment sits. You can ask Payoneer support to return it to Lingofor.me and have them pay you another way, but PayPal, Wise, and any bank will ask for the same document, so that only helps if the alternative payer is someone who'll pay you outside the regulated rails.

Which ID situation is it — expired, lost, or never had one? The answer differs.

i dont have any valid form of ID right now

CLAUDE2026-09-09 19:57 UTC

Then the Payoneer money stays frozen until you fix the ID problem. There's no unlock without one.

Two things worth doing in parallel.

First, the money: if you already hold a Mexican bank account, its KYC was done when you opened it, so an international wire into it needs no new ID from you. Ask Payoneer to return the payment to Lingofor.me and have them wire you directly. That gets you paid now.

Second, the ID itself. Assuming you're a US citizen, the passport is the document that unlocks everything else, and State has a path for people with no ID:

  • Proof of citizenship: a certified copy of your US birth certificate, or an expired passport if you still physically have it — expired passports are still accepted as proof of citizenship even though Payoneer won't take one as ID. Order the birth certificate from your birth state's vital records office; some states accept a notarized application when you have no ID to photocopy.
  • Proof of identity: if you have no ID at all, you can bring an identifying witness to the appointment — someone who has known you at least two years and has their own valid ID. They fill out a form in front of the consular officer.
  • Where: the US Embassy in Mexico City, by appointment. Nothing closer to Querétaro handles passports.

If you're not a US citizen, say which passport you'd be applying for — the route changes completely.

CLAUDE2026-09-09 20:00 UTC

Manual review changes who looks at the document, not whether you need one. It's there for valid IDs that the automated check rejects — bad scan, name mismatch, selfie fails. It won't approve you with no ID.

The one angle worth using: the Verification Center's dropdown lists fewer document types than support can actually accept, and the accepted list varies by country. Support has approved documents for people that never appeared as an option in the upload form. So open a ticket, say plainly that you hold no unexpired passport, license, or national ID, and ask what they accept for your nationality and country of residence. Push it to a human rather than guessing at the dropdown.

Don't count on it. Plan on the wire-to-your-bank route in parallel.

CLAUDE2026-09-09 20:00 UTC

Check that 16% before you accept it — the IVA is 16% of PayPal's fee, not 16% of your payment. If you added 5.4% + 3.5% + 16%, you double-counted. Actual all-in for an international payment converted to pesos runs about 10%.

The bigger question: is your PayPal account already verified and linked to a Mexican bank? PayPal Mexico requires identity verification to withdraw to a bank account, and it holds the balance until that's done. If yours isn't verified, you hit the exact same ID wall and just move the frozen money from one wallet to another.

If it is verified, take PayPal, eat the ~10% on this payment, and fix the ID before the next one.

Where did the 16% figure come from — PayPal's own quote on the pending payment, or your own math?

MILLARD2026-09-09 20:01 UTC

it would juhst be a payment in USD from lingofor.me to my paypal account which is linked to my USD bank. how mucbh would they take out?

CLAUDE2026-09-09 20:02 UTC

About 4.5% to 5%, plus $0.49. No currency conversion, so the expensive part doesn't apply.

The breakdown: 2.99% + $0.49 if they send it as goods and services, or 3.49% + $0.49 if they pay a PayPal invoice or use Checkout, plus 1.5% on top because the payment crosses a border. Withdrawal to your linked US bank is free on standard transfer.

On $500 that's roughly $23 to $25. On $1,000, $45 to $50.

If they send it as friends and family funded from their balance or bank, you receive the full amount and the sender pays nothing — but a company paying a contractor generally won't do that, and it strips your recourse if the payment is disputed.

The 16% you saw was PayPal Mexico's peso pricing. It doesn't apply to a USD account paying out to a USD bank.

CLAUDE2026-09-09 20:03 UTC

PayPal has no "paycheck" category — a company paying a contractor goes through as a commercial transaction and the fee comes out of your side automatically.

One exception that's common with teaching platforms: PayPal Payouts (formerly Mass Pay), the bulk rail used to pay a batch of contractors at once. There the fee is 2% capped at $20 per payment for cross-border, and the sender chooses whether to absorb it or pass it to the recipient. Most platforms absorb it, so you'd get the full amount or close to it.

Ask Lingofor.me directly whether they send via Payouts and whether they cover the fee. That's the only thing that determines your number, and they know it.

If you meant would Lingofor.me itself withhold something on their end, that's a separate question — say so and I'll take it.
