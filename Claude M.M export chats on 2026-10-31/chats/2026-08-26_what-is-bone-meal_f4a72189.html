what is bone meal?

CLAUDE2026-08-26 20:10 UTC

Ground-up animal bone, sold as a slow-release fertilizer high in phosphorus and calcium.

MILLARD2026-08-26 20:10 UTC

what kind of bones?

CLAUDE2026-08-26 20:10 UTC

Cattle bones, mostly — slaughterhouse byproduct, sometimes with pig or other livestock bone mixed in.

MILLARD2026-08-26 20:10 UTC

what about chicken bones?

CLAUDE2026-08-26 20:10 UTC

Rarely used commercially — poultry bone goes into poultry meal for animal feed instead. You can make your own from chicken bones: bake them until brittle, then grind.

MILLARD2026-08-26 20:11 UTC

are chicken bones good for bonemeal and tomato and pepperplants?

CLAUDE2026-08-26 20:11 UTC

Yes. Chicken bone gives the same phosphorus and calcium as beef bone — good for fruit set and helps against blossom end rot. Bake them dry and grind fine; coarse chunks take years to break down.

MILLARD2026-08-26 20:12 UTC

when should i add the bonemeal to plants? while sprouting is ok?

CLAUDE2026-08-26 20:12 UTC

No — skip it while sprouting; mix it into the container soil at transplant, a tablespoon per 10L jug worked into the root zone.

MILLARD2026-08-26 20:13 UTC

what else do tomatoes and peppers need and how can i make it at home?

CLAUDE2026-08-26 20:13 UTC

Nitrogen, potassium, magnesium, and steady organic matter — containers leach fast, so they need feeding every couple of weeks once fruiting starts.

Nitrogen (leaves, early growth): diluted urine, 1 part to 10 parts water, once a week early on. Free and strong. Coffee grounds and composted kitchen scraps are milder.

Potassium (fruit size and flavor, what they eat most of): dried banana peels ground to powder, or wood ash — a teaspoon per jug, no more, it raises pH.

Magnesium (yellowing between leaf veins): Epsom salt, 1 tsp per 4L water, once a month.

Calcium: your bone meal covers it. Ground eggshells help but break down slowly.

Ease off nitrogen once they flower or you get big green plants and little fruit.
