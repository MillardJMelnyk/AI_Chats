CLAUDE2026-08-03 17:32 UTC

The interview is easy and informal — expect a short conversational chat about your experience, not a test.

Process (from candidates)
Glassdoor rates the interview difficulty 1.7 out of 5, with 67% calling the experience positive. One candidate described about four days from online application to interview, a very relaxed and informal conversation that didn't last long, with the interviewer friendly and casual and mainly explaining the work rather than probing experience or education. Another write-up describes it the same way: a discussion of your experience and what's expected in the role. No demo lesson is reported anywhere — unlike Lingoda, which does require one.

What they are
Online school founded 2018 under FieldFor Technologies LLC, based in Coral Gables, Florida, serving students in 16+ countries across the Americas. Official teacher page: 1:1 video classes, 24/7 flexible scheduling, 30-minute minimum but up to 2 hours, CEFR-based conversational method split into work cycles (conversational, verb, vocabulary, grammar, exercises, review), material provided. Students get teachers at random, so you read the previous teachers' notes on each student before class. Two class types: Regular (their material/program) and Specific (student brings their own material).

What teachers actually say
Pay US$2–5 per 30-minute class ($4–10/hr) with small bonuses; staff meetings unpaid; the 15-minute pre-class wait unpaid; student no-show pays half. Glassdoor 2.7/5 with 38% recommending; Indeed 1/5 from a handful of reviews. Recurring complaints: teaching material full of errors, disorganized coordination, payroll mistakes you have to chase, pressure to take classes outside your stated availability, and last-minute class assignment. One reviewer said they were assured they'd only teach classes they accepted, then were bombarded with requests from day one. Consistent positive: the students themselves.

How to prep
Have a two-minute version of your teaching background ready — your A1→A2 curriculum work and the C2 discussion class both fit their conversational, CEFR-level model exactly. Mention you build your own materials; given theirs is reportedly error-ridden, that's your strongest card.

Ask them these (the reviews say the answers matter more than anything they'll ask you):
- Exact rate per 30-minute class, and what the bonus condition is
- Is the 15-minute pre-class window paid? Are staff meetings paid?
- What happens on a student no-show
- Are you paid as a contractor in Mexico, and how — bank transfer, PayPal, what currency
- Can classes be assigned outside my declared availability without my acceptance
- What's the realistic number of classes per week for a new teacher

Get the pay and availability answers in writing before signing anything.

Six pages. Beyond what I gave you earlier, I added:

  • Their full methodology from the official FAQ — the modified CEFR ladder (A0/A1/A2 basic, B1/B2 intermediate, C1/C2/DM advanced), the six class types inside each cycle, quizzes every 2 cycles, and the fact that basic levels use bilingual teachers while advanced levels are native-only.

  • Scripted answers to the seven questions most likely to come up.
  • The pay math with the unpaid 15-minute window folded in.
  • Contract and payment questions specific to being paid into Mexico — currency, method, fees, invoicing.
  • Time zone note — Querétaro is UTC−6 with no DST; check whether the invite is in Florida time.
