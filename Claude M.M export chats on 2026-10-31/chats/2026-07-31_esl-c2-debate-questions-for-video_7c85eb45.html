Not having watched it, I can't key these to timestamps — they're keyed to the beats of Stapleton's story (joining, the "Bard of Google" comms job, the perks, the Rubin severance and the 2018 walkout, retaliation, leaving).

Opening debate questions (before you press play)

  • Can a company be moral, or can only the people inside it be?
  • "Don't be evil" — is a corporate slogan a genuine commitment or a marketing asset from day one?
  • Should work give you meaning, or is a job that supplies your identity a trap?
  • Free meals, gyms, shuttles, nap pods: generosity or a leash?
  • Is an employee who goes public a traitor, a hero, or just someone who ran out of internal options?
  • When a company drifts from its founding ideals, is that betrayal or simply what scale does to every organisation?
  • Who carries more responsibility for a company's ethics — the executives who set policy, or the thousands who stay and keep it running?

Questions during the video

Early years / why she joined
- Was she hired into a job or recruited into a belief system? Where is the line between a strong culture and indoctrination?
- She calls it a "moral project." What does a company gain by framing employment that way?

The comms job — writing speeches and blog posts for leadership
- What is the difference between an in-house storyteller and a propagandist? Is there one?
- Would you take that job? What would you tell yourself while doing it?
- If you write a company's story about itself for twelve years, how would you ever notice the story going false?

Perks and campus life
- At what point does a workplace that provides everything for you stop being generous and start being something else?
- Are these benefits or are they compensation you've already paid for?

The $90 million severance and the 2018 walkout
- Was the walkout really about that payout, or was the payout just the thing that made everything else visible?
- Do mass employee protests change companies, or only their public relations?
- Google's code of conduct told employees to speak up if something wasn't right. What is a rule like that actually worth when it's tested?

What happened to her afterwards
- Does the company's response tell you more about its values than its slogan ever did?
- Is retaliation always deliberate, or can an organisation punish someone with nobody deciding to?

Leaving / disillusionment
- Is disillusionment a loss, or is it just seeing clearly for the first time?
- "Change it from within" — realistic strategy, or the most effective way ever invented to keep people quiet?
- At what point does staying make you complicit?

On the account itself (good for critical-reading practice)
- How much do you trust an insider memoir? What does she gain from telling this?
- Notice where she blames the company, where she blames individuals, and where she blames herself. Which does she do least, and why might that be?
- Does her humour make the criticism sharper or does it soften it?
