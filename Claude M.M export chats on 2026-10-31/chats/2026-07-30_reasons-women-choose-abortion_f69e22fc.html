Best data is the Guttmacher 2004 survey (1,209 U.S. abortion patients, published 2005). Two cuts:

All reasons cited (multiple allowed, so it exceeds 100%):- Having a child would interfere with education, work, or caring for existing dependents — 74%
- Can't afford a baby now — 73%
- Doesn't want to be a single mother / relationship problems — 48%
- Has completed childbearing / others depend on her — ~38%
- Not ready for a child — ~32%
- Doesn't want people to know she's pregnant or had sex — ~25%
- Not mature enough — ~22%
- Health problem with the woman — ~12%
- Possible fetal health problem — ~13%
- Partner or parents want the abortion — ~14%
- Rape — ~1%; incest — under 0.5%

Single "most important" reason (957 respondents):- Not ready for a child / another child — 25%
- Can't afford a baby right now — 23%
- Done having children / others depend on her — 19%
- Single motherhood or relationship problems — 8%
- Not mature enough — 7%
- Interferes with education or career — 4%
- Her own health — 4%
- Fetal health — 3%
- Partner or parents want it — ~1%
- Rape / incest — under 0.5% each

Florida is the one state that records a reason for every abortion, and its numbers run similar: the "hard cases" (rape, incest, life of the mother, fetal abnormality) together are roughly 1–2% of abortions, with the overwhelming majority reported as elective/social-economic.

Caveats worth knowing: the 2004 study is the most recent large national one of its kind, it sampled clinic patients rather than all abortions, and pro-life groups (e.g. Johnston's Archive) argue Guttmacher's hard-case figures are inflated relative to state reporting, while Guttmacher argues state forms undercount because reasons are recorded by providers, not patients. The 2013 Turnaway study (Biggs et al.) found the same top categories with somewhat lower percentages — financial 40%, timing 36%, partner 31%.
