Scenario sweep, Dir > 0 frame (mirror everything for dn):

  1. New ppLo, higher → pattern intact. Used.
  2. New ppLo, lower → cock. Used.
  3. Cocked, next ppHi lower → trigger. Used.
  4. Cocked, next ppLo higher (rolling) → decock. Used.
  5. Uncocked, new ppHi lower → currently ignored. This is the hole.
  6. Cocked, new ppHi higher → currently ambiguous (stay cocked? decock?).
  7. Cocked, another even-lower ppLo → currently no effect.

Case 5 is the answer to your question. A reversal is the failure of both structures: higher lows fail AND higher highs fail. Your current logic only lets the low-side break first. But half of real tops break the high side first — a lower high prints, then the lower low. Current logic sleeps through the lower high, then cocks on the lower low, then waits for yet another lower high before firing. One full swing late.

So the clean generalization: drop the asymmetry. Each pp series has a binary state, intact or broken — broken when pp[n] is worse than pp[n-1] in Dir terms, repaired when better. Cocked = exactly one series broken (CDir set). Trigger = both broken. Decock = the broken one repairs. Whichever side breaks first cocks; the other side confirms. Your existing rules become the special case where the opposite-type series happens to break first.

This also resolves case 6 for free: a higher high while cocked-by-low doesn't repair the broken low, so it neither decocks nor triggers — state holds until the low side repairs or the high side breaks. No special case needed. And it stays one code path: "worse/better in Dir terms" is just sign multiplication, and the cock/trigger roles are no longer hardwired to pp type.

Case 7: repeated breaks of the already-broken series could increment a cock-strength counter — free information, useful later as a filter, no logic change.

Two genuine cross-type uses, both future, not KISS:

  • Retracement depth: your cock condition is binary (retrace > 100% of prior swing). ppHi[n] vs ppLo[n] cross comparison gives the continuous version — cock at parameterized X% instead. Generalizes, adds a parameter, skip for now.
  • Trigger quality: when the trigger ppHi prints, is it above or below the cocking ppLo? Below = clean breakdown; above = overlapping chop. Gate or tag the signal. Also later.

One decision still open from before: decock is rolling (vs the cocking pp), so a "higher low" that's still below the pre-cock low decocks. Cheap to flag now, annoying to discover in backtest.

OK, why does antialiasing require multichrome?

CLAUDE2026-06-13 00:29 UTC

Because antialiasing IS painting with intermediate colors. A pixel is a square that's all one color, full stop. When a 0.5px line passes through a pixel, the renderer can't half-fill it — it fills it entirely with a color 50% of the way between the line color and the background. The "fraction covered" is encoded as "fraction blended." Many partial coverages → many blend levels → the shade variation you saw.

So one logical color (slate blue, nothing else drawn), but the raster necessarily contains a gradient of slate-to-white pixels. Thin appearance and uniform pixels are mutually exclusive — that's the trade.

Your eye doesn't see pixels — it sees light summed over an area. A line's visual weight is the total darkness it deposits, not its geometry.

A 1px-wide black line: each pixel column contributes 1 unit of darkness. A pixel at 50% gray: half a unit.
