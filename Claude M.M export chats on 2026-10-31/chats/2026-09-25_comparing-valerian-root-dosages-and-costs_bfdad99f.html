I've been taking 2400 milligrams of valerian root by Carlisle Each Night Before I sleep, but I feel the dose is too high. I'm looking for an alternative that comes in 1000 milligram units I came across this extract, but I have no idea how to compare milligram to milligram to figure whether it's more costly or less. Here's the information from the Mercato Libre page

CLAUDE2026-09-25 16:10 UTC

You can't compare them from this listing. The page never says how many mg of root each mL or each drop represents, and it gives two different bottle sizes (100 mL in the specs, 60 mL in the description).

It's also a different plant. This is Mexican white valerian (Valeriana ceratophylla), while Carlyle uses standard valerian (Valeriana officinalis). Even with a mg figure, 1000 mg of one isn't necessarily equal in strength to 1000 mg of the other.

To get the numbers, paste this into "Preguntas al vendedor" on the listing:

¿Cuántos mg de raíz de valeriana equivale 1 mL del extracto? ¿Cuál es la relación de extracción (por ejemplo 1:5)? ¿El frasco contiene 60 mL o 100 mL? ¿Cuántas gotas hay en 1 mL?

(It asks how many mg of root 1 mL equals, the extraction ratio, whether the bottle is 60 or 100 mL, and how many drops are in 1 mL.)

Once they answer, the cost is: $229 ÷ (bottle mL × mg of root per mL) = pesos per mg. Compare that to the price of your Carlyle bottle divided by its total mg.

The 40 drops are a serving size, but the listing still doesn't say how many mg of root are in those drops, so a mg-for-mg comparison still isn't possible.

  • 60 mL bottle: about 30 nights, roughly $7.60 per night.
  • 100 mL bottle: about 50 nights, roughly $4.60 per night.

You can compare that to your Carlyle cost per night, but it won't tell you whether 40 drops is weaker or stronger than your 2400 mg.

is there any such thing as a tool that clips capsules in half and seals the ends at the same time

CLAUDE2026-09-25 16:20 UTC

No. It's Highly likely no consumer tool like that exists. Pill splitters work on tablets and just crush or spill capsules.

What people use instead is a set of empty capsules plus a small hand-held capsule filler. You open the Nutricost capsules, pour the powder into the filler, and fill smaller empty capsules at the dose you want.

On Mercado Libre, search "encapsuladora manual" for the filler and "cápsulas vacías" for the empty capsules.

Half a Nutricost capsule is 250 mg of powder, which fits in a size 2 or size 3 empty capsule. Once you have a filler, the Cibele raw powder also becomes practical, and it's the cheapest per mg.

Literally, Carlyle does contain more plain root powder, because Nutricost has none at all; it's all extract.
