CLAUDE2026-07-23 00:08 UTC

I'd go with this one:

"Talking About Hobbies and Free Time - Level A1/2"
https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=IJAfosOKW5w

Why it works for reported speech at A1:
- Short, simple present-tense sentences ("I like...", "I play...", "I go...") — easy to convert into "He said (that) he likes..." / "She said (that) she plays..."
- Topic (hobbies) gives students plenty of concrete, personal statements to report
- Level-matched, so vocabulary won't fight with the grammar point

How I'd run it in your hour:
1. Watch once straight through (no pausing) — just listening.
2. Watch again, pausing after each person speaks. Write their direct-speech sentence on the board (e.g., "I play football on weekends").
3. Student converts it to reported speech ("He said he plays football on weekends") — drill the tense shift (play→played is optional at A1; many A1 syllabi keep reported speech in present for simplicity, your call).
4. Final round: student reports the whole video's content back to you in 3-4 reported sentences, no notes.

If that one doesn't suit your student's level once you watch it, the "Easy English" street-interview channel (easyenglish.video) has dozens of similar short clips with real people giving simple direct-speech answers — good backup if you want a different topic.

This is a real, non-ESL, human-made podcast — a genuine NPR-affiliate show, not made for language learners, but the topics and speech are simple and clear (it's aimed at kids, so vocabulary and sentence structure are naturally easy):

"But Why: A Podcast for Curious Kids" — Vermont Public / NPR
Episode idea: "Why do lizards do what they do?" or the axolotl episode — both fun, visual topics.
Podcast page: https://www.vermontpublic.org/podcast/but-why-a-podcast-for-curious-kids

It's audio-only (real podcast, not video), calm pace, one host asking simple questions, one expert answering in short plain sentences — ideal for reported speech: "The host asked why lizards can climb walls." / "The expert said lizards have special feet." / "She said geckos lick their eyes to keep them clean."

Class flow:
1. Play 5-6 minutes, no pausing — just listening for the topic.
2. Replay in short chunks. After each Q&A exchange, student says the direct-speech line, you write it up.
3. Convert together to reported speech (question → "He asked...", statement → "She said...").
4. Student gives a final 3-4 sentence reported-speech recap of what was asked and answered, no notes.

If your student prefers something visual instead of audio-only, the axolotl and lizard episodes both have accompanying video/photos on the Vermont Public site you can pull up alongside the audio.
