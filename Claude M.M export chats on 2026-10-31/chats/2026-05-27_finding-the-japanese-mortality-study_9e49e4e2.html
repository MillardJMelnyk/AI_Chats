CLAUDE2026-05-27 18:19 UTC

The piece most commonly cited alongside that Campbell/Clancy interview is a short commentary in JMA Journal (the Japan Medical Association's peer-reviewed journal):

Kakeya H, Nitta T, Kamijima Y, Miyazawa T. "Significant Increase in Excess Deaths after Repeated COVID-19 Vaccination in Japan." JMA Journal 2025;8(2):584–586.

  • JMA Journal (official): https://www.jmaj.jp/detail.php?id=10.31662%2Fjmaj.2024-0298
  • PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40416011/
  • PMC full text: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12095670/
  • DOI: 10.31662/jmaj.2024-0298

One thing worth flagging so you don't go looking for something that doesn't exist in that form: the JMA Journal piece is a 3-page commentary, not itself a 22-million-person cohort study. It argues that Japan had the world's highest per-capita mRNA dose rate, that excess deaths in 2023 exceeded 1,400 per million (about triple the U.S. rate), and that only ~10% of those excess deaths were attributed to COVID itself.

The "20–22 million" figure Clancy cites in the interview is from a separate analysis of a Japanese vaccination-records database presented by Japanese researchers including molecular oncologist Yasufumi Murakami, claiming deaths cluster around ~100 days post-injection after each booster. That underlying dataset analysis does not appear to be published as a peer-reviewed paper under that headline claim — it's been circulated via presentations and Clancy's commentary rather than a single citable journal article. If you want that one specifically, searching "Murakami" + the Japanese vaccination database is the trail; the JMA Journal piece is the closest formal publication.

The record count has been reported as 18M → 21M → 22M → 25M because the database has grown as volunteers added FOI-obtained records.
