MILLARD2025-08-20 16:41 UTC

in "perverting our grasp on cause and effect", grasp on or grasp of ?

CLAUDE2025-08-20 16:41 UTC

"Grasp of" is correct.

You have a grasp of a concept, not a grasp on it. "Grasp on" would be physical (like gripping something), while "grasp of" is the idiomatic phrase for understanding or comprehension.

MILLARD2025-08-20 16:42 UTC

they seem equivalent

CLAUDE2025-08-20 16:42 UTC

You're right that they're used somewhat interchangeably in practice, but "grasp of" is the more standard/formal idiom for understanding concepts. "Grasp on" does appear and is understood, but it's less conventional.

For this analytical passage, "grasp of" would be the safer choice, but either works if you prefer the sound of "grasp on."
