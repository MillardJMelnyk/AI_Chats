CLAUDE2026-03-29 17:23 UTC

The short answer: with InpDepth=4, InpDeviation=1, and InpBackstep=1, those 1–2 bar legs are mathematically valid outputs, not a bug.

Here's why:

InpDepth = 4 sets the lookback window for finding a local high/low. A pivot only needs to be the extreme within 4 bars — so on a volatile instrument like XAUUSD on a 1-minute chart, a bar can easily be the highest/lowest point in a 4-bar window.

InpDeviation = 1 is the minimum price movement (in points, not percent) required between two consecutive ZZ pivots. With gold on a 1-minute chart, 1 point is essentially nothing — a fraction of a pip. Any minor fluctuation clears this threshold.

InpBackstep = 1 sets the minimum number of bars between two consecutive pivots. At 1, adjacent bars can be consecutive pivot points, which is why you see single-bar legs.

The core issue is that your settings are essentially as permissive as they can get. The ZigZag will connect nearly every local swing, no matter how small. On XAUUSD 1-minute data, price moves enough between almost any two bars to qualify as a new pivot under these parameters.

To get meaningful legs, you need to tighten at least two of the three:

  • Raise InpDepth to 10–20+ (forces pivots to dominate a wider window)
  • Raise InpDeviation substantially — on XAUUSD, a meaningful move might be 50–200 points depending on your broker's point size (5-digit vs 3-digit pricing)
  • Raise InpBackstep to at least 3–5

The deviation parameter is usually the most intuitive lever: set it to roughly the minimum swing size you actually care about.
