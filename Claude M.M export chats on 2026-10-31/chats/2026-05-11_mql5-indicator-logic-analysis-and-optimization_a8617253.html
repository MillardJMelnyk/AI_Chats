Here's the analysis across all four versions.

1. P/L Label vs. Signal Reality

All four versions feed signals through the same HandleSignalEvent / TradeSimulation_MJM.mqh pipeline with signalPriceOverride, so the P/L figures are internally consistent with whatever signals each version generates. The numbers reflect what each version's logic actually does — the question is whether the logic itself is trustworthy, which is where the problems lie (see below).

2. How _134837 Amassed $65K

Two structural differences from the other versions create this:

A. Ungated midline crosses. In _135505 and _153218, midline crosses are suppressed whenever a boundary-trigger latch is active:

bool midCross = (g_triggerMaId == 3 && Crossed(midDiffPrev, midDiffNow));

In _134837, there is no g_triggerMaId state at all. Midline crosses fire unconditionally:

bool midCross = Crossed(midDiffPrev, midDiffNow);

This means _134837 never gets "stuck" waiting for a boundary cross-back that might not arrive for many bars. Every midline cross is a potential reversal signal, so in a trending market it catches direction changes faster and more frequently.

B. Per-bar trigger designation instead of stateful latch. The trigDesignated flag is computed fresh each bar using sameSideAsMid (both boundary MAs on the same side of the midline) — it doesn't persist across bars. So even if a trigger-cross fires on one bar, the next bar's midline cross is still eligible. The other versions' latching mechanism blocks midline signals until the latched MA is crossed back, which can suppress valid reversals for extended periods.

The net effect is a significantly higher signal rate. Over a 3-month trending gold market, those additional midline reversals accumulate large P/L — but this also means higher churn and sensitivity to transaction costs that aren't fully modeled.

3. Best-Performing Logic Compilation

Comparing what each design choice contributes:

Keep from _134837:
- Ungated midline crosses. The gating in _135505/_153218 blocks profitable reversals too often. The ungated version is the single biggest contributor to P/L.
- Per-bar trigDesignated rather than stateful latch. Avoids the pathological "stuck latch" problem where the system waits indefinitely for a cross-back.
- Symmetric outside-spread calculation (max(high - chTop, chBot - low)). Direction-neutral, avoids the body-color bias in _153218.

Keep from _153218:
- Trigger-cross signal directions: MA1 cross-back → SHORT, MA2 cross-back → LONG. This is semantically correct (price retreating from above the channel is bearish). _135505 has these inverted, which is a large part of why it scores only $8K.

Keep from _135505:
- The trigInsideToOutside gate on latch activation. Requiring that close actually transitions from inside to outside the channel before latching prevents spurious latches on bars where the wick extends beyond the threshold but the close is still inside. _153218 latches on spread alone (no transition required), which over-triggers.
- Direction-based MA selection for the latch ((d > 0) ? 1 : 2) rather than body-color-based (bodyIsNegative ? 2 : 1). Body color is noisy bar-to-bar; the established channel direction is more stable.

Discard from vanilla:
- The entire multi-step same-bar processing loop (see #4 below).
- The bid-ask spread adjustment (AdjustSignalPrice). While realistic in principle, it asymmetrically shifts signal prices and interacts badly with the sim's close-based P/L accounting.
- The failback/reentry machinery. It creates signal cascades without clearly improving signal quality.

Optimal composite would be _134837's core (ungated midCross, per-bar trigger designation) with _153218's trigger-cross signal polarity and _135505's latch-activation gate and direction-based MA selection.

4. The Vanilla Version's Major Problem

The for(int step = 0; step < 6; step++) loop inside the per-bar signal processing. This allows up to 6 signal events to fire on a single bar — a midline cross, then an outside switch, then a latched trigger cross-back, etc. — each priced at a different level (midline, boundary MA, offset boundary). Combined with g_sameBarMultiEventBar = i re-enabling processing of the same bar after each event, the vanilla version can execute synthetic round-trips within a single bar's price range.

In bar-mode backtesting, this is a look-ahead artifact. The sim sees the bar's full OHLC and executes a sequence of trades at different price levels that could never have been sequenced in real time. The $92K P/L is inflated by these impossible intra-bar trade chains. The chart confirms it — the top-left panel shows a dramatically higher arrow density (especially in the choppy zone around 14:35–15:07) than any other version, with multiple buy/sell pairs stacked on adjacent or same bars.

describe in English how to correctly implement an out-of-bounds overextension catch based on the assumption that these highly probably signal exhaustion

CLAUDE2026-05-11 14:28 UTC

When price pushes well beyond a channel boundary, that overextension is likely exhaustion — the move is about to reverse. The mechanism to catch this:

Activation: On each bar, measure how far the bar's extremity (high for upside, low for downside) extends past the nearer channel boundary. If that distance exceeds the threshold AND the prior bar's close was inside the channel (confirming this is a fresh breakout, not a continuation of an already-outside condition), the system arms a trigger on that boundary MA. Which boundary is determined by channel direction — uptrend arms MA a (the high MA), downtrend arms MA b (the low MA).

While armed: The system watches for close to cross back inside that boundary MA. This is the exhaustion confirmation — price overextended, then failed to hold. Midline signals continue to operate normally during this period (no suppression). The armed trigger is an additional signal source, not a replacement.

Firing: When close crosses back inside the armed boundary, fire a reversal: armed on MA a → SHORT (price failed above, now retreating), armed on MA b → LONG (price failed below, now recovering). Price the signal at the boundary MA value. Disarm the trigger.

Key constraints:
- Only one boundary can be armed at a time
- Arming requires close transitioning from inside to outside (not just a wick poking past)
- The trigger fires the opposite direction of the overextension — it's a reversal signal, not continuation
- If a midline cross fires while the trigger is armed, the trigger simply disarms — the midline cross already reversed direction, so the trigger's job is done
- Spread measurement is symmetric: always measure from the bar's extremity to the nearest channel edge, regardless of body color
