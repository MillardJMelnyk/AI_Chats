I'm watching an interview of Ammon Hillman where he talks about Jesus being in the Garden of the Gethsemane with an unnamed boy in addition to Peter, James and John. What evidence does Hillman offer for the presence of this unnamed boy?

CLAUDE2026-09-24 21:46 UTC

He relies on three main pieces of evidence, and I'm going off his known public talks rather than the specific interview you're watching.

  • Mark 14:51–52. This is his main text. During the arrest, a neaniskos (young man or youth) who had followed Jesus, wearing only a linen cloth (sindōn) over his naked body, is seized and runs off naked, leaving the cloth behind. Hillman takes neaniskos to mean a boy, not an adult.
  • Secret Gospel of Mark. This is a letter attributed to Clement of Alexandria, which Morton Smith reported finding at Mar Saba in 1958. It quotes a longer version of Mark in which a youth comes to Jesus at night, wearing a linen cloth over his naked body, and is taught "the mystery of the kingdom of God." Hillman treats this as the same youth, and as evidence of a secret nighttime initiation.
  • Greek magical papyri (PGM) and ancient mystery-religion practice. He points to rituals that used young boys as mediums or "seers," combined with drugs (pharmaka), and argues that the Gethsemane scene matches that pattern: night, a secluded garden, a boy in ritual linen, and Jesus as a magician or initiator. Morton Smith's Jesus the Magician is behind this reading.

How much weight each piece can bear:

  • The Mark passage exists. Highly likely. Who the young man is and why he's there is unresolved among scholars.
  • The Secret Mark letter is genuine. Likely to unlikely, since it's actively disputed. Only photographs survive, and several scholars argue Smith forged it.
  • The magical papyri connect to Jesus. Unlikely. This is Hillman's interpretive leap by analogy; no ancient text directly links the two.
