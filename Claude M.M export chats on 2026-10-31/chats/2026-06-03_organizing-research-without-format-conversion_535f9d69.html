CLAUDE2026-06-03 14:50 UTC

Your frustration is well-founded — the "normalize everything first" requirement is a real barrier, and it's partly an artifact of how most RAG pipelines are designed rather than a fundamental necessity.

A few things worth naming clearly:

Why they require it anyway

Most local AI document systems (AnythingLLM, Obsidian + plugins, PrivateGPT, etc.) use a chunk-embed-index pipeline. They need to extract raw text to embed it. The conversion step is their way of getting text out of formats they don't natively parse well — especially older formats, scanned images, handwritten notes, or proprietary containers.

Where the requirement is real vs. overstated

  • Scanned images / handwritten notes: genuinely need OCR first. No way around it.
  • PDFs with embedded text: most modern tools can extract these directly — no conversion needed if the PDF is text-based.
  • DOCX, RTF, ODT: many tools parse these natively now (LlamaIndex, LangChain loaders, Unstructured.io).
  • HTML/web clippings: usually parseable directly.
  • Proprietary formats (OneNote, Evernote .enex, old .doc): this is where conversion is genuinely necessary, and where your pain is probably concentrated.

The more honest framing

The real work isn't format conversion — it's deciding what goes in. 20 years of clippings includes a lot of noise. A system that ingests everything indiscriminately will surface everything indiscriminately. The pipeline forces you to touch each item, which has an accidental organizing benefit even if it feels like friction.

What would actually help

Given what you've described — volume, heterogeneous formats, decades of accumulation — the most pragmatic path is usually:

A pipeline that handles formats in place rather than converting to a single target. Unstructured.io (open source, runs locally) is the best current tool for this — it ingests PDFs, DOCX, HTML, images, email, and more without requiring you to normalize them first, and feeds directly into embedding pipelines.

Pair it with LlamaIndex or direct Ollama integration, and you can build a retrieval system that works across your existing files without mass conversion.

The OneNote problem is still the exception — that one genuinely requires the PowerShell COM export step you've already encountered.

CLAUDE2026-06-03 14:54 UTC

Fair.

The common formats — PDF, DOCX, HTML, RTF, plain text — are all directly readable by local LLMs today with no conversion. Unstructured.io handles the extraction layer for all of them. OneNote and old binary .doc are the only genuinely awkward ones.

A pipeline that works:

  1. Unstructured.io extracts text from whatever format it's in
  2. Ollama (local LLM) reads each document and produces a summary + relevance/quality score + tags
  3. Store those metadata outputs — not necessarily the full document — in a searchable index
  4. At query time, retrieve against the metadata first, then pull the source document for the LLM to reason over

This runs locally, overnight, without you touching individual files. The LLM does the curation. Your 20-year archive goes in as-is.

The only decision you actually have to make is which local model to use for the analysis pass — something in the 7-32B range (Mistral, Qwen, LLaMA 3) is fast enough to process thousands of documents overnight on your RTX 3090 and smart enough to make real relevance judgments.
