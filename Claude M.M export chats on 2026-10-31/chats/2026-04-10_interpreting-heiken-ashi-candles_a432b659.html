Interpreting Heiken-Ashi candles

MILLARD2026-04-10 20:45 UTC

referring to image, given the muted colors are the standard chart candles and the bright colors are the Heiken-Ashi, explain how to interpret the HA in simple English

CLAUDE2026-04-10 20:45 UTC

Looking at your chart, the bright cyan and magenta candles are the Heiken-Ashi (HA) overlaid on the standard candles.

Core idea: HA smooths out noise by averaging price data. Each HA candle incorporates information from the previous candle, so it responds more slowly but shows trend direction more clearly.

How to read them:

  1. Solid cyan bodies with little or no lower wick = strong uptrend. The trend is clean and you'd want to stay long or look for long entries.

  2. Solid magenta bodies with little or no upper wick = strong downtrend. The trend is clean and you'd want to stay short or look for short entries.

  3. Small bodies with wicks on both sides = indecision/consolidation. The trend is weakening or reversing. You see this in the choppy middle section of your chart around 18:36-18:52.

  4. Color change = potential reversal signal. When HA flips from magenta to cyan (or vice versa), it's suggesting momentum has shifted. Not a guaranteed entry, but a heads-up.

What makes HA useful vs. standard candles:

Look at the left side of your chart around 18:20-18:28. The standard candles show some mixed bodies during the selloff, but the HA stays solidly magenta with minimal upper wicks—clearer confirmation that sellers are in control.

The tradeoff: HA lags. By the time HA confirms a reversal, price has already moved. That's why traders often combine it with your Keltner Channels or TEMA for earlier signals, using HA as trend-direction confirmation rather than entry timing.

MILLARD2026-04-10 21:21 UTC

how is it calculated?

CLAUDE2026-04-10 21:21 UTC

HA Close = average of the current bar's open, high, low, close
(O + H + L + C) / 4

HA Open = midpoint of the previous HA candle's body
(previous HA Open + previous HA Close) / 2

HA High = highest of: current bar's high, current HA Open, current HA Close
max(H, HA Open, HA Close)

HA Low = lowest of: current bar's low, current HA Open, current HA Close
min(L, HA Open, HA Close)

Why this matters:

The HA Open is the key. Because it depends on the previous HA values (not the previous real candle), each HA candle carries forward smoothed information from the entire history. This is what creates the lag but also what filters out the single-bar noise.

The HA Close being a 4-price average also smooths things—a bar with a long wick but small body won't dominate the HA close the way it would on a standard chart.

First bar problem: On the very first bar of a chart, there's no previous HA candle. Most implementations just use (O + C) / 2 of the real candle as the seed for HA Open.

MILLARD2026-04-10 21:21 UTC

so there are no parameters to alter the calculation?\

CLAUDE2026-04-10 21:21 UTC

Correct. Standard Heiken-Ashi has no parameters—the formula is fixed.

If you want tunability, the common variants are:

  1. Smoothed Heiken-Ashi — Apply a moving average (EMA, TEMA, etc.) to OHLC before running the HA calculation. The MA period becomes your parameter. More smoothing = cleaner trends but more lag.

  2. HA on a higher timeframe — Some platforms let you display M5 or M15 HA candles on an M1 chart. The timeframe choice acts like a parameter.

  3. HA of HA — Run the HA formula on HA values. Rarely useful, mostly over-smoothed mush.

If you wanted to experiment, smoothed HA with your TEMA as the pre-filter would be the natural fit given your existing indicator stack. You'd feed TEMA-smoothed OHLC into the HA formula instead of raw OHLC.
