The measurement fork. Nobody ever polled "do you oppose the occupation and ethnocide." Two different things were measurable, and they diverge by roughly an order of magnitude after 1967:

  • Line A — opposition to the Zionist project itself (pre-1948) / to the state's existence (post-1948).
  • Line B — opposition to occupation and settlement while accepting the state (1967 onward).

Line A is what your question literally asks. Line B is where the mass of Jewish opposition to what Israel does to Palestinians actually sits after 1967. I run both.

The denominator moves more than the opinion does. World Jewry: ~7.7M (1880) → 16.6M (1939) → ~11M (1945) → 15.7M (Sep 2023). The anti-Zionist mass base was Eastern European and Yiddish-speaking. It was killed. That single fact drives the biggest number change in the whole table — not persuasion.

What the numbers rest on. Active Zionism itself was ~1% of world Jewry before WWI — roughly 130–165k shekel-payers against ~13M Jews. Anti-Zionist organization was comparable or larger. The 1930s peak isn't a guess: in the December 1938–January 1939 Polish municipal elections the anti-Zionist Bund took about 38% of votes cast for Jewish lists against 39% for both Zionist parties combined, and in Warsaw won 17 of the 20 Jewish council seats. Add anti-Zionist Agudath Israel and organized rejection was a plurality-to-majority in the country holding ~20% of world Jewry. On the other side of the same decade, the American Council for Judaism — the only US organization ever built to fight a Jewish state — peaked around 15,000 members in 1944, 0.3% of American Jews, was down to ~3,000 by the early 1950s and 117 names by 1969. By 1945 roughly 80% of American Jews favored a Jewish state.

Inflection points, ranked by size of effect:

  1. 1941–45. Not persuasion — extermination. The anti-Zionist demographic base ceased to exist. Line A tier 1 drops from ~45% to ~10% inside four years.
  2. 1948. Remaining organized anti-Zionism folds. Non-Zionism becomes unspeakable in communal institutions.
  3. 1967. Line A stays flat; Line B is born and immediately larger than Line A ever was post-war.
  4. 1982 (Lebanon). ~400,000 in Tel Aviv, ~10% of Israel's entire population. Tier 2 quadruples.
  5. 1987–93 (First Intifada, Oslo). Line B tier 1 peaks. Opposition to occupation becomes a majority-adjacent diaspora position.
  6. 2000–02 (Second Intifada). Israeli peace camp collapses; Line B falls ~10 points and never fully recovers.
  7. 2014 onward. Diaspora generational split. Line A rises for the first time since 1945, driven almost entirely by US Jews under 40.

The funnel. Across the whole period, tier 2 runs ~25–30% of tier 1, and tier 3 ~20–25% of tier 2 — so roughly 5–7% of private disagreement ever became actual work. The 1930s Polish case is the one exception, where tier 3 sits at ~25% of tier 1, because voting a Bundist ticket was cheap and the parties were genuine mass organizations. Everywhere else, the gap between believing and doing is the standard social-movement ratio.

Where this is soft. Pre-1948 tier 1 is inference from electoral behavior, not opinion data — nobody polled Jews in Grodno. Line B post-1990 is real survey data. And the post-1948 floor of Line A never went to zero: 4–8% throughout, mostly Satmar and adjacent haredim who oppose the state on theological grounds and have nothing to do with the political left that now dominates the visible end of the number.
