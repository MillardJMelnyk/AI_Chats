CLAUDE2026-09-26 17:50 UTC

No product, off-the-shelf or custom, fully avoids it: every jaw-forward mouthpiece pushes on the lower teeth, so some forward drift of the lower front teeth is a known risk with all of them. What cuts it way down is a custom, adjustable device made by a dentist that grips all your teeth evenly instead of loading the front ones, used with a morning repositioner (a small bite piece you chew on for a few minutes after waking to put the jaw and teeth back).

In Querétaro, so no import hassle, the Spanish term is "dispositivo de avance mandibular" (DAM). Two local leads: SO DENT Querétaro in Candiles, a dental specialty clinic that has posted about DAMs, and a clinic that offers a mandibular advancement device for snoring and apnea for nighttime use, starting with an evaluation appointment.

Things to ask for, in Spanish:
- "A medida y ajustable (titulable)" — custom-made and adjustable, so you advance the jaw only as far as needed.
- "Cobertura completa de ambas arcadas" — covers all teeth on top and bottom.
- "Con férula o guía de reposicionamiento matutino" — includes the morning repositioner.
- "¿Cómo controlan el movimiento de los incisivos inferiores?" — how they monitor lower front teeth movement.

The only design that touches no teeth at all is a tongue-retaining device (suction bulb that holds the tongue forward), but it moves the tongue, not the jaw, and likely means importing.

MILLARD2026-09-26 17:51 UTC

How did the tongue retaining the devices work?

CLAUDE2026-09-26 17:51 UTC

It's a soft silicone bulb that sits outside your lips. You squeeze it, stick your tongue tip in, and let go; the suction holds your tongue forward between your lips all night so it can't fall back and block your throat. Nothing grips your teeth, so they can't be pushed.

MILLARD2026-09-26 17:51 UTC

Which method is more effective

CLAUDE2026-09-26 17:51 UTC

Jaw-forward devices, likely: in head-to-head studies both reduce snoring and apnea about equally, but people stick with the jaw device far more often because the tongue device is harder to sleep with (drooling, sore tongue tip, falling out), and a device you don't wear does nothing.

Research this and any similar, competitive products for their effectiveness and compare that to the jaw protrusion mouthpieces

CLAUDE2026-09-27 14:46 UTC

Airwaav RX1 has no sleep evidence behind it; jaw-forward mouthpieces do. Highly likely the jaw devices are more effective.

Airwaav RX1
- How it works: it sits on your lower teeth only, and small bumps inside prompt your tongue to push forward. The jaw doesn't move.
- The science it cites: the published study behind it tested 17 healthy people aged 18–43 while running for 10 minutes. It measured breathing rate during exercise, not sleep, snoring, or apnea. I found no sleep study of the RX1.
- Reviews: 4.6 stars on their own site. One reviewer says it stopped snoring but shrank after molding, leaving the molars feeling clamped. Others report dry mouth and mixed results on snoring.
- For you: it probably doesn't push the front teeth forward, since there's no jaw pull. But it ships from the US ($89.99 for two), and they give 30 nights to return, not 60.

Similar tongue-based products
- Tongue-retaining suction bulbs (aveoTSD, Good Morning Snore Solution): in sleep trials they cut snoring and apnea about as much as jaw devices, but people drop them more often, as covered earlier. They touch no teeth.
- Daytime tongue stimulator (eXciteOSA): a mouthpiece that sends small electric signals to strengthen the tongue muscle. You wear it awake, 20 minutes a day for six weeks, then weekly. Early research suggests it cuts breathing pauses by almost half in mild sleep apnea. It's aimed at mild cases, and it's an import.

Ranking for effectiveness
1. Custom jaw-forward device from a dentist: Highly likely the most effective. Strongest sleep evidence, and you already know it works on you.
2. Tongue-retaining bulb: Likely about equal on paper, but harder to sleep with.
3. Daytime tongue stimulator: Likely modest help, mild cases only.
4. Airwaav RX1: Unknown. No sleep data exists, only customer reviews.
