CLAUDE2026-09-30 18:35 UTC

Harm from the vaccine's design. Under Bruesewitz, a manufacturer can't be sued for any injury or death caused by side effects of a covered vaccine that was made correctly and properly labeled, even if a safer design was possible.

The Act adds three smaller shields:

  • Warnings presumed adequate: if the manufacturer complied with FDA requirements, its directions and warnings are presumed proper unless the plaintiff shows fraud or withheld information, or proves by clear and convincing evidence that the manufacturer failed to exercise due care (§300aa-22(b)(2)).
  • No duty to warn the patient directly: the manufacturer can't be sued solely for failing to warn the injured person directly; warnings go to the doctor (§300aa-22(c)).
  • Punitive damages barred: no punitive damages if the manufacturer complied with FDA requirements, unless it committed fraud, intentionally withheld information from regulators, or engaged in other criminal or illegal activity (§300aa-23(d)(2)).

What's not shielded: manufacturing defects and inadequate warnings. Even those claims must first go through the federal compensation program before a lawsuit can be filed (§300aa-11(a)(2)).

The connection is that "design defect" isn't a separate kind of lawsuit; it's one of the three legal grounds for suing over an actual injury.

Under product-liability law, when someone is injured or killed by a product, they can win only by showing the product was defective in one of three ways: it was made wrong (manufacturing), it was labeled wrong (warnings), or it was built wrong in concept (design). Most vaccine injuries are side effects of a correctly made, properly labeled dose, so the only ground left to sue on is design, and that's the one the NCVIA bars.

In practice: if a correctly made, properly labeled vaccine injures or kills someone through its side effects, there's no lawsuit available against the manufacturer. The only route is the federal compensation program.
